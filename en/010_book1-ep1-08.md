#### 8

Amphitryon would remember this week of travel to the end of his days --- of which not so many remained, roughly half as many as he had already lived, but not so few either, praise be to the Moirai the casters of lots; though no praise had ever yet stayed the implacable Atropos the Moira, poised to cut the thin thread of a man's life.

The summer month of Hecatombeon was made, from the first, for the enjoyment of earthly pleasures, and the pilgrims lingered a good long while at Lake Copais (which, strictly speaking, was not much on the way at all), where graying veterans splashed about like children, and Licymnius, giddy with sun and freedom, took it into his head to wrestle Nasal --- and did wrestle him, briefly, until Amphitryon intervened and peeled the mock-snarling Thelem off his brother-in-law, after which he seized them both by the scruff of the neck and dragged them out into deep water, ignoring their struggles and howls, and dunked them there to the guffaws of the soldiers and the shrieks of a delighted Alcmene.

Then, having crowned themselves with wreaths of sacred laurel and sworn to Apollo not to remove them before returning home, they all set off toward Phocis and had almost reached the northern slopes of Parnassus when Amphitryon, at a halt, proposed a small detour: down to the Gulf of Corinth to visit Kirra, a tiny seaside town that served as Delphi's harbor.

He himself did not know what had prompted this. Was it the plain reason --- the need for news of the trading galleys hired to bring the remaining plunder from Taphos, and to personally arrange the transport of the valuables to Thebes, having first traded part of the Taphian treasure for copper and priceless purple dye; or was it the hidden wish to wring one more week of happiness out of fate... no, he did not want to dwell on it, but within a couple of days the wheels of the wagons and the hooves of the horses were already thundering along the rocky shore of the Gulf of Corinth, with the veterans' sandals slapping the stone behind them.

The sun was sinking toward the horizon, and when the chariots came too close to the cliff edge, beneath which, forty cubits down, the sea grumbled with something like displeasure, one could see the water seething into foam, lilac in the light of setting Helios, while the waves dealt out weighty slaps to the perfectly innocent rocks.

The sky was clear, though farther out to sea a handful of clouds had begun to gather, standing stock-still like stubborn sheep, refusing to obey the shepherd's whip --- the south wind Notus, passing by on some errand of his own and deciding, on a whim, to misbehave.

"Ha-a-ai, tempest over waters, ha-a-ai, hundred-headed Typhon," sang Licymnius, who rode in the lead, carried away by his feelings, Alcmene joining in, gazing fondly at her brother as he confidently drove their chariot, "ha-a-ai, Tartarus is raging..."

"Ha-a-ai, tempest over waters," the veterans took up raggedly, "ha-a-ai, tempest over cosmos..."

Amphitryon had fallen behind, letting the soldiers pass ahead, and now rode last, listening to the long-familiar song with an inexplicable melancholy. The horses tossed their heads, straining to bolt forward, but he stubbornly held them back and looked around him, as if trying to make out, in the clouds over the sea or in the thistle bushes pushing up through the scattered stones, the cause of his state, to understand it and become the old Amphitryon again. Had he been a philosopher, he might have said that the moment had come when you understand that everything had been well just now --- but it would never be better than this again.

No, he was no philosopher, he was a mercenary warrior, and later a lawagetas, a mercenary commander; he always grew angry when people called him a hero, and so he simply listened to the song, breathed deep, and did not notice that the tongues of the waves were licking the gray rock of the cliffs with ever greater fury, climbing higher and higher, while the south wind Notus, that vagabond, had long since fled to his abode of winds, frightened by something, or simply remembering some urgent business.

"Ha-a-ai, tempest over waters..."

At first Amphitryon did not grasp what had happened, or whether anything had happened at all. Simply, one of the waves, the steepest and angriest of them, suddenly rose above the rest, doubling the height of its now-quieted sisters, and hung frozen in the air like a pedestal of foam --- and before that wave came crashing down, the earth sighed and stirred beneath the feet of the men, the hooves of the horses, and the wheels of the puny human wagons.

A closeness spread through the air, a bitter, intoxicating closeness; no one had time to be frightened, and into the sudden silence Nasal's voice rang out like mockery, still dragging out the refrain of the song; the second tremor was much weaker, but it was this one that made the bay mare --- one of the two harnessed to Licymnius's chariot --- rear up, striking out with her forehooves, and bolt headlong in a frenzy of neighing, heedless of the road and infecting her yokemate with the same madness.

The veterans' formation broke apart; Thelem cut off another "ha-a-ai" mid-word, staring open-mouthed after Licymnius's chariot as it bounced over the stones; Licymnius himself threw his whole body backward, leaning over the fallen Alcmene and trying at any cost to check the maddened horses --- and it seemed to Amphitryon that he heard a battle cry, achingly familiar, roll out over the sea and make the clouds on the horizon shudder and begin, unhurried, to move toward the shore.

He did not at once realize that he himself was crying out. He was the one crying out, throwing up his arms with the reins clenched in a death grip above his head, as he had cried out more than once in battle before the start of a headlong chariot charge; crying out though he saw no enemy, yet felt its presence in his gut, threatening the wind, the sea, the sky, the shuddering earth, the maddened mares... an enemy that had no name.

And then he lashed his own horses' backs with all his strength, driving them mad at the charioteer's will, to match the madness of the horses now racing a spear's throw ahead of him, pressing ever closer to the impatiently licking jaws of the cliff edge --- beneath which stood, unfallen, the absurd, impossible, monstrous pedestal-wave.

His war cry saved the veterans' lives, many of whom would otherwise inevitably have been trampled. Those who, by chance, found themselves in the path of Amphitryon's chariot instantly scattered in every direction --- a soldier's trained reflexes had saved men more than once in cases where wise Metis the Thought proved entirely useless --- while clever Nasal dropped to the ground like a whirlwind and seized his own team by the bridles, hanging his whole weight on them and wrenching their bared teeth rightward toward the cliff, forcing the animals to dance in place and letting Amphitryon pass at full speed, scraping past the left side of his chariot.

Which very nearly threw both Thelem and his horses off the cliff, but that no longer mattered in the least; and salt spray tore free from the gigantic wave, like an involuntary round of applause.

The thunder of hooves was deafening, the wreath of sacred laurel slid off his head almost at once, the earth swayed in time with the gallop, the reins burned his palms as they strained to tear free of his stiffened grip, twice the chariot lifted both left wheels off the crunching gravel, and both times a wheezing Amphitryon kept his balance by some miracle, and sweat streamed down his forehead and stung his eyes unbearably, but Amphitryon could see anyway that Licymnius still had not mastered the cursed mares... there he was, closer... closer still... the rear wheel hangs for an instant over the edge of the cliff, but Licymnius, baring his teeth in a terrible grimace, wrenches the left rein --- and manages to win one cubit of life from nameless fate... two cubits... three... nearly five cubits of life --- and into that gap between the side of Licymnius's chariot and the edge of the cliff, beyond which the foaming void began, Amphitryon drove himself, his horses, and his chariot, the way in battle he drove a spear between the rim of a shield and an enemy's outthrust sword.

"Ha-a-ai!.."

He jumped. For an instant the two chariots ran side by side, their rails grinding and crumpling into each other, and Amphitryon, not understanding what he was doing, launched himself sideways through the air, crashing down onto a shouting Licymnius, nearly hurling his young brother-in-law onto the stones, and clawed his fingers around the fists of Alcmene's brother, gone rigid on the reins.

A moment later Amphitryon felt that his back was about to tear apart.

And the sky smelled of horse sweat and death.

Then came the pain of a lip bitten clean through, a joyful, happy pain, because the ground was slowing its rush, gradually ceasing to hurtle toward them, thundering and jolting; and Amphitryon licked the blood from his lip, savoring its salty taste, and tried to straighten his fingers: his own first, and then, a little later, those of a bewildered, blinking Licymnius.

Only then did the rain come pouring down.
