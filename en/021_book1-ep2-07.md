#### 7

"Listen, Autolycus," Amphitryon sighed and pushed away his half-emptied cup.

He did not care for white wine, least of all diluted so heavily, but that was not the point.

Autolycus, sitting across from him, lazily picked at a roasted thrush and waited for him to go on.

"In search of this man I have crossed the whole of Boeotia, traversed the Isthmos, and finally caught him here, in Argolis," Amphitryon reminded himself, noticing that he was beginning to chafe under Autolycus's silence and his ironic, faintly mocking smile.

The two men were alike: Amphitryon, son of Alcaeus, grandson of Perseus and great-grandson of Zeus, and Autolycus, son of Hermes and grandson of the Thunderer; the one they called the craftiest of all the Hellenes.

Both were powerful, thickset men, well matched in the count of scars earned in past battles; both deceptively slow-moving, even a touch heavy, standing firmly on the earth and knowing the worth of women, friendship, and gold; except that Amphitryon's gaze was always fixed on his interlocutor's face, which Autolycus never did --- his eyes, even in the tensest moments, kept their mocking, absently inattentive expression.

It was not for nothing that hook-nosed Autolycus, son of the wily and fickle Hermes, was cursed in every dialect from Dodona to Rhodes, called an oath-breaker, a rustler of herds, and a seducer of young maidens --- called so, yet without a single proof pinning the blame on Autolycus specifically, for he (like his divine father) had never once been caught red-handed.

Moreover --- even when swearing oaths, Autolycus[^21] (worthy of the name given him by Hermes the Trickster) had such a knack that afterward, breaking the oath in substance, he never once broke it in form.

[^21]: Literally "a wolf unto himself," i.e., "a lone wolf."

"Listen, Autolycus," Amphitryon sighed, pushed the cup aside, and decided not to trouble his head with further deliberation, "I did not come to Argos merely to pay you a visit. Everyone knows how skilled you are at wrestling..."

"We could wrestle, if you like," Autolycus smirked into his curly beard and turned toward his guest, leaning on his elbow so that the mighty muscles rippled along his slightly tensed arm.

"Why? I already know you're the better wrestler..."

It was true. Amphitryon knew that in a wrestling match between the two of them, victory would go to the son of Hermes. Unless it were a fight with no judge holding a forked staff, and no rules at all... Though even then, if it came down to a fight without weapons, Autolycus would win all the same, for an excessive devotion to the rules had never been a trait of the wily son of a wily father.

It was not for nothing that Hermes Cyllenios was considered the patron not only of athletes, but of thieves as well.

"Why?" Amphitryon repeated once more. "It's simply that I was told you're not only a skilled wrestler, but a seasoned teacher as well. You won't deny that you have students!"

"I do," Autolycus nodded in agreement, still not understanding (or pretending not to understand) what Amphitryon was driving at. Yet the wrestler's shrewd brown eyes --- the eyes of a seasoned strategist rather than a simple athlete --- followed his guest closely, from under cover. And when Amphitryon spoke again, something flickered in those eyes, suggesting that Autolycus had known all along what his guest was about to say.

"Basileus Creon would be overjoyed if a man like you, Autolycus, settled in Thebes --- even if only for a time. And I... believe me, I won't be stingy. The thing is, I have sons growing up. They're not yet five, but children grow quickly. I want them to grow into real men."

Amphitryon thought for a moment.

"Warriors," he clarified.

Then he thought a little more.

"Heroes," Autolycus put in, with a faint smile, just as Amphitryon opened his mouth to say the very same thing.

"Yes, heroes!" Amphitryon agreed, with a touch of defiance.

"Perhaps," Autolycus drawled thoughtfully, holding a strip of dried meat up to the light and examining it. "But a hero stands alone. Like you. Like me. And you have two."

"There are two Dioscuri as well," Amphitryon shot back stubbornly. "They're full brothers by their mother. And yet Castor is the son of Tyndareus, while Polydeuces is the son of Zeus. Castor, by the way, has already agreed to teach my sons weaponcraft. I didn't even have to talk him into it..."

"And why not the art of the charioteer?" Autolycus asked slyly, still studying the unfortunate strip of meat. "Castor boasts everywhere of his skill at taming horses; many consider him the best --- and not only in the Peloponnese."

"Well..." Amphitryon faltered, not knowing how to explain to Autolycus that he meant to teach the children the charioteer's art himself, considering Castor's fame somewhat overblown.

"Never mind," Autolycus broke in magnanimously. "What difference does it make? Yes, you're right --- there are two Dioscuri. Though between the two of them they scarcely have wit enough for one... And you have two as well. Yet a hero stands alone, all the same. Or are you seeking teachers only for the elder? What's his name --- Alcides, is it? A good name... a strong one."

Amphitryon took an irritated swig from his cup, spilling wine down the front of his chiton.

"I wanted to ask you, Autolycus, to teach both of them. Equally. To teach them wrestling. And to make no distinction between Alcides and Iphicles. As I make none myself."

"It seems I've wasted my time," he thought, "and a pity, too. Polydeuces refused to teach the boys fist-fighting, and now this one as well... Should I go to Messenia, to Idas Apharetid?"

Autolycus scratched his chin, parting the strands of his curly beard --- and suddenly stared past the stone parapet of the terrace, scanning the courtyard as though he had spotted something unusual there.

Amphitryon glanced involuntarily in the same direction.

A gaunt man in a threadbare chlamys was crossing the courtyard. The man's face could not be seen beneath his low-pulled hood, but he walked with a light, youthful gait, all but dancing as he went. The man struck Amphitryon as vaguely familiar, and Amphitryon flinched. No --- he had no wish at all to return to the events of nearly five years past, when...

No.

He did not want to remember it.

Besides, the passing man had already crossed the courtyard and vanished, though it seemed to Amphitryon that he gave a slight nod in farewell.

"I agree," Autolycus said slowly, and the old mocking look returned to his eyes. "When do you want me to move to Thebes?"
