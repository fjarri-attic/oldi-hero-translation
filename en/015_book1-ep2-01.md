### Second epeisodion

#### 1

"Even Taphos was easier to take," Amphitryon thought helplessly, when an entire army of wet nurses, nannies, midwives, and assorted women, led by a flushed Nausicaa, once again drove him out of the gynaeceum, intercepting him well short of the coveted door.

He went down to the courtyard and started pacing in circles, like a lion in a cave, trying to pay no attention to the many-voiced din drifting over the fence from the street. That turned out to be hard --- almost harder than thinking about the fact that Alcmene was screaming in labor behind the gynaeceum's door, had been screaming for nearly half a day now, and there was nothing he could do to help her, hero though he might be three times over.

"It's the Eileithyiai!" someone's piercing shriek carried in from the street. "The Eileithyiai, the birth-goddesses! It's all their fault!.."

"What's their fault?" several voices broke in at once.

"They won't let her give birth, that's what! On Hera's orders! Sitting at the threshold, pulling faces..."

"I'll g-give you faces to pull!" Amphitryon thought angrily, yanking out the bolt and wrenching the gate open with a jerk.

The crowd of onlookers fell back at the sight of him, and some spry little old crone, a dwarf who looked like a weasel, darted under Amphitryon's arm and was in the courtyard in an instant.

Amphitryon had started to turn toward her, but instead of fleeing, the old woman ran straight up to him and clamped onto his wrist.

"I'm Galinthias!" she caterwauled, spitting and pulling absurd faces. "Galinthias, daughter of Proetus! Drive them out, my lord, drive out the Eileithyiai! Drive them out! Away! I'm Galinthias, daughter of Proetus, I'm a wise woman, I know everything..."

And she went skittering and hopping about the courtyard, shrieking hysterically:

"Aaaah! A son is born to Alcmene, by the will of great Zeus! A godlike son, mighty, a hero, a Slayer of Monsters! Fat chance for the Eileithyiai, fat chance, they're forgotten, no one brings them offerings! Cold stand their altars, the Thunderer has punished the wretches! Aaah! The daughter of Proetus spoke the truth, she who serves Three-Formed Hecate[^16]! The truth! The truth! The truth!.."

[^16]: Hecate is a night goddess, the patroness of magic and spirits, but at the same time the bestower of earthly blessings, aiding with childbirth. Her attributes are dogs, snakes, keys, and daggers.

Amphitryon shook his head, as if to rid himself of a delusion, and stepped toward the little crone, firmly intending to put her out on the street. Least of all did he want to see a servant of Hecate right now, however harmless and half-mad she might look.

But as he drew close to the wheezing Galinthias, the door to the gynaeceum opened, and a beaming Nausicaa appeared on the threshold. In her plump arms was a small bundle, carefully shielded from the draft by Creon's wife.

The bundle croaked softly.

Amphitryon forgot about the crowd, about the shrieking Galinthias, daughter of Proetus, about everything in the world --- all he could see now was that bundle, and had a flood come that instant, Amphitryon would not have noticed.

"A son?" he breathed, mouthing the word with barely any voice.

"Who else would it be?" Nausicaa laughed, settling the bundle comfortably in the crook of her elbow. "Not like me --- I bear nothing but girls... Look, hero --- what a boy! The envy of everyone!"

Amphitryon approached Nausicaa and timidly peered under the folded-back swaddling cloth. A wrinkled red little face looked back at him, blowing bubbles out of a toothless mouth --- the most beautiful, the most wonderful of all human faces.

Reaching out, Amphitryon weakly touched the infant's cheek.

"I'll call you Alcides, little one," he whispered, almost soundless. "In honor of Alcaeus, my father and your grandfather. Alcides --- it means Strong One... A good name. Whoever's son they choose to consider you, it will be I who gives you the name! You had a good grandfather; and I hope you'll have a good father --- even if you come to call the Thunderer father instead. You will be strong, little Alcides, and until then I'll be strong for you. Agreed?"

The child screwed up his face and croaked considerably louder.

"They scared the little one, scared the poor thing," Nausicaa began soothing the newborn, rocking him in her arms. "And you, hero, stop mumbling and... and don't go pushing where you shouldn't! It's too soon for you on the women's side, they've no time for you there! Go on, go on, find something to do, give orders about the feast, hand out gifts..."

"A boy!" Galinthias suddenly howled, dashing headlong for the unbarred gate. "A son! A son is born to Alcmene, a godlike hero, a hero, a Slayer of Monsters! A son! A fat chance for the Eileithyiai, a fat chance!.. They're forgotten, Hera's own daughters..."

Her scrawny little body slipped like an eel through the gap between the gate's halves --- and a moment later the onlookers beyond the gate set up a clamor ten times louder than before.

"Wait!" Amphitryon, coming to his senses, dashed for the gate, burst out into the street, looked around, and spotted Galinthias, daughter of Proetus, running for all she was worth away from the house.

"Wait, Galinthias! Stop, will you!.."

The old woman turned around without breaking stride --- and Amphitryon tore from his neck a golden disk bearing an image of Helios in his chariot.

The chain, unable to bear it, snapped; two links fell into the dust, but Amphitryon did not stoop to pick them up.

"Catch, daughter of Proetus! My thanks to you!.."

With the confident motion of a discus-thrower, Amphitryon sent the ornament flying through the air; the disk described a shallow, whistling arc, and the crowd of onlookers burst into cheers when the little crone twisted nimbly and, leaping up, caught the costly gift out of the air.

Then the old woman took off running again, and had Amphitryon seen the predatory joy that twisted her little face just then, he might well have grown deeply thoughtful --- but he saw nothing.

He simply went back into the courtyard, barred the gate behind him, raked his fingers through his already unruly hair --- and resumed his aimless wandering about the yard, only now smiling and humming under his breath.

...And Galinthias, daughter of the utterly unknown Proetus, ran on and on, clutching the gifted disk to her withered, bared breast, veering sharply northwest right by the Cadmea, toward the outskirts of Thebes, until she reached a lonely hovel whose half-rotten wooden supports were thickly overgrown with green ivy.

The old woman ran inside, threw back the mat covering the floor, and with an effort heaved up the trapdoor lid revealed beneath it --- then began climbing down into the black pit that opened up, down a ladder that creaked ominously even under her slight weight.

"Has it come to pass?" someone below asked impatiently, out of the darkness.

"Yes," Galinthias answered curtly, and added sharply, in a tone of command so unlike her manner of speech until now:

"Begin! We have little time..."

Flint struck, sparks flew, and after a while a small hearth-fire caught in the corner; its flame pushed the darkness back only with effort, picking out the earthen walls, a huge stone crudely hewn into the traditional shape of an altar --- and two people standing by that stone.

One of them was hastily unwrapping a bundle very much like the one joyful Nausicaa had been holding.

"Hurry!" Galinthias, now down the ladder, snapped at him.

The man nodded silently, and soon a naked child lay on the altar --- a girl, alive, but making no sound, as though drugged with a sleeping draught. Galinthias bent over her and smiled tenderly, the smile of a mother leaning over a cradle. Her glittering little eyes never left the child; her withered hands rummaged busily through the rags of her clothing, as if the daughter of Proetus were afflicted with the itch --- and the barely distinguishable figures behind the crone's back began swaying weakly from side to side, mumbling something inarticulate that could equally have passed for a lullaby or a stifled howl.

"I hear it!" burst from one of them, the one who had unswaddled the girl. "I hear Tartarus... I hear, my fathers!.. I hear..."

Galinthias did not even turn around.

"A son is born to Alcmene by the will of great Zeus!" she began muttering, dancing in place. "A godlike son, mighty, such as has never been born before!.. Accept this offering, Deliverer, infant, Nameless Hero --- accept the offering, but no longer by the will of Zeus, but of those older than the Thunderer... we sacrifice sincerely to the newborn..."

"I hear Tartarus!" both helpers were now howling behind her. "We hear it, our fathers... o-o-oh... not long now... not long!.."

Galinthias's right hand wrenched free of the rags with a jerk, gripping in its fist a flint knife with a chipped blade; almost without a swing she drove the knife into the belly of the girl, who did not even cry out, and drew the blade up at a slant, ripping the chest open with a faint crunch. The daughter of Proetus, without looking, thrust her left hand out to the side --- and one of the helpers, without a moment's delay, placed a smoking brand from the hearth into it.

The knife rose again; a drop of blood fell from its blade and, hissing, landed on the proffered brand. An unnatural spasm arched Galinthias's body; she screwed her eyes shut and threw back her head, pressing her sharp, birdlike nape into her shoulders.

"Accepted," she moaned fervently. "It is done! Now..."

...At that very moment, in Nausicaa's arms, little Alcides began to cry at the top of his lungs.
