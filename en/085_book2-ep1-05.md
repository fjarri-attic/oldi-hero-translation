#### 5

"...And why are you idling? Off with you, quick march --- go carry the baggage!"

Lichas scratched the back of his head, turned round unhurriedly, and sized up --- from below, looking up --- a hulking brute in a leather kilt that had slid down to his very loins.

A plated belt, badge of an Ilian dekarchos, cinched the bawler's bare belly tight.

"Are you an Ilian or a Trojan?[^48]" Lichas asked thoughtfully.

[^48]: The city was named Ilion in honor of Ilus (father of the present ruler, Laomedon); it was named Troy in honor of Tros, father of Ilus and grandfather of Laomedon.

The dekarchos's flattened mug spread into a pleased grimace --- apparently he'd caught something flattering to himself in Lichas's question.

"Trust Zeus to send a blockhead..."

"No, but still --- Trojan or Ilian?"

"Why, of course, both at once!"

"Then go and carry baggage for two," Lichas summed up, and turned away.

"What? How dare you, you whelp, you slave?!" --- hot breath reeking of garlic scorched the back of his neck.

"First of all, I'm not a slave, I'm a free man" --- just in case, Lichas took a couple of steps to the side. "And second --- if you can count that far --- I'm not a whelp, because you're not my dad. So --- sorry about that."

Every one of the dekarchos's thoughts showed clearly on his reddening face --- not so much because that face was expressive, as because there were so few thoughts to show. It was plain that the sense of what Lichas had said was dawning on the big lout slowly and painfully, until at last it dawned in its full unpleasantness --- and, growling something inarticulate, the dekarchos stepped forward to deal the glib-tongued brat a proper clout across the ear.

And he dealt it.

Only not to Lichas, but to the crudely planed post of the awning Lichas had been leaning against --- because a pebble had gotten into the boy's sandal, and Lichas had bent down to shake it out.

The dekarchos, having bruised his hand and driven a splinter into it, kicked out at the youngster in a fury --- but an instant before that, having lost his balance, the boy was already hopping on one foot, and the dekarchos's kick landed on the wretched post once again. And then Lichas fell. Right next to a heap of horse dung. And the dekarchos fell too. Right next to Lichas.

And it became clear to everyone at once that the dung was perfectly fresh.

"Why are you tormenting the master dekarchos, you little wretch?" a lean, elderly, bearded man asked rhetorically, hauling Lichas up by the scruff of his neck.

"Me?" Lichas asked, genuinely astonished.

"Well, not me, was it?! Who tripped him up?"

"This one?" Lichas raised his bare foot --- the sandal with the pebble had stayed behind by the post --- and, by pure chance, caught the sharp-eyed do-gooder square in the groin with his knee.

The man doubled over with a deep, hollow grunt and was almost at once knocked to the ground by the dekarchos, who was just then getting up.

"Right, that's it..." the two men breathed out in unison, and Lichas understood it was time to resort to the last remedy.

"Alci-i-ides!" he shrieked piercingly across the whole yard. "Iphi-i-icles!"

And added the main thing:

"They're picking on the little ones!"

"Picking on him, all right," the aggrieved parties confirmed in unison, closing in on Lichas from both sides.

Lichas squeezed his eyes shut and pitied himself very much.

Then he opened his eyes and pitied his tormentors very much instead, along with the heap of dung, which the Trojan and the bearded man, having landed back in it, had thoroughly smeared across the ground.

"What is going on here? What is this brawl about?!" A young man in a patterned chiton and an expensive cloak of dyed wool stepped up to Lichas and wrinkled his nose fastidiously, as though the boy stank.

The question, however, was meant more for Alcides and Iphicles --- the brothers, having managed to dump their baggage at the far end of the outer courtyard, had returned just in time (from Lichas's point of view) or at the worst possible moment (from the point of view of the dekarchos and his fellow picker-on of Lichas).

"What is this brawl about, I ask?!" the young man repeated imperiously, knitting his thick black brows and looking the twins over with contempt --- sweaty, dust-caked, in torn loincloths.

"A basileus's scion," Lichas decided at once.

The twins exchanged a glance --- they clearly had no idea how to answer the young man's question.

"About me," Lichas answered proudly, and got a box on the ear from the young man for it.

"And that's what'll happen to anyone who touches my..." the young man didn't finish.

Shrugging, Iphicles took the proud owner of the fine cloak by an arm and a leg ("the right arm and the left leg," the meticulous Lichas noted, for some reason) and carried him back to where he'd come from.

And set him down there.

After which he unhurriedly returned to his brother --- and the two of them settled in to wait.

The courtyard came to a boil. No one was carrying baggage anymore, or pouring water into cauldrons, or pinching the bare-legged slave women, or gossiping --- everyone was hastily sorting themselves into spectators and participants, and the spectators chattered all at once, while the participants shouted even louder, working out where the weapons were --- ah, in the storeroom?.. as custom required?.. what a pity, what a pity... and locked, too?! --- and, snatching up sticks and stones along the way, a wave of men eager for a fight, led by the trio of innocent victims, rolled down upon the brothers.

Like a Poseidonian swell breaking on coastal cliffs.

With the sole difference that cliffs do not grab at what breaks over them with their hands, nor fling it every which way.

"Don't get carried away, Alcides," Iphicles tossed out a warning during a brief lull. "This isn't the Hydra... tear off a head, and it won't grow back."

"But I..." Alcides began, then glanced past his brother's shoulder, and the unhealthy gleam that had briefly faded from his eyes flared up again.

From the direction of the palace steps, a sizable procession was approaching the scene of the melee --- and Alcides at once recognized, walking in its midst, Augeas, basileus of Eleia, and Laomedon, wanax of Troy; both of whom had once driven Heracles from their lands in disgrace.

"Got you!" Alcides announced happily, and, forgetting everything else, advanced on his enemies.

Laomedon, who looked rather like a eunuch, barked out some short command --- and it turned out at once that by no means everyone had surrendered their weapons: the household servants and the guardsmen closed around him and the pale, stumpy Augeas in a ring, bristling with the stings of light spears and swords.

And Lichas understood that trouble was about to happen.

Big trouble.

The merry scuffle between the brothers and a bunch of harmless ruffians was no more. Heracles, having remembered an old grudge, was advancing on Laomedon's guard; the spears of seasoned soldiers spoke plainly enough of their intentions; the Ilian dekarchos --- the first pebble of the avalanche --- was already running with a heavy log in his hands around the corner of the courtyard toward the weapons storeroom, hoping to break the lock... and Lichas dashed headlong for the brothers' baggage.

Grabbing hold of the thinner end of Alcides's club --- Lichas still could barely lift it off the ground even now --- he dragged it along, sobbing, stumbling, shouting hoarsely:

"Heracles! I'm here!.. Here I am... Heracles, a weapon!.."

"The lad's gone quite out of his wits," muttered an old cook, watching what was happening with indifference. "Oh, he'll strain something... calling out to Heracles, and where is this Heracles of his?.." She bent down and unhooked a knapsack that had caught its cord on one of the knots on the club and was dragging along behind --- but Lichas didn't notice this and didn't hear the cook's words; he wept and hauled, hauled and wept, until suddenly the club leapt into the air, and Lichas became strong, so very strong, and then --- weak, so very weak, because the club scorched his palms as it tore free from his hands and flew off into the sky...

"Thanks, little one!" Iphicles breathed out --- or did the huddled Lichas only imagine it? --- and with a roar threw himself into the fray to help his brother, trampling the scattered bodies; but just then, cutting through the din that reigned over the courtyard, a short, terrible cry rang out from the gates:

"Halt! Everyone, halt!"

And then, with a bronze boom, as though the Hundred-Handed had all struck the walls of Tartarus at once:

"You heard me!"

For an instant everything froze; mouths open in a soundless scream, sticks flung up into the air, swords and spears thrust forward, a club raised overhead...

And in the silence that fell, hunching his tousled head down between his skinny shoulders, Lichas turned around.

By the gates, beside a lanky, splendidly dressed Oechalian of about forty-five --- beside this man, who did not interest Lichas in the least --- stood Iolaus. Next to the Oechalian, Iolaus looked absurdly young and short, but Lichas did not doubt for a moment who it was that had stopped the slaughter.

"What's all the noise about?" Iolaus said, more calmly now, ambling out into the middle of the courtyard; but in his throat the echoes of his recent shout still seethed.

So does an arrow still quiver after it strikes its mark.

Silence.

Not the forest kind, the tranquil kind --- a different one.

"About me," Lichas reported guiltily, for the second time, sidling up to Iolaus and expecting an inevitable clout.

"This one won't miss," flashed through his mind.

Iolaus looked at the crestfallen boy, at Alcides coming back to himself, at Iphicles with the club on his shoulder, at the ring of soldiers bristling with bronze points...

"All right," he grunted. "You've had your exercise, and that's enough. Where's the baggage?"

"Over there" --- the hands of the brothers and Lichas all pointed together toward the far end of the courtyard.

"Pick it up and follow me," Iolaus said, stretching his whole body with a crack, as if after long, tiring labor. "They'll show us to our quarters now. As for the club... you'd best hand it over to the armory. Let Lichas carry it there. Well then, shall we?"

* * *

Already at the door of the chambers assigned to them --- the farthest ones in the eastern wing of the ground floor, reached by a gallery --- the breathless Iphitus the Oechalian caught up with Iolaus.

"Everything's fine," he said, answering the unspoken question. "Once they found out who they'd been fighting and that they'd survived it, they swelled up with pride like toads before rain. Now they're croaking about it. Your father and your uncle --- do they drink much, by chance?"

"Why?" Iolaus asked, not understanding.

"No reason. Everyone wants to drink with Heracles. So they'll have something to tell their children in their old age. And basileus Eurytus asks that you come to the megaron toward evening. All right?"

"All right," Iolaus answered, somewhat puzzled.

And soon enough, evening came.
