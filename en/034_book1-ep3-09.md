#### 9

Hermeias was neither all-seeing nor all-powerful. Nor was he a seer, like blind Tiresias, or like Prometheus Iapetid.

But he was a god; and in the Family he was not counted among the least of them.

Travelers often called on the Trickster, and it was no wonder that hermae --- wooden or stone posts bearing the likeness of the Patron God on top --- turned up, now and then, on nearly every road of Hellas. Often the likeness had little in common with the original, but the ritual of dedicating a herm to Hermes Cyllenios was observed unfailingly whenever one was set up, and travelers were not too lazy to leave the god gifts, hoping for his favor; so the Trickster made use of the roadside posts more than once. To him they were not mere carved stones --- right now the youth sitting on the ground was present within each one of those posts, was them, as they were parts of him; and Hermeias could see everything happening on the roads, sometimes clearly, sometimes dimly, but see it he did, through the blind, cracked eyes of the images atop the hermae-posts.

* * *

...here a merchant caravan drags itself across Boeotia, from Panacton toward Iolcus; faces flash by, horse muzzles, bundled goods... no, the one Hermeias is looking for is not here! The road to Mycenae. Ah, here a few ragged men with clubs are robbing two frightened travelers... at another time Hermeias would not have failed to give the little brigands a fright, but there is no time for them now --- forgive me, travelers, pray to someone else!... Onward, onward... the valley of the Cephissus, the foothills of Cithaeron, the gaze slides from herm to herm, from post to post --- oh, how sparsely they stand here, too little of *me* in this stretch, not enough to cover the whole road...

And then, in a flash of recognition --- past the last herma on this road, beyond which the road itself gradually peters out, turning into a winding, stony mountain path --- a familiar stocky silhouette flickers into view at last, a bow slung across its shoulder.

Myrtilus was walking into the mountains, having left his chariot abandoned by the roadside.

Why? Hunting, was it? Without warning his friends, abandoning his pupils, leaving his family behind?!

The question went unanswered. Myrtilus turned a bend and vanished from sight.

* * *

...Dummy took a deep breath and opened his eyes.

"Your teacher Myrtilus is in the foothills of Cithaeron right now," Hermeias said dully. "And he's moving farther from Thebes with every step."

"Is he all right?" Iphicles frowned.

"Yes. Alive and well. For now..."

That strange "for now" had slipped from Hermeias unexpectedly, even to himself, but the twins paid no attention to the uninvited word.

"That's it!" Alcides breathed out. "An oracle! Hear that, Iphicles: our Dummy's an oracle! Why'd you keep quiet about it before? Some friend you are! We tell you everything, and you go hiding things from us!..."

"Cithaeron's way over there," Iphicles said quietly. "What would Myrtilus be doing there?"

"I don't know," Hermeias admitted honestly.

"You don't know?!" Alcides was genuinely astonished. "No, Dummy, you really are a rascal! What do you mean you don't know?! You owe it to us to know!"

"First of all, I don't owe anyone anything. And second, I really don't know. What, you think oracles know everything? No..."

And here Dummy fell silent mid-word, not even noticing that Alcides had gone silent too, his face turning to stone, as though listening to something.

In Hermeias's mind, disrupting his concentration, a frightened cry rang out like an echo: "In the name of Hermes Pompaios! What are you doing with that knife, good man?! Stop!..." Hermeias covered his face with his hands and, cutting himself off from the brothers, tried to focus on the voice calling out to him from somewhere there, in the wooded foothills of Cithaeron.

* * *

"...What are you doing with that knife, good man?!. Stop!"

An elderly shepherd in a goatskin over his shoulders backed away in fright from a stern man in an expensive embroidered chiton and a cloak pinned with an iron fibula.[^28]

[^28]: Back then iron was more valuable than gold.

At the man's feet lay a broken bow, one end touching a pyramid of piled stones; the quiver of arrows that any archer or hunter would carry was simply nowhere to be seen.

In his hand the stranger gripped a broad, slightly curved knife.

"Don't be afraid," the man answered, his voice calm and dead. "This knife isn't for you."

And he wedged the blade upright between the stones.

"See these stones?"

"I see them," the shepherd wanted to flee, but something held him in place.

Fear? Curiosity? Hope of gain?

Stupidity?!

"This is the altar of a future hero, the son of Zeus and Alcmene. On it I will sacrifice myself to my pupil... my former pupil. And I'll leave you my clothes and my chariot with its horses --- it's down there by the road --- if you do me one favor."

"But maybe it'd be better..."

"Be quiet and listen! I don't want my restless shade wandering the earth, knowing no peace and striking terror into people. So I ask you this: when it's all over, burn my body, so that my soul may rest in peace in Hades. Agreed?"

"But... why do you have to die? Buy a kid goat from me --- and we'll sacrifice it to whoever you like!"

"No. I swore an oath. And I lost the contest. The gods helped me, but it was I who lost, not the gods. I, Myrtilus the archer, lost, and I will keep my oath. So --- will you help me, or will my shade haunt you to the end of your days?!"

"Of course, of course," the shepherd nodded hastily, watching Myrtilus turn his back to him and walk toward the altar, between whose stones an ominous shoot had already pushed through toward the sun, gleaming bronze --- broad, and slightly curved at the tip.

* * *

*"Dummy! Wake up! He's... Alcides has it again! Do something! I don't want..."*

Hermeias took his hands from his face, and the first thing that struck his eyes was Alcides, senseless and strangely contorted, and Iphicles, nearly in tears, standing over his brother's faintly trembling body.

"Dummy! *They* are about to call him!"

"A fit," Hermeias guessed. "He's about to have a fit..."

An incomprehensible unease gripped the Trickster. Nothing like it had happened to him in a long time. He had wanted to see one of Alcides's fits --- it was for that very reason he had spent more than two years sitting in Thebes --- but now, when Hermeias had finally gotten what he wished for, he was at a loss. At a loss, like an ordinary mortal!

Whatever Hermeias was about to see, whatever decision he might have to make afterward --- he was afraid to make that decision alone!

"Chiron!" --- the thought flashed like a revelation. "He has to see this! He stands outside the Family's interests --- the ideal witness... Besides, Chiron is a Kronid too, and his word might mean something to dad!"

Hermeias caught up the convulsing, writhing Alcides in his arms, catching, in passing, the heat of that small body, in which some monstrous wave seemed to be seething, foaming with molten darkness.

"Iphicles, hold on to my belt! Now!"

Iphicles did not recognize Dummy's voice, gone suddenly hard and commanding, but obediently clung to his friend's belt.

Hermeias stepped forward, opening the Dromos. The outlines of the house they had been standing beside blurred into a haze; small wings trembled impatiently, sprouting from the heels of the Trickster's sandals...

One step.

Another.

Iphicles stumbles, but his fingers only close tighter around Dummy's belt.

The third step they took already on Pelion.
