#### 5

The roof was repaired quickly enough, the gynaikeion was set back in order, and for several days Amphitryon scarcely left his wife's side, trying to stay near her at all times and keep Alkmene from leaving the house.

But after the fire, which had turned out well in the end, nothing out of the ordinary happened, so that Amphitryon gradually calmed down, and when Alkmene expressed a wish to go to the bazaar with two servants to do some shopping, he raised no objection---and why should he have?

So off they went---Alkmene and two dark-skinned, broad-hipped Phoenician slave women.

The bazaar, in the lower part of the city, greeted the women with a loud babel of voices, the cries of hawkers, a motley riot of clothing and shades of skin; and, first and foremost, with every kind of smell---from the fragrance of peaches and almonds to the sharp reek of fresh fish.

First Alkmene had a craving for fruit. She spent a long while choosing pomegranates to her liking, ones whose broken halves gleamed with ruby-red, juicy, sweet-tart seeds; then she bought some dried melon. Passing by the fish stalls, she slowed her step, sniffed the air, and thought better of buying fish, turning instead toward the butchers'---where various smoked meats, to which Alkmene had lately taken a liking, hung from knotty poles.

The slave women followed their mistress obediently.

And that was when a beggar latched on to them: short, gaunt, wrapped in an unspeakably filthy chlamys whose hood kept his face in shadow the whole time.

"Alms for a poor cripple!" the beggar wailed behind the women's backs, clutching at the slaves' clothing (he himself, meanwhile, looked perfectly healthy). "Give, for the sake of Zeus! And for the sake of Apollo! And for the sake of Athena! And Dionysos! And Hermes! And Artemis! And... and all the rest of them! Come on, give! It's nothing to you! Alms!..."

There were a great many gods, and the ragged man could have gone on naming them forever, so Alkmene tried to get rid of the beggar by pressing the smallest of her pomegranates into his hand---a mistake. The beggar instantly grasped that there was profit to be had here, and redoubled his whining.

So Alkmene tried simply ignoring the beggar---and, strangely enough, it worked. Especially once she stopped by the poles hung with Thessalian smoked meats. Alkmene spent a long time picking fastidiously through them for the best piece, while the fat, black-bearded seller kept up an endless stream of praise for his wares, eyeing the slave women's enviable hips all the while---and between his chatter and the smell of smoked meat, Alkmene's head began to spin, so that in the end she apparently pointed not at the piece she had all but settled on, but at the one beside it, a somewhat suspicious one.

The fat man briskly took down the indicated piece, showering her with compliments; one of the slave women was already holding out a basket for it---when, right from behind her back, out sprang the beggar, who had fallen silent and so been forgotten by everyone.

He snatched the smoked goat meat from the merchant's hands, leapt back, and, spraying spittle, sank his yellow teeth into the meat, grimacing and devouring the stolen prize with incredible speed.

A silence fell---broken only by the beggar's loud smacking, while his Adam's apple bobbed up and down his unwashed neck as the half-chewed lumps went down. The silence, however, lasted rather less than the beggar would have liked---Alkmene gasped in astonishment, both slave women shrieked, and the black-bearded merchant, cursing, produced a hefty cudgel from somewhere and, waddling resolutely on his short legs, advanced on the thief, who went right on chewing.

And the impudent fellow would surely have gotten a sound thrashing---and serves him right!---but just then the lover of other people's goat meat stopped working his jaws, dropped the remains of the meat into the dust, and clutched at his stomach with both hands.

"Poisoned! Ohh... 'm dyin'!!!" The beggar's howl rose over the bazaar, all but drowning out the general noise and clamor.

"Faking it, the scoundrel," the owner of the stolen meat said, raising his cudgel over the thief writhing on the ground---but he did not bring it down, freezing instead in indecision.

The cries quickly gave way to an inarticulate howling; the man no longer writhed on the ground but only twitched feebly, clutching at his own belly; beads of sweat broke out over the wretch's face, foam appeared on his lips, and his breath came hoarse and labored through teeth clenched in a spasm.

He no longer howled or groaned, but only whimpered feebly.

It suddenly dawned on Alkmene *who* might have been in the beggar's place, had he not eaten that ill-fated goat meat---and, despite the noon heat, a chill ran through her.

And it dawned on the fat seller *whose* meat had poisoned the beggar---and he hastened to vanish, doing so with such virtuosity that the luckless thief would have done well to learn the art from him.

"A healer!" Alkmene cried, as though coming to herself.

"A healer!" the slave women wailed after her.

"A healer!" the crowd chimed in raggedly.

Before long a healer did indeed push his way through to the scene---a wizened little gray-haired old man, resembling a dandelion gone to seed, if for a joke one were to dress it in a long-skirted himation and a pair of brand-new red sandals, plainly just bought right there in the bazaar.

At the sight of his prospective patient, the healer grimaced with distaste, but bent over the poisoned man all the same, felt his thin wrist, pressed an ear to his chest, then a hand to his forehead; wincing, he sniffed at his breath.

"Bad," he pronounced with an air of profundity.

"Will he live?" Alkmene blurted.

The healer looked at her sternly.

"He might live," he informed her, then thought a moment and added, "Or he might not live. It's all still in the lap of the gods..."

And he made to leave.

"I..." Alkmene touched the healer's elbow. "I'll pay for his treatment."

The healer made to leave a good deal more slowly.

One of the slave women whispered something in his ear, pointing a finger at her mistress.

The healer thought better of leaving altogether.

"You, and you," he said, pointing at two sturdy men in the crowd, both with the calloused hands of craftsmen. "Pick him up and follow me. She'll pay you afterward."

It never occurred to the craftsmen to disobey. The crowd parted to let the healer through; behind him the appointed bearers lugged the poisoned man, after them came Alkmene with her slave women, and tagging along behind the women were a dozen or so idle onlookers, of the sort found at any bazaar.

The healer's tent turned out to be not far off. At its entrance, all manner of remedies were laid out on a mat, and a scrawny youth in a dirty loincloth---his apprentice, no doubt---was pounding some highly suspicious roots in a wooden mortar.

"Emetic," the healer ordered curtly, giving the apprentice a kick in his stuck-out backside, and the boy, abandoning the mortar, hurriedly vanished into the tent.

The healer waved to the bearers, and everyone, save the women and the onlookers, followed the apprentice. There must have been another way out of the tent, for before long disgusting sounds began coming from its far side---the thief's stomach disgorging the stale goat meat, along with everything else that happened to be in it. Judging by how long the sounds went on, the beggar had not gone hungry for the last ten years.

...When it was all over, the healer came out to Alkmene once more.

"He'll live," he answered her unspoken question flatly. "You could feed him worms and he'd pull through. He'll be laid up a day or two, that's all."

"Do I owe you anything?" Alkmene asked bluntly, having already paid the bearers.

"Of course!" the healer agreed amiably, studying the toes of his splendid new footwear. "Have a couple of amphorae of wine sent to me, and a sack of millet flour. Better yet, two sacks. My thanks in advance, mistress."

And, nodding farewell, he disappeared into the tent.

The onlookers began to disperse.

And no one saw the half-dead ragged man, carefully laid down under the awning behind the healer's tent, open his eyes, glance quickly about, make sure no one was near, spring nimbly to his feet---and dash off down the nearest lane as fast as if wings had suddenly sprouted from his torn krepides sandals...
