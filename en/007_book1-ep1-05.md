#### 5

The roof was repaired quickly enough, the gynaeceum was set back in order, and for several days Amphitryon scarcely left his wife's side, trying to stay near her at all times and keep Alcmene from leaving the house.

But after the fire, which had after all ended well, nothing out of the ordinary happened, so that Amphitryon gradually calmed down, and when Alcmene expressed a wish to go to the bazaar with two servants to do some shopping, he raised no objection --- and why should he have?

So off they went --- Alcmene and two dark-skinned, broad-hipped Phoenician slave women.

The bazaar, in the lower part of the city, greeted the women with a loud babel of voices, the cries of hawkers, a motley riot of clothing and shades of skin; and, first and foremost, with every kind of smell --- from the fragrance of peaches and almonds to the sharp reek of fresh fish.

First Alcmene had a craving for fruit. She spent a long while choosing pomegranates to her liking, ones whose broken halves gleamed with ruby-red, juicy, sweet-tart seeds; then she bought some dried melon. Passing by the fish stalls, she slowed her step, sniffed the air, and thought better of buying fish, turning instead toward the butchers' --- where various smoked meats, to which Alcmene had lately taken a liking, hung from knotty poles.

The slave women followed their mistress obediently.

It was there that a beggar attached himself to them. Short, gaunt, wrapped in a hooded chlamys filthy beyond all measure, its hood keeping the beggar's face in shadow the whole time.

"Alms for a poor cripple!" the beggar wailed behind the women's backs, clutching at the slaves' clothing (though for all that he looked perfectly healthy). "Give, for the sake of Zeus! And for the sake of Apollo! And for the sake of Athena! And Dionysus! And Hermes! And Artemis! And... and all the rest of them! Come on, give! What is it to you?! Alms!.."

There were a great many gods, and the ragged man could have gone on naming them forever, so Alcmene tried to get rid of the beggar by pressing the smallest of her pomegranates into his hand --- to no avail. The beggar instantly grasped that there was profit to be had here, and set up his whining with renewed vigor.

So Alcmene tried simply ignoring the beggar --- and, strangely enough, it worked. All the more so once she stopped by the poles hung with Thessalian smoked meats. Alcmene spent a long time picking fastidiously through them for the best piece, while the fat, black-bearded seller kept up an endless stream of praise for his wares, eyeing the slave women's enviable hips all the while --- and between his chatter and the smell of smoked meat, Alcmene's head began to spin, so that in the end she seemed to point not at the piece she had all but settled on, but at the one beside it, a somewhat suspicious one.

The fat man briskly took down the indicated piece, showering her with compliments; one of the slave women had just held out a basket for it --- when, right from behind her back, out sprang the beggar, who had fallen silent and so been forgotten by everyone.

He snatched the smoked goat meat from the merchant's hands, leapt back, and, spraying spittle, sank his yellow teeth into the meat, grimacing and devouring the stolen prize with incredible speed.

A silence fell --- broken only by the beggar's loud smacking, his Adam's apple bobbing up and down on his unwashed neck as it worked half-chewed lumps down his throat. The silence, however, lasted rather less than the beggar would have liked --- Alcmene gasped in astonishment, both slave women shrieked, and the black-bearded merchant, cursing, produced a hefty cudgel from somewhere and, waddling resolutely on his short legs, advanced on the thief, who went right on chewing.

And the impudent fellow would surely have gotten the thrashing he deserved --- serve him right! --- but just then the lover of other people's goat meat stopped working his jaws, dropped the remains of the meat into the dust, and clutched at his stomach with both hands.

"Poisoned! Ohh... 'm dyin'!!!" the beggar's howl rose over the bazaar, all but drowning out the general noise and clamor.

"Faking it, the scoundrel," the owner of the stolen meat said, raising his cudgel over the thief writhing on the ground --- but he did not bring it down, freezing instead in indecision.

The cries quickly gave way to an inarticulate howling; the man no longer writhed on the ground but only twitched feebly, clutching at his own belly; beads of sweat broke out over the wretch's face, foam appeared on his lips, and hoarse breath tore its way with difficulty through teeth clenched in spasm.

He no longer howled or groaned, but only whimpered feebly.

It suddenly dawned on Alcmene *who* might have been in the beggar's place, had he not eaten that ill-fated goat meat --- and, despite the noon heat, a chill ran through her.

And it dawned on the fat seller *whose* meat had poisoned the beggar --- and he hastened to vanish, doing so with such virtuosity that the luckless thief would have done well to learn the art from him.

"A healer!" Alcmene cried, as though jolted awake.

"A healer!" the slave women wailed after her.

"A healer!" the crowd took up raggedly.

Before long a healer did indeed push his way through to the scene --- a dry little gray-haired old man, resembling an autumn dandelion, if for a joke one were to dress it in a long-skirted himation and a pair of brand-new red sandals, plainly just bought right there in the bazaar.

At the sight of his prospective patient, the healer grimaced with distaste, but bent over the poisoned man all the same, felt his thin wrist, pressed an ear to his chest, then a hand to his forehead; wincing, he sniffed at the smell of his breath.

"Bad," he pronounced with an air of profundity.

"Will he live?" burst from Alcmene.

The healer looked at her sternly.

"He might live," he informed her, then thought a moment and added, "Or he might not live. It's all still in the lap of the gods..."

And he was about to leave.

"I..." Alcmene touched the healer's elbow. "I'll pay for his treatment."

The healer was about to leave much more slowly.

One of the slave women whispered something in his ear, pointing a finger at her mistress.

The healer thought better of leaving altogether.

"You, and you," he said, pointing at two sturdy men with the calloused hands of craftsmen, standing among the onlookers. "Take him and carry him after me. She'll pay you afterward."

The craftsmen did not so much as think of disobeying. The crowd parted to let the healer through; behind him the appointed bearers dragged the poisoned man, after them came Alcmene with her slave women, and trailing the women in turn was a dozen or so idle onlookers, of the sort found at any bazaar.

The healer's tent turned out to be not far off. At its entrance, all manner of remedies were laid out on a mat, and a scrawny youth in a dirty loincloth --- his apprentice, no doubt --- was pounding some highly suspicious roots in a wooden mortar.

"Emetic," the healer ordered curtly, giving the apprentice a kick to his protruding backside, and the boy, abandoning the mortar, hurriedly vanished into the tent.

The healer waved to the bearers, and everyone, save the women and the onlookers, followed the apprentice. There must have been another way out of the tent, for before long disgusting sounds began coming from its far side --- the thief's stomach disgorging the stale goat meat, along with everything else that happened to be in it. Judging by how long the sounds went on, the beggar had not gone hungry in the last ten years.

...When it was all over, the healer came out to Alcmene once more.

"He'll live," he answered her unspoken question flatly. "You could feed him worms and he'd pull through. He'll lie about a day or two, and that's the end of it."

"Do I owe you anything?" Alcmene asked directly, having already paid off the bearers.

"Of course!" the healer agreed amiably, studying the toes of his splendid new footwear. "Have a couple of amphorae of wine sent to me, and a sack of millet flour. Better yet, two sacks. My thanks in advance, mistress."

And, nodding farewell, he disappeared into the tent.

The onlookers began to disperse.

And no one saw how the half-dead ragged man, carefully laid down under the awning behind the healer's tent, opened his eyes, glanced quickly about, made sure no one was near, sprang nimbly to his feet --- and dashed off down the nearest lane with such speed, as though wings had suddenly sprouted from his torn crepidae sandals...
