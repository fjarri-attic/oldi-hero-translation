#### 13

"A brother, I want a brother!" Hermeias squealed, plainly becoming his old self again --- a frivolous trickster, a deceptively frivolous god, an unpredictable, noisy schemer. "A twin brother! To hold me back from unreasonable acts! Like Iphicles held back Alcides during his fits!"

"Why didn't he hold his brother back today, then?" asked Chiron, clearly not expecting an answer.

But an answer came.

"Because the rotten Dummy wanted to kill us!" a sleepy child's voice rang out from the far corner of the cave. "That's why! There! Get out of here, Dummy! Didn't you get enough of the rock back then?! Want more? Here!.."

And a flushed Iphicles hurled at his enemy the first thing that came to hand --- an apple.

The boy threw the apple rather well aimed, but the plump golden fruit, for some unfathomable reason, hung frozen in the air, an elbow's length short of Dummy's head bent over the tub.

Hermeias turned lazily, reached out his hand, plucked the apple straight out of the air, and bit off nearly half of it with a crunch.

"How'd you do that?" Iphicles asked, puzzled, forgetting his grievance for a moment.

"Like this!" the Trickster smirked, and crunched down the rest of the apple.

"Well, there he sits, munching apples," Alcides, also awake now, elbowed his brother in the side. "Like it's nothing. What'd you want to kill us for, you glutton?!"

"Yes, what for?" Iphicles joined in, rubbing his eyes.

"And what did you attack me first for?" Hermeias answered, matching the twins' tone, and for clarity jabbed a finger at Alcides.

"Me?!" Alcides was genuinely astonished. "Why are you lying? You were the first one..."

"No, you really did attack him," honest Iphicles did not back his brother up. "You had a fit. You headbutted him right in the stomach! Nearly doubled Dummy over..."

"Don't remember," Alcides grumbled sullenly. "Pity!"

"Fine, Alcides went first," Iphicles waved his hand impatiently. "But you gave it right back to him too! So what was there to kill for?! Take me --- why did you want to kill me?"

"You?" Hermeias was surprised in his turn, sitting down on the edge of the tub. "I wouldn't have laid a finger on you! Alcides --- yes... well, I lost my temper --- it hurt too much! I got angry..."

"Yes, you wanted to kill Alcides," Iphicles, funny and disheveled, was thinking hard about something, but the thought kept slipping away, and the words came to the boy with difficulty. "Of course, Alcides... and then you started killing Alcides --- and me! That is..."

He got thoroughly tangled up, fell silent --- and then suddenly his face lit up. It seemed Iphicles had finally managed to catch the thought he needed and wrap it in more or less coherent words.

"That is, it made no difference to me which of us you'd kill!" he blurted out. "Alcides or me --- it's all the same as *me*! And I very much wanted to kill you first... well, I wanted it for the two of us, for myself and for Alcides!"

Iphicles beamed, remembering how he alone had wanted to kill the villain Dummy for the two of them.

"And didn't I crack you good with that rock!" he finished, already more good-naturedly.

Chiron, unseen by Hermeias, gave Iphicles a thumbs-up --- meaning, very good indeed!

"Shame I didn't see it," Alcides sniffled, gradually calming down too.

"But you --- you broke off a club like that!" Hermeias hurried to console him. "If you'd landed that on me, I'd have dropped straight into Hades!"

"What club?" Alcides perked up with interest.

"About like that one, there," Hermeias leaned out of the cave, looked around, and, beckoning Alcides over, pointed him toward a hazel tree growing nearby. "See the outermost trunk?"

Alcides looked doubtfully at the hazel, then at Dummy --- plainly suspecting he was simply lying --- and turned his gaze back to the indicated trunk.

"You broke it," Iphicles confirmed. "I saw it myself."

"Don't remember," Alcides muttered once again, clambered out of the cave, and tried to break the hazel, bracing his knee against it and yanking the trunk with both hands as hard as he could.

Naturally, the tree had no intention of breaking.

"You're lying," Alcides declared confidently. "All of you are lying: Dummy, and you, Iphicles, and this... horse-uncle."

Then he looked around in surprise and, without any transition, added:

"Where even are we? Uh, I meant to ask before... and fell asleep."

"At my place. On Pelion," said Chiron, who had been silent until then.

"On Pelion?" the boy's eyes went round. "So then you're... the wise centaur Chiron?! We rode on your back..."

"Can't speak to the 'wise' part," the centaur smiled, "but Chiron is indeed my name."

"But... how did we get here? It's a long way from Thebes..."

"The Dummy dragged us here," Iphicles grumbled.

"I don't remember any of it!" Alcides was genuinely upset. "How did he drag us here, anyway? By the scruff of the neck? Did we walk for a month?!"

"What month," Iphicles sighed. "'Hold onto my belt,' he says... we took three steps --- and here we were."

"Well then! Dummy, how'd you manage that? And that thing with the apple..."

"Do you two even know who your friend here really is?" Chiron cut in, putting on an air of mock sternness.

"We know!" the twins answered in chorus. "Dummy! A liar and..."

"And a traitor," Iphicles couldn't help adding, though there was no longer any of his old anger in his voice.

"That's right. And he's also a god," the centaur said quietly, but in a way that made everyone go still and fall silent at once. "The god Hermes, one of the Family... er... one of the Olympians. And by the way, boys, he's your kinsman. Same as me. A distant one, mind you."

"There's never been any Dummies in our family!" Alcides shouted hotly, not yet having fully taken in what Chiron had told them.

"No horses, either," Iphicles added quietly.

"And who was your great-grandfather, heroes?"

"Perseus!" the brothers answered proudly, as one.

"And who was Perseus's father?"

"Zeus the Thunderer," the twins said, a tone quieter. "Kronid."

"Well, I'm a Kronid too! And Zeus is my half-brother, and, incidentally, the father of this one here..." Chiron pointed at Hermeias, who had straightened up importantly ("This one?!" Iphicles gasped, disbelieving, while Alcides simply stuck his tongue out at Dummy). "So regardless of whom you consider your father, heroes..."

"Our dad is Amphitryon!"

"Fine, Amphitryon, I'm not arguing," the centaur smirked, barely noticeably. "But either way, we're kin. Whether you like it or not."

The twins exchanged glances.

"Well, you --- you're all right, I suppose," Alcides graciously permitted Chiron to count as their kinsman. "But this one here..."

"We don't have kin like that, ones who go around picking on the little ones!" Iphicles finished for his brother, with finality. "And anyway --- what kind of Hermes is he?! A slob and a swindler! And that... what's it called? Well, that..."

Hermeias nearly jumped when Iphicles finally recalled the word he needed --- one he'd once overheard from a drunken Autolycus and later repeated in front of his mother, for which he'd been soundly whipped by Amphitryon.

"Trickster, show them," Chiron, barely holding back laughter, called Hermeias the Trickster for the first time.

And the twins, staring wide-eyed at Dummy, saw.

They saw the dirty stains vanish of their own accord from the once-elegant chiton of Hermeias-the-Dummy, saw the crumpled fabric dry out and smooth itself, quickly regaining its pristine look; and now not even the most scrupulous witness would have found a single hole in it.

They saw the ever-lasting, hard-wearing Talaria[^30] fit themselves onto Dummy's feet of their own accord, their little wings trembling and blurring together into hazy half-circles; saw their friend-and-enemy, laughing, rise an elbow's height above the floor and, waving his arms freely, sail out of the cave. Outside, Hermeias traced a few intricate figures through the air, and when he came down, two snakes hissed in the Trickster's hand, coiling around the caduceus rod that had appeared from nowhere. It was the well-known attribute of the god Hermes, patron of athletes, travelers, merchants, and thieves.

[^30]: Talaria --- the proper name of Hermes's winged sandals.

Hermeias absently flicked the snakes on the nose --- and they vanished along with the rod.

"Enough?" the Trickster inquired, winking at the centaur. "Or should I fly some more?"

"And here we've been making sacrifices to you," Iphicles said, taking offense for no clear reason.

"And he picks on the little ones!" Alcides added.

Then some thought crossed his mind, and the boy's eyes gleamed suspiciously.

"Once we get home, I'm going to smash your altar!" he promised the taken-aback Hermeias. "Or better yet..."

"Better yet, we'll dump manure on it!" Iphicles chimed in. "And set it on fire... Let him eat that!"

A great deal more might have been promised to Hermeias just then --- but the twins were cut short by Chiron's laughter, at last breaking loose, unstoppable, something like a horse's whinny. The centaur roared with laughter, his whole mighty body shaking, clutching his stomach with his hands (his human one, since his hands couldn't reach the horse's).

The twins fell silent mid-word, staring in bewilderment at the laughing Chiron; Hermeias couldn't hold out either and burst out snorting; before a minute had passed, the brothers had joined him too --- and soon the astonished nymphs and satyrs of Pelion could behold all four of them, quite literally doubled over with laughter at the mouth of Chiron's cave: the wise centaur, the youthful god, and two mortal boys --- the future heroes of Hellas.
