#### 2

By evening he forced himself to get up and go out into the yard.

It was time to learn to live all over again.

His body obeyed poorly, but it obeyed nonetheless. Iolaus's frailty made itself felt, and Amphitryon, recalling without much gratitude Alcmene's excessive fussing over her beloved grandson, forced himself not to think about the price paid for the chance to breathe again and to clutch at the corridor walls when dizziness struck. Skilled at trading past victories for handfuls of other people's lives, he had also learned not to spare friends and kin, not sparing himself either... now that helped, but not all the way.

The time to pay for his bargain with Hades would come, if it hadn't come already --- but a warrior in battle has no time for torment. This was today's battle: the need to go out into the yard, to get used to things and spaces grown absurdly large, to be wary of a bolt from the blue, and to catch the servants' pitying glances.

"Poor little thing!" tossed off a slave girl running across the yard. "So puny!.."

And she was startled when the pale, sickly boy smirked crookedly and, biting his lower lip in a fatherly manner, lashed the chattering slave girl's shapely hips with the whip of an unaccustomedly sharp gaze.

As though appraising a pedigreed mare.

"If Zeus could see me now! --- Amphitryon glanced upward despite himself. --- Now that would give him a laugh... yes, it'll be a good while before I father any new children! What, Amphitryon the Exile, got banished from everywhere? --- from Mycenae, from your wife's bed, from life, from Erebus... In a word: hero! Well, why not? I'll go to Iolcus, come down to the harbor: where's Argus the shipwright, or Tiphys the helmsman? Will you take me along to Colchis? Once, they'd have counted it an honor, I'd wager..."

He nearly jumped when he felt a heavy hand stroking the crown of his head.

Behind him stood Licymnius, Alcmene's half-brother.

Over the past five years Licymnius had changed a great deal. His once-luxuriant hair had thinned, his face had become blotched with patches of indeterminate color, and only his eyes remained almost the same --- black, almond-shaped, fringed with thick lashes --- except that the whites had cracked into a web of bloody veins and clouded over, so that Licymnius always looked as though he had just woken up.

"Back among the living?" Licymnius inquired cheerfully, looming over the boy. "Well, praise the gods! You ought to be running more, Iolaus, tearing your clothes to shreds, not sitting home with the nurses!"

"And you ought to drink less," Amphitryon nearly blurted out.

Looking up at his former brother-in-law from below turned out to be no simple matter, and a rather irritating one.

Especially when this nearly forty-year-old man kept stroking the crown of your head without pause.

"When I was your age..." --- Licymnius trailed off, as though assuming his listener already knew what Licymnius had been up to at that age, and admired it thoroughly. "Ah, little Iolaus... the ground burned under my feet! Bruises, bumps --- more than you could count! And you, just like some girl --- sick all the time, all the time... And you've got no real friends, either. You know, dear boy, as your great-uncle and a descendant of Perseus, I..."

"You'd better be on your way, Licymnius," Amphitryon retorted before he could stop himself, hearing his own voice with surprise --- thin and high. "As an uncle... and a great-descendant of Perseus."

And he shook the pudgy hand off himself.

As it turned out, that was a mistake --- the hand cracked against the back of his head an instant later, sending Amphitryon sprawling to the ground.

He barely managed to twist aside in time to keep his cheekbone from meeting the handle of a wooden spade lying on the ground.

His body obeyed poorly, but it obeyed.

"You whelp!" --- enraged, Licymnius bent down and grabbed the insolent boy by the scruff of the neck. "Mouthing off, are you, grandma's little pet! Aggh... owwwww!.."

The last part concerned the spade, which had just found its way between his legs.

The next thing poor Licymnius had occasion to discover was that a smack with a spade turns out to be considerably less pleasant than it seems at first.

That is, even at first such a smack hardly seems like a gift from fate, but by the end --- well, not at all...

"That hurts," Licymnius said thoughtfully, lying on his back. "It really hurts. I, as your grandfather... and so on."

A scowling boy's face bent over him.

Without the faintest glimmer of sympathy.

"Complain to your sister, and I'll kill you," little Iolaus promised in a bored voice. "And another thing, grandpa: don't pat my head anymore. Ever. Agreed?"

"Agreed," Licymnius nodded, feeling that he was losing his mind.

And touched a loose tooth with his finger.
