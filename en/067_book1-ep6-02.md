#### 2

By evening he forced himself to get up and go out into the yard.

It was time to learn to live all over again.

His body obeyed poorly, but it obeyed nonetheless. Iolaos's frailty made itself felt, and Amphitryon, recalling without much gratitude Alkmene's excessive fussing over her beloved grandson, forced himself not to think about the price paid for the chance to breathe again and to clutch at the corridor walls when dizziness struck. Skilled at trading past victories for handfuls of other people's lives, he was able to not spare friends and kin, while not sparing himself... it helped now, but only so far.

The time to pay for his bargain with Hades would come, if it hadn't come already---but a warrior in battle has no time to agonize. This was today's battle: the need to go out into the yard, to get used to things and spaces grown absurdly large, to be wary of a bolt from the blue, and to catch the servants' pitying glances.

"Poor little thing!" a slave woman called out as she hurried across the yard. "All droopy, he is!..."

And she was startled when the pale, sickly boy smirked crookedly and, biting his lower lip just as his father used to, lashed the talkative slave woman's ample hips with the whip of an unexpectedly sharp gaze.

As though appraising a thoroughbred mare.

[If Zeus could see me now!]{.thoughts} Amphitryon glanced upward despite himself. [He'd have a good laugh at that... yes, it'll be a good while before I father any new children! What, Amphitryon the Exile, got banished from everywhere?---from Mycenae, from your wife's bed, from life, from Erebos... In a word: hero! Well, why not? I'll go to Iolkos, come down to the harbor: where's Argos the shipwright, or Tiphys the helmsman? Will you take me along to Kolchis? Once, they'd have counted it an honor, I'd wager...]{.thoughts}

He nearly jumped when he felt a heavy hand stroking the crown of his head.

Behind him stood Likymnios, Alkmene's half-brother.

Over the past five years Likymnios had changed a great deal. His once-luxuriant hair had thinned, his face had become blotched with patches of indeterminate color, and only his eyes remained almost the same---black, almond-shaped, fringed with thick lashes---except that the whites were cracked with a web of red veins and had clouded over, so that Likymnios always looked as though he had just woken up.

"Back among the living?" Likymnios inquired cheerfully, looming over the boy. "Well, praise the gods! You ought to be running more, Iolaos, tearing your clothes to shreds, not sitting home with the nurses!"

[And you ought to drink less,]{.thoughts} Amphitryon nearly blurted out.

Having to look up at his former brother-in-law turned out to be no easy matter, and irritating besides.

Especially when this man of nearly forty kept stroking the crown of your head.

"When I was your age..." Likymnios left it unfinished, as though assuming his listener already knew what Likymnios had been up to at that age, and was duly impressed. "Ah, little Iolaos... I never sat still for a minute! Bruises, bumps---more than you could count! And you, just like some girl---always sick, sick... And you've got no real friends, either. You know, dear boy, as your great-uncle and a descendant of Perseus, I---"

"You'd better be on your way, Likymnios," Amphitryon retorted before he could stop himself, surprised to hear his own voice come out thin and high. "As an uncle... and a great-descendant of Perseus."

And he shook off the pudgy hand.

As it turned out, that was a mistake---the hand cracked against the back of his head an instant later, sending Amphitryon sprawling to the ground.

He barely managed to twist aside in time to keep his cheekbone from meeting the handle of a wooden spade lying on the ground.

His body obeyed poorly, but it obeyed.

"You whelp!" Enraged, Likymnios bent down and grabbed the insolent boy by the scruff of the neck. "Mouthing off, are you, grandma's little pet! Aggh... owwwww!..."

The last part concerned the spade, which had just found its way between his legs.

The next thing poor Likymnios had occasion to discover was that a smack with a spade turns out to be considerably less pleasant than it seems at first.

That is, even at first such a smack hardly seems like a gift from fate, but by the end---well, even less...

"That hurts," Likymnios said thoughtfully, lying on his back. "It really hurts. I, as your uncle... and all that."

A boy's scowling face bent over him.

Without the faintest glimmer of sympathy.

"Complain to your sister, and I'll kill you," little Iolaos promised in a bored voice. "And another thing, uncle dear: don't stroke my head anymore. Ever. Agreed?"

"Agreed." Likymnios nodded, feeling that he was losing his mind.

And touched a loose tooth with his finger.
