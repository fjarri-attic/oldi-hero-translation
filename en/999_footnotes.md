## Notes

{#fn-1 .footnote}
*Parodos*: the opening song of the chorus in a Greek tragedy.

* * *

{#fn-2 .footnote}
*Alalkomeneis*: one of the epithets of Athena, daughter of Zeus and Metis; she will later earn the epithet Pallas, after defeating the giant Pallas and stretching his skin over her shield.

* * *

{#fn-3 .footnote}
*Thanatos*: the Death, the twin brother of Hypnos the Sleep, a son of Nyx the Night.

* * *

{#fn-4 .footnote}
*The Moirai*: the three goddesses of fate: Klotho the Spinner, Atropos the Inevitable, Lachesis the Allotter. Literally, "moira" means "lot."

* * *

{#fn-5 .footnote}
*The Hekatoncheires*: the Hundred-Handed, firstborn of Ouranos the Sky and Gaia the Earth: Briareos, Gyges, and Kottos.

* * *

{#fn-6 .footnote}
*Basileus*: the ruler of a city. An analogue would be a high king or a grand prince.

* * *

{#fn-7 .footnote}
*Lapiths:* one of the tribes descended from the Titans; the tree people.

* * *

{#fn-8 .footnote}
*Perseid*: a descendant of Perseus; in this case, his grandson (compare Zeus Kronid, "son of Kronos," or Autolykos Hermesid, "son of Hermes," and so on). Sometimes the ending "-ad" was used instead (Aload, Heliad, etc.).

* * *

{#fn-thalamos .footnote}
*Thalamos*: the private chambers, master bedroom. The original uses an antiquated Russian word with the same meaning. [TN]

* * *

{#fn-kline .footnote}
*Kline*: ancient Greek furniture, served as a bed or a couch. The original uses an antiquated Russian word with a similar meaning of "something to lie on". [TN]

* * *

{#fn-9 .footnote}
*Lawagetas*: a leader of the army, commander.

* * *

{#fn-10 .footnote}
*Enyalios*: "the Warlike"---a common epithet of Ares, the god of war.

* * *

{#fn-pantelis .footnote}
*Pantelis the River-God*: a reference to the joke poem "The morning after they found three corpses there" by M. Bukanov. [TN]

* * *

{#fn-11 .footnote}
*Peplos*: a loose-fitting outer garment worn by women.

* * *

{#fn-12 .footnote}
*Boiotia*: the central and most fertile region of Central Greece, with the capital in Thebes.

* * *

{#fn-13 .footnote}
*Hekatombaion*: corresponds roughly to the period from July 15th to August 15th.

* * *

{#fn-14 .footnote}
*Aegletes*: "the Radiant"---one of Apollo's epithets.

* * *

{#fn-15 .footnote}
*Stasimon*: a choral ode performed between the epeisodia of a Greek tragedy. Literally, "a standing song."

* * *

{#fn-16 .footnote}
*Hekate*: the night goddess, the patroness of magic and spirits, but at the same time the bestower of earthly blessings, aiding with childbirth. Her attributes are dogs, snakes, keys, and daggers.

* * *

{#fn-17 .footnote}
*Wanax (wanaka)*: a king. (Mycenae was ruled by kings of higher rank and social standing than basileis.)

* * *

{#fn-18 .footnote}
*Pelops*: a son of Tantalos, killed by his own father (Tantalos, having prepared a meal from Pelops's flesh, served it to the Olympians) and restored to life by Hermes at Zeus's command. Pelops later treacherously hurled Myrtilos the charioteer, son of Hermes, from a cliff---thereby bringing a curse down upon his entire line. Among the Pelopids there followed bloody feuds between brothers, matricides, and the like.

* * *

{#fn-19 .footnote}
*Kyllenios*: the Kyllenian, an epithet of Hermeias (Hermes), born on Mount Kyllene in Arkadia.

* * *

{#fn-20 .footnote}
*Palaestra*: a private gymnastics school for boys aged twelve to sixteen. Palaestras had open courtyards, running tracks, pools, covered gymnasia, and so on.

* * *

{#fn-21 .footnote}
*Autolykos*: literally "a wolf unto himself," i.e., "a lone wolf."

* * *

{#fn-22 .footnote}
*Rejoice*: Gk. *chaire*, the customary greeting among the Hellenes.

* * *

{#fn-23 .footnote}
*Mousagetes*: "the Leader of the Muses", one of Apollo's epithets.

* * *

{#fn-24 .footnote}
*Titanomachy*: the war between the gods and the Titans, as a result of which most of the defeated Titans were cast down into Tartaros.

* * *

{#fn-25 .footnote}
*Kylix*: a ladle-shaped drinking cup.

* * *

{#fn-26 .footnote}
*Graiai*: old hags, sisters of the Gorgons, who shared a single eye and a single tooth between them.

* * *

{#fn-27 .footnote}
*Dromos*: Gk. "corridor".

* * *

{#fn-28 .footnote}
Back then iron was more valuable than gold.

* * *

{#fn-29 .footnote}
*Momos*: the god of mockery and slander, whose wise counsels proved ruinous to whoever heeded them; his epithet was "truthful through lies."

* * *

{#fn-30 .footnote}
*Talaria*: the proper name of Hermes's winged sandals.

* * *

{#fn-31 .footnote}
*Strophe*: a part of a stasimon.

* * *

{#fn-32 .footnote}
*Antistrophe*: a part of a stasimon.

* * *

{#fn-33 .footnote}
*Epode*: the concluding part of a stasimon.

* * *

{#fn-34 .footnote}
*Pous (pl. podes)*: a foot, about 30 cm.

* * *

{#fn-miasma .footnote}
*Miasma*: ritual pollution incurred by bloodshed or sacrilege, which barred a person from sacrifices and communal life until removed by purification. [TN]

* * *

{#fn-35 .footnote}
*Orgyia (pl. orgyiai)*: a unit of length, around two meters.

* * *

{#fn-36 .footnote}
*Telestas*: a nobleman, close to the ruler.

* * *

{#fn-37 .footnote}
*Metageitnion*: corresponds roughly to the period from August 15th to September 15th.

* * *

{#fn-38 .footnote}
*Rhapsode*: otherwise called an *aoidos*, a reciter of poetry.

* * *

{#fn-39 .footnote}
*Helikon*: the mountain where the Muses gathered.

* * *

{#fn-40 .footnote}
*Talent*: a unit of weight, roughly 26 kilograms.

* * *

{#fn-khumar .footnote}
*Khumar*: an authors' anachronistic joke. The word "khumar" exists in Russian, meaning a hangover state. It was borrowed from Arabic through Turkic languages, where it had the same meaning. But it is very unlikely that it would be in use in the Bronze Age Greece. [TN]

* * *

{#fn-41 .footnote}
*Euros*: the southeast wind, brother of Zephyros, Notos, and Boreas.

* * *

{#fn-42 .footnote}
*Exodos*: the concluding song performed as the chorus withdraws from the stage.

* * *

{#fn-43 .footnote}
Eurystheus refused to count two of Herakles's labors---the Lernaian Hydra and the Augean stables---since Herakles had not performed them alone, and for the stables he had even demanded payment; thus the ten labors became twelve, and their completion took twelve years.

* * *

{#fn-apollodorus .footnote}
All but the last paragraph are quoted from (Pseudo-)Apollodorus, *The Library*, translation by James G. Frazer (1921). The last paragraph is paraphrased from Apollonius, *The Argonautica*. [TN]

* * *

{#fn-pushkin .footnote}
*This tale is a fib...*: the original quotes A. S. Pushkin, *The tale of the golden rooster*; here we use the translation by Vladimir Gurvich. The quote continues as "... and yet, / Lessons can be learned from that.", which is a connection every Russian speaker immediately makes. [TN]

* * *

{#fn-odyssey-1 .footnote}
This and the previous stanza quoted from Homer, *Odyssey*, translation by William Cullen Bryant (1873). [TN]

* * *

{#fn-44 .footnote}
*The Phlegraian Fields*: literally "the burning grounds" (Gk.); located on the Chalkidiki peninsula, on its westernmost prong, Pallene. [Note that today's Phlegraian Fields are located near Naples. The Pallene location follows the classical sources (Herodotus, Strabo, Pindar) who placed the site of Gigantomachy there, and mentioned that the Italian region got its name after the Gigantomachy myth due to its fiery geology.---TN]

* * *

{#fn-hyksos .footnote}
A reference to a Russian saying "an uninvited guest is worse than a Tatar". Unfortunately, "Hyksos" does not have an established singular form. [TN]

* * *

{#fn-45 .footnote}
*Lykos*: Greek for "wolf"; i.e. Lykos of Thebes means "the Theban Wolf."

* * *

{#fn-46 .footnote}
Kopreus, son of Pelops and Eurystheus's uncle on his mother's side, fled from Elis to Mycenae after committing a murder, and was purified of miasma there.

* * *

{#fn-47 .footnote}
During the Trojan War, the Cretan king Idomeneus (grandson of Minos and, in a sense, the Minotaur's nephew) appears already as a vassal of Mycenae.

* * *

{#fn-48 .footnote}
The city was named Ilion in honor of Ilos (father of the present ruler, Laomedon); it was named Troy in honor of Tros, father of Ilos and grandfather of Laomedon.

* * *

{#fn-49 .footnote}
*Rhyton*: a sacred vessel for pouring libations to the gods.

* * *

{#fn-50 .footnote}
*Lekythos*: A ladle.

* * *

{#fn-51 .footnote}
*Erytheia*: the purple island of the Sunset, past the Okeanos-stream; home to the three-bodied giant Geryon, grandson of Medusa the Gorgon, whose herds were tended by the Gigas Eurytion (Eurytion---Greek for "kinsman of Eurytos") and the two-headed hound Orthros.

* * *

{#fn-52 .footnote}
*Lysios*: "the Loosener," "the Deliverer."

* * *

{#fn-53 .footnote}
*The Gorgons*: daughters of Phorcys the Old Man of the Sea and Ceto the Abyssal: Stheno, "the Forceful"; Euryale, "the Far-Roaming"; Medusa, "the Protectress" (Gk.).

* * *

{#fn-54 .footnote}
*Thyraios*: "of the Door"; one of the most ancient epithets of Apollo.

* * *

{#fn-55 .footnote}
The sacred animals, plants, attributes, and cult centers of Apollo.

* * *

{#fn-56 .footnote}
*Naulon*: the fee paid to Charon for passage across the Acheron.

* * *

{#fn-57 .footnote}
*Admetos*, basileus of Pherai, an Argonaut---Apollo's favorite, whom the god had saved from death more than once. Seven years before the events described, Admetos had asked his friends, his parents, and his household to die in his place (that is, to be an atoning sacrifice). The only one who agreed was Admetos's wife Alkestis---whom Herakles later wrested away from Thanatos, the god of death. It was afterward hushed up why Apollo's favorite, for all his virtues, had no intention of dying himself, offering that honor to others instead.

* * *

{#fn-58 .footnote}
*Menoites*, son of Keuthonymos---herdsman of the cattle belonging personally to Hades. It was Menoites the herdsman who once reported to Three-Bodied Geryon that Herakles was stealing his cattle, and later reported to Hades that Herakles had killed Geryon.

* * *

{#fn-59 .footnote}
*Maionia*: the ancient name for Lydia; the "Maionians" were the local tribes, its native population.

* * *

{#fn-60 .footnote}
A little later, Pergamon, Epidauros, and the island of Kos would become centers of the cult of Asklepios.

* * *

{#fn-61 .footnote}
*Shekel*: a Hittite unit of currency, about ten grams of silver; one sixtieth of a silver *mina*.

* * *

{#fn-62 .footnote}
*Ida*: a mountain range east of Troy.

* * *

{#fn-63 .footnote}
*Agora*: a square.

* * *

{#fn-64 .footnote}
Through his father, the pious Aiakos, Telamon is a grandson of Zeus; through his mother, Endeis, a grandson of the centaur Cheiron.

* * *

{#fn-65 .footnote}
Telamon's son, Ajax---who will take part in the future Trojan War, and be the strongest of the Greeks---will lose to Odysseus the contest for the slain Achilles's armor and, in a fit of madness, slaughter a herd of cattle, believing he is killing his dishonest comrades-in-arms; then fall on his own sword.

* * *

{#fn-66 .footnote}
Laomedon's youngest son Podarkes, before becoming king of Troy, was ransomed out of formal slavery by his sister Hesione, who gave up her peplos for her brother; Podarkes means "given," Priam means "ransomed."

* * *

{#fn-68 .footnote}
*Ate*: the goddess of deception.

* * *

{#fn-69 .footnote}
*Eubouleus*: literally "good counsel," an epithet of many gods.

* * *

{#fn-70 .footnote}
*Alastor*: "Evil eye" (Gk.); a demon of corruption and the evil eye who dwells in Hades.

* * *

{#fn-71 .footnote}
*Promachos*: "the Warrior," one of Athena's epithets.

* * *

{#fn-72 .footnote}
*Dikte*: a mountain on Crete, in a cave of which Rhea hid the infant Zeus from Kronos, who was devouring his children.

* * *

{#fn-73 .footnote}
*Pallas*: "the vanquisher of Pallas," the epithet Athena received after the Gigantomachy.

* * *

{#fn-74 .footnote}
*Attam*: the Greek pronunciation of the name "Abraham." On his way for the apples of the Hesperides, Herakles passed through Palestine, where he stopped Attam (Abraham) from sacrificing his own son Akab (Jacob) to a god. Herakles's companions explained to Attam that the mighty "Achivite" (as the Achaians were called in Palestine) was a son of a god, and his messenger. [This is the authors' intentional mystification. Translated as is. [TN]]

* * *

{#fn-75 .footnote}
The name Zeus actually sounds more like "Dzeus."

* * *

{#fn-76 .footnote}
The word "Hell" derives from "Hades." [in Russian; while "Hell" in English also derives from "a concealed place", it is not related to "Hades." [TN]]

* * *

{#fn-77 .footnote}
Iolaos became an Olympic victor in the chariot race almost immediately after the Gigantomachy.

* * *

{#fn-78 .footnote}
Endeis, the daughter of the centaur Cheiron, was the mother of Peleus and Telamon.

* * *

{#fn-79 .footnote}
*Taraxippos*: literally "horse-frightener"; a demon from among Hades's subjects.

* * *

{#fn-80 .footnote}
*Harmonia* was the wife of Kadmos, the founder of Thebes; her wedding necklace (a gift from Aphrodite) was a source of misfortune for its owner.

* * *

{#fn-81 .footnote}
To clean the Augean stables, Herakles dammed the rivers Alpheios and Peneios, and the flood of water washed away all the dung (rumor has it, along with the stables).

* * *

{#fn-odyssey-2 .footnote}
Quoted from Homer, *Odyssey*, translation by William Cullen Bryant (1873). The last four lines added by the authors. [TN]

* * *

{#fn-82 .footnote}
*The Island of Pelops*: the Peloponnese.

* * *

{#fn-83 .footnote}
In single combat for the right to become Deianeira's husband, Herakles defeated Acheloos, who had taken the form of a bull, breaking off one of his horns.

* * *

{#fn-84 .footnote}
*Priapos*: the son of Dionysos and the nymph Chione (in another version, of Aphrodite), the possessor of manly endowments so exceptional that they had to be carried about on a cart.

* * *

{#fn-85 .footnote}
*Protesilaos*: Gk. "Iolaos the First" and at the same time "First of the People", son of Iphikles, basileus of Phylake, would go down in the history of the Trojan War as the warrior who was the first to fall at Troy. Although after his death, unlike other dead men, he appears (accompanied by Hermeias) to his wife Laodameia and talks with her---and Laodameia vanishes from Phylake, having staged her own suicide.
