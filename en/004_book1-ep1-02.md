#### 2

Crossing the courtyard, Amphitryon ran lightly up the steps and entered the house he had left almost a year before, when he set off on the campaign against the Taphians.

[Was it worth it, all those captured islands?]{.thoughts} he thought, as he approached the thalamos[\*](#fn-thalamos){.noteref}, and smiled joylessly, leaving the question unanswered. The slave girl, curled up asleep on the threshold and snuffling contentedly, did not wake at his coming---so he had to give her a kick first, and then clamp a hand over her mouth, lest she wake the whole household with a shriek of fright. When it dawned on the foolish girl that no one meant to violate her (which disappointed her not a little), and that this was merely the long-absent master come home, she scurried off into the depths of the house, while Amphitryon unclasped the fibula of his cloak, let it fall to the floor, stepped across the threshold, and froze like a callow youth.

He had had women. He, a thirty-three-year-old man, had had a great many women---both before Alkmene and after her; he knew every trick of the priestesses of Aphrodite, he knew the fleeting passions of the daughters and sisters of those hospitable hosts in whose houses he had had occasion to stay, he had tasted the keen pleasure drawn from the terror and pain of captive women, many of them not yet come of age---but never had he loved anyone as he loved this woman sprawled across the kline[\*](#fn-kline){.noteref}, the daughter of his uncle Elektryon, the daughter of his own sister Anaxo, his niece, his cousin, and his wife, all at once.

To the point of oblivion.

Fiercely, and with utter self-abandon.

With the kind of love the gods do not forgive.

Amphitryon sharply exhaled air that had suddenly begun to burn painfully, and dropped to his knees beside the kline, tearing off his linen chiton and flinging it aside, burying his face in the all-permitting heat of her, before he could even wonder that the bedding lay crumpled and tossed about, as though lavish offerings had been made upon it to Cypris the Black, mistress of carnal pleasures, or as though Alkmene had been tormented all night by nightmares.

He did not think, and did not wonder.

He was clumsy and greedy and unusually hasty.

He---was.

...When the power of thought returned to him, he sank back wearily against the pillows, flinging wide his powerful, corded, scarred arms, and stared blankly up at the ceiling.

Something would not let him relax.

This was not how he had pictured the moment of his return. Everything had been too ordinary, too quick and bland, and dissatisfaction lodged in his mind like a splinter, keeping him from calming down and saying to himself: [Well, I'm home at last!]{.thoughts}

Amphitryon's heavy palm, calloused from spear and sword-hilt, settled absently on the breast of his wife, who had still not fully woken.

Her breast was damp and hot.

"Praise heaven," Alkmene murmured sleepily, arching a little beneath his touch. "And here you told me---a wound, you said, some stupid spear-jab... and that's why it won't work like it used to. Well, it worked at last... Like it used to. Lying about that wound, weren't you? The captive women wore you out, didn't they?"

At first he did not understand.

"What wound?" he asked, raising his head.

Alkmene did not answer. She was breathing evenly and deeply, and the tangled mane of her black hair glimmered faintly in the twilight of the thalamos.

"What wound, I said?" Amphitryon repeated irritably, going cold with a strange foreboding that at this very moment something in his fate was tearing, only no one knew it yet, and nothing could be changed anymore.

"Your wound, what else," Alkmene answered without opening her eyes, a faint huskiness in her voice, the kind that used to stir him so. "Why, you've been at it since practically midnight... tried some five times, this way and that---and then you grind your teeth and pace back and forth! Well, sleep, sleep, you need your rest... don't you go off anywhere again, my love, let's live like ordinary folk... I'll bear you a son... two... and a daughter..."

He waited until Alkmene had fallen fully asleep, then rose and went out into the courtyard wearing nothing but a loincloth.

The sky was gray and washed-out.

Amphitryon Perseid stared into the sky, and only the enemies he had slain in his many battles could have confirmed it: yes, it was with just such eyes that he looked at us as he dealt the final blow. Not for nothing did people whisper that in the gaze of Perseus the Gorgon-Slayer, and of all his descendants, something remained of the gaze of Medusa, who turned the living into Parian marble.

"Whoever you are," Amphitryon growled hoarsely, purple veins bulging on his neck, "whoever you are---be cursed! Do you hear? Cursed!"

Then he fell to his knees, as he had fallen before his wife's kline, and pressed his forehead into the sand of the courtyard, gone cold overnight.

The frightened slave girl peeked out of the house, gave a soundless gasp, and ducked back inside.
