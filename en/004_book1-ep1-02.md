#### 2

Crossing the courtyard, Amphitryon ran lightly up the steps and entered the house he had left almost a year before, setting off on the campaign against the Taphians.

"Was it worth it, all those captured islands?" he thought, approaching the thalamos[^thalamos], and smiled joylessly, leaving the question unanswered. The slave girl asleep on the threshold did not wake at his coming, curled into a ball and breathing sweetly in her sleep --- so he had to give her a kick first, and then clamp a hand over her mouth, lest she wake the whole household with a shriek of fright. When it dawned on the foolish girl that no one meant to violate her (which disappointed her not a little), and that this was merely the long-absent master come home, she scurried off into the depths of the house, while Amphitryon unclasped the fibula of his cloak, let it fall to the floor, stepped across the threshold, and froze like a boy.

[^thalamos]: The private chambers, master bedroom. The original uses an antiquated Russian word with the same meaning. [TN]

He had had women. He, a thirty-three-year-old man, had had a great many women --- both before Alcmene and after her; he knew every trick of the priestesses of Aphrodite, he knew the chance passion of the daughters and sisters of those hospitable hosts in whose houses he had had occasion to stay, he had tasted the keen pleasure of the terror and pain of captive women, many of them not yet come of age --- but never had he loved anyone as he loved this woman sprawled across the kline[^kline], the daughter of his uncle Electryon, the daughter of his own sister Anaxo, his niece, his cousin, and his wife, all at once.

[^kline]: Ancient Greek furniture, served as a bed or a couch. The original uses an antiquated Russian word with a similar meaning of "something to lie on". [TN]

To the point of oblivion.

Fiercely, and with utter self-abandon.

With the kind of love the gods do not forgive.

Amphitryon exhaled sharply, the air gone suddenly painfully searing, and dropped to his knees beside the kline, tearing off his linen chiton and flinging it aside, burying his face in the hot permissiveness of her, without even having time to marvel that the bedding lay crumpled and tossed about, as though lavish offerings had been made upon it to Cypris the Black, mistress of carnal pleasures, or as though Alcmene had been tormented all night by nightmares.

He did not think, and did not marvel.

He was clumsy and greedy and unaccustomedly hasty.

He --- was.

* * *

...When the capacity for reasoning returned to him, he sank back wearily against the pillows, flinging wide his powerful arms, knotted with muscle and scarred, and stared blankly up at the ceiling.

Something would not let him relax.

This was not how he had pictured the moment of his return. Everything had come out too ordinary, too quick and flavorless, and a splinter of dissatisfaction sat lodged in his mind, keeping him from settling down and saying to himself: "Well, I'm home at last!"

Amphitryon's heavy palm, calloused from spear and sword-hilt, settled mechanically on the breast of his wife, who had still not fully woken.

Her breast was damp and hot.

"Praise heaven," Alcmene murmured sleepily, arching a little beneath his touch. "And here you told me --- a wound, you said, got jabbed with a spear in a stupid way... and that's why it wasn't working, like before. Well, it worked this time... Like before. Lying about that wound, weren't you? Wore yourself out on captive women, did you?"

At first he did not understand.

"What wound?" he asked, raising his head.

Alcmene did not answer. She was breathing evenly and deeply, and the tangled mane of her black hair glimmered faintly in the twilight of the thalamos.

"What wound, though?" Amphitryon repeated irritably, going cold with a strange foreboding that something in his fate was tearing right now, only no one knew it yet, and nothing could be changed anymore.

"Your wound, what else," Alcmene answered without opening her eyes, a faint huskiness in her voice, the kind that used to stir him so. "Why, you've been struggling with it since practically midnight... started up some five times, this way and that --- and then you grind your teeth and pace back and forth! Well, sleep, sleep, you need your rest... don't you go off anywhere again, my love, let's live like ordinary folk... I'll bear you a son... two... and a daughter..."

He waited until Alcmene had fallen fully asleep, then rose and went out into the courtyard wearing nothing but a loincloth.

The sky was gray and faded.

Amphitryon Perseid stared into the sky, and only the enemies he had slain in his many battles could have confirmed it: yes, it was with just such eyes that he had looked upon them as he dealt the final blow. Not for nothing did people whisper that in the gaze of Perseus the Gorgon-Slayer, and of all his descendants, something remained of the gaze of Medusa, who turned the living into Parian marble.

"Whoever you are," Amphitryon growled hollowly, purple veins bulging on his neck, "whoever you are --- be damned! Do you hear? Damned!"

Then he fell to his knees, as he had fallen before his wife's kline, and pressed his forehead into the sand of the courtyard, gone cold overnight.

The frightened slave girl peeked out of the house, gave a soundless gasp, and hid herself again.
