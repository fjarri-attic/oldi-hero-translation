#### 13

...Soon, soon the strings will ring, soon the all-wise rhapsodes will sing of how the serpent-legged giants and the blessed gods fought for power over the world, how Zeus's thunderbolts blazed, how boulders and burning trees flew up into the sky.

All will be remembered by name: how Apollo pierced the left eye of the Gigas Ephialtes with a golden arrow, how Poseidon brought down part of the island of Kos upon the Gigas Polybotes, and how Athena crushed the fleeing Enceladus with Sicily, how the prophetic Moirai slew the Gigantes Agrius and Thoon, who fought with bronze clubs, how Hephaestus hurled red-hot stones at the monstrous Clytius, Dionysus struck Eurytus with his thyrsus, Hecate struck Mimas with her torch, unseen Hermes slew the Gigas Hippolytus; and the Thunderer struck down into the dust Porphyrion, king of the giants, and many, many others...

All will be remembered; and godlike Heracles, the best of mortals, will not be forgotten.

Even the people of Kos and Sicily --- the islands the Olympians heaped upon their foes --- will eagerly sing along in the common chorus.

If not now, then later depictions of the Gigantomachy will adorn the Pergamon Altar, the northern frieze of the Siphnian Treasury at Delphi, the shield of the Athena statue in the Parthenon --- and no one, anywhere, ever, will tell of it, sing of it, or even learn of it: how, late that night, having returned to the orphaned Phlegrae, the twins buried the small, cooling bodies...
