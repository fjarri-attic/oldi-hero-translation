#### 13

...Soon, soon the strings will ring, soon the sagacious rhapsodes will sing of how the serpent-legged giants and the blessed gods fought for power over the world, how Zeus's thunderbolts blazed, how boulders and burning trees flew up into the sky.

All will be remembered by name: how Apollo pierced the left eye of the giant Ephialtes with a golden arrow, how Poseidon brought down part of the island of Kos upon the giant Polybotes while Athena crushed the fleeing Enkelados with Sicily, how the prophetic Moirai slew the giants Agrios and Thoon, who fought with bronze clubs, how Hephaistos hurled red-hot stones at the monstrous Klytios, Dionysos struck Eurytos with his thyrsus, Hekate struck Mimas with her torch, unseen Hermes slew the giant Hippolytos; and the Thunderer cast into the dust Porphyrion, king of the giants, and many, many others...

All will be remembered; and godlike Herakles, the best of mortals, will not be forgotten.

Even the people of Kos and Sicily---the islands the Olympians heaped upon their foes---will dutifully join in the chorus.

If not now, then later, depictions of the Gigantomachy will adorn the Pergamon Altar, the northern frieze of the Siphnian Treasury at Delphi, the shield of Athena's statue in the Parthenon---and no one, anywhere, ever will tell, or sing, or even learn how, late that night, having returned to orphaned Phlegra, the twins buried the small, cold bodies...
