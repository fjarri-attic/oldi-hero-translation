#### 15

The news that the guest of honor was leaving, and leaving at once, stunned the city.

The Thebans tried with all their might to persuade Iolaos to stay for a week... for three days... for just one!---Iolaos was adamant.

They even showed him a sacred place about which no one knew for certain whether it boded good or ill; and for that reason they tried to keep silent about everything connected with this ambiguous relic.

The place was a deep crack in the earth, west of the city, which had appeared quite recently and under strange circumstances---Amphiaraos the Soothsayer, foreseeing the rout, had managed after all to flee from beneath the walls of Thebes in his chariot, but the earth had opened up and swallowed the Argonaut-seer.

No particular omens had accompanied this, and most of the Achaians were inclined to think that the gods had taken Amphiaraos up to Olympos alive, though they were puzzled all the same: why cast a holy man down into the earth only to raise him up afterward?

Iolaos stood over the crack for a while, spat into it, and ordered the horses harnessed.

As if to say: he had been a guest long enough, best not to outstay his welcome!

"Perhaps there is some wish of yours we might grant?" the Thebans asked.

"Such as?" Iolaos grinned crookedly, and the townsfolk thought that a young man who had not yet lived even a quarter of a century should not grin like that. "I have no wishes. Except, perhaps... when I die, bury me in the tholos of my beloved grandfather Amphitryon. Agreed?"

And, without waiting for an answer, he walked off to the chariot, while behind his back the Thebans exchanged glances and whispered that the guest's request (or was it a joke?) bordered on sacrilege.

At the very last moment a stout, breathless woman in a hastily thrown-on peplos ran up to Iolaos.

"I," she mumbled, "I'm a sick-nurse... from the house of Teiresias, my lord!... a sick-nurse, I am..."

"Dead?!" With a pained grimace, Iolaos reined in the horses and for some reason glanced up at the sky. "Is the old man dead?!"

"No, my lord! He's alive, alive, only he's in a bad way... this morning the fever had him shaking so, we thought---that's it, the old soothsayer's done for! He pulled through... and he was saying all sorts of things. I figured---since it was about Herakles, I ought to tell you..."

"What things?"

"Tell Amphitryon... yes, yes, that's just what he said, he's gone clean senile!---tell Amphitryon, he said, that Herakles will die by the hand of one already dead. Let him..."

"Let him what?!"

"I don't know, my lord! He didn't say. 'Let him...' and that's all."

Iolaos swore and sent the horses straight into a gallop.
