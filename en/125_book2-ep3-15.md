#### 15

The news that the guest of honor was leaving, and leaving at once, stunned the city.

The Thebans tried with all their might to persuade Iolaus to stay for a week... for three days... for just one!---Iolaus was adamant.

They even showed him a sacred place about which no one knew for certain whether it boded good or ill; and for that reason they tried to keep silent about everything connected with this ambiguous relic.

The place was a deep crack in the earth, west of the city, which had appeared quite recently and under strange circumstances---Amphiaraus the Soothsayer, sensing the rout, had managed after all to flee from Thebes in his chariot, but the earth had opened up and swallowed the Argonaut-seer.

No particular omens had accompanied this, and most of the Achaeans inclined to the opinion that the gods had taken Amphiaraus up to Olympus alive, while wondering all the same: why drop a holy man down only to raise him up afterwards?

Iolaus stood over the crack for a while, spat into it, and ordered the horses harnessed.

As if to say: he had been a guest long enough, best not to outstay his welcome!

"Perhaps there is some wish of yours we might grant?" the Thebans asked.

"Such as?" Iolaus grinned crookedly, and the townsfolk thought that a young man who had not lived even a quarter of a century should not grin like that. "I have no wishes. Except, perhaps... when I die, bury me in the tholos of my beloved grandfather Amphitryon. Agreed?"

And, without waiting for an answer, he walked off to the chariot, while behind his back the Thebans exchanged glances and whispered that the guest's request (or was it a joke?) bordered on sacrilege.

At the very last moment a stout, breathless woman in a hastily thrown-on peplos ran up to Iolaus.

"I," she mumbled, "I'm a nurse... from the house of Tiresias, my lord!.. a nurse, I am..."

"Dead?!" Wincing painfully, Iolaus reined in the horses and for some reason glanced up at the sky. "Is the old man dead?!"

"No, my lord! He's alive, alive, only he's in a bad way... this morning the fever had him shaking so, we thought---that's it, the old soothsayer's done for! He pulled through... and he said all sorts of words. I figured---since it was about Heracles, I ought to tell you..."

"What words?"

"Tell Amphitryon... yes, yes, that's just what he said, he's gone clean out of his mind!---tell Amphitryon, he said, that Heracles will die by the hand of one already dead. Let him..."

"Let him what?!"

"I don't know, my lord! He didn't say. Let him...---and that's all."

Iolaus swore and set off at a gallop straight from a standstill.
