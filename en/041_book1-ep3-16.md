#### 16

"...Fine, we understand," the twins answered sullenly. "If we beat someone up, that was Alkeides. If someone beat us up, that was Iphikles. Threw a discus farther---that was Alkeides..."

"Broke mom's vase---that was Alkeides too," one of the brothers added, perfectly seriously, and neither Hermeias nor Cheiron could tell which one had said it. Cheiron privately concluded it had been Alkeides; Hermeias, that it had been Iphikles.

"Oh, they must be looking for us back home!" The brothers had suddenly remembered. "We'll catch it now... both of us. Listen, Dummy: which one of us is Alkeides---the one who gets more of a hiding, or less?"

"You'll both get it equally," Hermeias comforted them, smiling. "But not today! I'll see to that! No one will even notice you were gone... Am I a god or not?!"

"Then we're definitely getting whipped!" Alkeides whispered to his brother, who at the moment cared little about Hermeias's divinity.

"Dummy, will you... well, after all this... still teach us to fight---not by the rules?"

Alkeides fixed the Trickster with the same questioning stare---clearly, this mattered a great deal to both twins.

"I will, I will," Hermeias assured the brothers.

"Seems I'll have to teach you a thing or two myself," Cheiron drawled thoughtfully.

"Teach us what?" the brothers asked in unison. "We haven't got hooves; we can't fight the way you do."

"No need. Plenty of others will teach you to fight. I'll teach you to think."

"Not by the rules?"

"Not by the rules," the centaur answered, without a trace of a smile.
