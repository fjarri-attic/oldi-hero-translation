#### 5

By midday Amphitryon's house had been cordoned off by soldiers --- for safety, and to prevent a recurrence, as the young dekarchoi explained, citing the basileus's orders while whispering incredible tales about Amphitryon's past to one another.

The soldiers loitered about the street, sweltering in their broad leather cuirasses, wandering into the courtyard and pestering the slave girls; on the whole they behaved decently enough, if intrusively --- and Amphitryon, never a man of great patience, was glad when Nasal, who had never actually left, stepped in to deal with it. Thelem --- suddenly younger, beaming, everywhere at once --- immediately took command, despite the timid protests of the dekarchoi, and, invoking the good old days, set to work. The soldiers bunched together near the gate; for a while an indistinct voice --- Nasal's --- came muffled from the middle of the huddle, after which the soldiers scattered back to their posts at a trot, but from that moment on they could be neither seen nor heard, as though Thelem had handed each of them Lord Hades's helm of invisibility.

There were no passersby on the street, but the dekarchoi, delighted to be rid of the responsibility, whispered Nasal's order to one another --- engage no one in conversation --- casting stern glances at the dogs and the heat-addled birds, which clearly wanted to strike up a conversation of their own, and that could not be allowed under any circumstances.

There was nothing else for the dekarchoi to do.

Then, some time later, Creon of Thebes turned up at Amphitryon's with his wife --- not as basileus, with almost no retinue, but ostensibly as family, which alarmed the already frazzled Alcmene no end.

The men withdrew at once to the megaron, while the women, offended by such neglect (mostly Nausicaa, since Alcmene had no time for offense), went off to the gynaeceum, where Nausicaa set about extracting details and passing on the latest gossip.

The visit, however, ended quickly enough, and Creon left with a pointedly inscrutable face, almost forgetting his wife --- lost in thought, no doubt.

At least, that was what Nasal decided, watching the terrace in front of the megaron, where Amphitryon stood leaning on the balustrade, next to Alcmene, who had just come out to join him.

What they were talking about, Thelem had no wish to hear, and prudently withdrew to a distance.

And this is what they talked about.

"Why did Creon come?"

"To congratulate us," Amphitryon answered without a trace of a smirk. "On the happy outcome."

"I know. Nausicaa told me. The gods intervened..."

"Which gods?"

"Oh, you foolish, gray-haired husband of mine... everyone knows --- only you have to ask! The impious men came to break down the gate --- and there at the gate stood Ares Enyalius, sword in hand, his sons Phobos the Fright and Deimos the Dread at his sides, Hermes and Athena Alalcomeneis to left and right, and silver-bowed Apollo on the roof!.."

"And the whole city is reaching under one of the impious fellows' chitons, feeling for the hole left by Apollo's arrow just below the small of his back," Amphitryon finished, tapping the edge of his palm against the railing. "Let them feel around... as long as they don't get the wrong hole. And Athena --- that would have been Autolycus, I expect."

"More likely Castor --- he's prettier... even if he's no virgin."

"Well, that he isn't, no arguing that," Amphitryon agreed.

"What else did Creon say?"

"He said it was Alcides who killed Linus."

"How does he know? Did he ask Tiresias?"

"He doesn't know. It simply *must be* so."

"Why?"

"There'll be a trial, Alcmene. Both brothers agree that it was Iphicles who angered Linus, by breaking a string --- Alcides was in the palaestra at the time... needling Autolycus. So: if it was Iphicles who killed Linus, then Iphicles is guilty, because a pupil who has done wrong must submit meekly to his teacher's blows. But if the killer was Alcides, then he's clean before the gods, because Linus was wrong to strike an innocent boy; and under the law of Rhadamanthys, whoever answers an unjust blow with a blow bears no guilt. Clear now?"

"Creon is wise..."

"Wise indeed."

"So it was Alcides who killed him?"

"So it seems."

"And everything will go on as before? Same as always?"

"No. Creon is wise, the basileus of Thebes... Once Alcides has been purified, I'll send him away from the city. Far enough that he won't be an eyesore to the respectable Thebans, or weigh on their minds, but close enough that we can visit him. Somewhere among the fishing villages near Lake Copais, say... no --- better to send him to Cithaeron, to the shepherds."

"For how long?"

"I don't know, Alcmene. A year, three, five --- however it turns out. I'm afraid to guess right now. I know only one thing: you bore me... me and Zeus, two sons. Thank you, Alcmene. That's all I know."

"No, you foolish, gray-haired husband of mine... I bore *you* two sons. And I'm grateful to you for them. Because it wasn't Ares who shielded the gates of our house with his own body today, and it wasn't Phobos and Deimos standing beside him. And Zeus... well, Zeus is Zeus, and tomorrow I'll go and bring him an offering."

"I'll come with you. All right?"

"No need. Better you keep an eye on the boys. And have a talk with them."

"I will. Linus's killer stands alone."

"Yes, my husband. The killer stands alone."
