#### 3

The dry, dead flesh of the forest --- one of the few forests in the lowlands of Thessaly --- crackled meekly as it burned in the campfire, leaving behind ash, bluish-gray smoke, and the warmth of rest.

"Do we leave behind anything more?" Iolaus thought, holding the girl who had gone quiet in his arms.

Laodamia was no longer weeping as she gazed at the fire --- it reminded her of the smoldering ruins of her native city --- and her reddened, wide-open eyes looked trustingly into the face of her savior, who had arrived just in time, like...

Like a god --- but for some reason the thought did not seem a good one.

"Where shall I take you, little one?"

"I want to be with you."

The words came easily and simply, like the fiery drops of a summer rain dancing over the puddles in the rays of a stunned Helios.

"With me --- later."

"I want it now. And always."

"Now. And always. And between them lies --- later."

"It doesn't happen like that."

"It does. You just have to live your way from 'now' to 'always.' And be able to look back."

"Then take me to Phylace. It's not far. My uncle rules there, my father's namesake --- he's an Acastus too... I'll wait for you. Even if you don't come back, I'll wait all the same."

"I'll take you to Phylace, little one. Wait for me. Even if I don't come back --- I will."

Words... words... the rain plays with the leaves come alive, and tears of joy are as salty as tears of sorrow...

"At home, in Iolcus, I often thought of you. As you stood by the tub opposite Eurytus --- naked, covered in scars, thinking about something of your own; and as you were on the hunt, when the accursed beast embraced you so terribly, and you didn't recoil from it, and your embrace was more terrible than the beast's..."

"Those were two different men."

"No. They had the same face. I dreamed of that face."

Words... fingers, hands, lips... the rain has grown quiet and wanders pensively among the trunks...

"You remind me of... a certain woman. Anyway, it doesn't matter."

"It matters. Have you had many women?"

"Many. Many --- and just one."

"It doesn't happen that way."

"It does."

"Is she beautiful?"

"She was."

"And now?"

"Now she has grown old, nods off in the middle of a conversation, and will soon die."

"It doesn't happen that way."

"It does. Let's not talk about her. Even if someday I myself ask you to --- don't talk about her. Ever."

"All right. I don't even know her name. Aren't you afraid?"

"Of what?"

"Of me. Right now, when you're kissing me..."

"I've never been afraid of kissing."

"That's not what I meant. It's just... maybe you don't know --- when I was born, an oracle told my father that my future husband would die young. That's why not one of the heroes has ever come courting me. Look how many came for Iole --- and for me, not one."

"They're fools."

"Who?"

"The heroes. They're afraid to go courting, and they're not afraid to die under the wreckage of ships, to fall from a cliff into the sea, and to cut each other's throats. And the gods laugh. Don't argue, little one, I know heroes better."

"And you?"

"I'm not one of the heroes. A different breed, understand? And I'm not afraid of any oracles. I simply don't listen to them."

"But what about --- dying young?"

"That's no danger to me."

"Why?"

"Because I've already died once. Old. I died so well back then that now nothing frightens me."

"You're strange. Strange and... reliable. I feel at peace with you. Almost as with my father, but still different. And you're only a year older than me... two? Five, no more..."

"No more. I'm older than you by a whole life, and that's much less than five years."

"It doesn't happen that way."

"It only ever happens that way. Sleep."

"I'm already asleep. You know, I used to want to bear a child by a god. Ideally, by Zeus. Like Semele with Dionysus, or Alcmene with Heracles. And now I don't. Now I want to have a child by you..."

"That's right, girl," Iolaus whispers, covering the curled-up Laodamia with his cloak. "If it's to be like Alcmene and Heracles, then we'll manage somehow without Zeus. It wouldn't be the first time. Sleep..."

:::

Waiting until the girl's breathing grows even and quiet, he raises his head, looks through a gap in the branches at the freshly washed sky, and sees what he expected to see.

The dead finger points mockingly to the southwest.

To Eleia, the domain of Augeas --- which Heracles has just invaded.
