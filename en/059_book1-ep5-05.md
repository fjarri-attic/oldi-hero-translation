#### 5

...Iphicles did not at once notice that he had been running with his eyes shut for some time already, but the forest, coming to its senses, hurled itself at his face, thrusting out a hard, gnarled palm --- and, falling on his side and snuffling through a broken nose, the youth came back to himself.

A moment before, he had been anything at all but himself.

"It can't be!" flashed the first thought, naive and useless as a wicker basket held up against a spear. Yet even a woven basket is a weapon in skilled hands, for the spear flying out from within swerved aside reluctantly, drawing back for a fresh strike; and the power of thought returned to Iphicles.

He knew what that spear meant.

Alcides's fit of madness.

Iphicles had long since learned to sense their approach at the same moment as his brother, sometimes even a little before, because at the very first instant Alcides always refused to believe that *it* was again drawing near, again closing its fingers around his throat and peering into the secret places of his soul. In recent years the madness had come upon his brother with enviable regularity, once every six months (that meant something... some conversation between Hermeias and Chiron --- and both had fallen silent as one, seeing the twins... six months? six years? six centuries?!) --- and now Iphicles cursed himself for having blindly trusted that interval, knowing that Alcides's previous fit had come almost a hundred days before. And now he had left his brother alone, face to face, with the black-braided, pale Lyssa the Rage.

The forest shuddered, tossing up its shaggy crowns, and hurled itself toward the place the tall youth with a bundle of short javelins on his shoulder had so recently been hurrying away from --- because Iphicles himself was already running back, running blindly, heedless of the path, heedless of his own breath, having flung away the javelins that hindered him, not noticing that the shoulder torn open by the Man-Eater of Cithaeron had rebelled and begun to bleed anew...

The smell of mildew grew sharper, deafening, settling in sticky flakes inside his lungs; a slick many-fingered hand reached cautiously out of nowhere and instantly recoiled, only to return with its fellows, and all of them began circling the huddled boy, a wounded beast driven into a corner and pressed to the ground, baring a hopeless, merciless snarl at its pursuers; something was not as it should be, but there was no time left to understand or weigh it, and everything spun into a furiously roaring whirl in which every particle was at once the Man-Eater of Cithaeron come alive, the terrified boy, and the slippery smell of mildew; flesh, sickness, and a healer's knife.

Something was wrong.

Iphicles tore onward without opening his eyes, cracking against tree trunks, tearing his legs on thorns and branches, falling and rising, meekly taking the whip-lashing curses of the underbrush; something was wrong, because back there in the clearing, one of the twins, trembling with fright and forgetting to wipe away the cold sweat, had for the first time snapped back and turned to face Tartarus, feeling on the back of his neck the warm breath of his brother, just as eight-year-old Iphicles had once felt it on Pelion, lifting a stone that had suddenly turned strangely light.

Prying Tartarus's fingers off himself (off himself?!) for the first time not in order to flee and hide but in order to fight and win, Iphicles understood that he had already arrived.

But his body, his stupid, uncomprehending body, went on racing, falling, rising, choking on the bitter air...

Something was wrong.

The rapture of battling phantoms lied, deceived; the bodiless legions scattered into shreds, victory turning into a senseless, endless struggle, a stone of Sisyphus; the madness of fear was becoming the madness of annihilation, remaining madness all the while --- Alcides now strained forward as before he had strained back, and once again Iphicles had to hold his brother in check; the circle had closed, and Iphicles's tightly shut eyes suddenly gained the power to see...

...faces. Twisted with rage, pain, fear, contorted and sweating, scattering aside and appearing again close by; mustached, beardless, pockmarked, young, mature, pitch-black hair caught in a strap, a scar flushing crimson on a nose...

Faces?!

...a sword. A broad bronze sword, oddly curved and tapering toward the tip, the binding of its hilt worn smooth and slipping in his palm, forcing his fingers to shift closer to the crossguard, tightening his grip...

A sword?!

Clinging with all his strength to his gasping brother from within, trying to wrench back the hand that would not obey, forcing the enemy blade to sever an ear rather than crunch into the base of a neck, tearing not Tartarus away from Alcides but Alcides away from Tartarus, straining to be in time and failing, beaten, lashed, bloodied, Iphicles already knew he had been deceived.

Everyone had deceived him.

Everyone.

Lyssa the Rage, who had forgotten the appointed time; Tartarus, delighted by the resistance; and the treacherous solitude of the Cithaeron glades.

* * *

"...I managed it! I managed it! They were just frightened of me --- Chiron was right!... I managed it..."

His eyelids, stuck together, would not obey; opening them was as impossible as it would have been for Tiresias to see --- but Iphicles had to do it, even if the effort proved to be the last of his life.

Alcides lay face up among the roots of an old elm that had broken through the surface, a smoking sword clenched fast in his hand, and beside the blood-smeared blade lay someone's severed hand.

A ring set with a large chrysolite gleamed on its finger.

Slumped back against a tree, a stranger with the face of a boar stared at Iphicles. The tip of his nose had been hacked off, and with every breath red, mucus-slick bubbles swelled around his terribly flared nostrils.

And the oleander bushes moaned.

"Chiron was right!... I managed it..."

"Yes," Iphicles answered, barely audible, not noticing that he had fallen to his knees. "Yes, Alcides."

"Iphicles... is it true? Did I really manage it?"

"Yes, Alcides. It's true."

The truth smelled of pain and mildew.

And behind the kneeling Iphicles stood an invisible warrior in a horsehair-crested helmet that all but completely concealed his face.
