#### 6

Amphitryon went bounding toward the palaestra, whistling, turning over in his mind the chance, granted to him quite by accident, to savor his childhood consciously.

How many, besides him, can boast of that?

Life, for all its snares and machinations, still sometimes showed him its pleasant side; especially these last days, when the words "Grandpa said so!" were enough for him to get his way, no appeal possible---whether about the training schedule or anything else.

[Rejoice, Iolaos,]{.thoughts} Amphitryon thought. [Rejoice, until---]{.thoughts}

"Rejoice, Iolie! Oh, how you've grown, how you've grown! Quite the big boy now!..."

And childhood was over.

The dust swirling behind him suddenly quickened its pace, caught up, surrounded him, powdering his throat with acrid ash and making his eyes water; the shaggy cloud blotted out the sun, and Amphitryon was forced to remember who he truly was.

He had been waiting for that voice.

That very voice---because back on the Isles of the Blessed, on the far side of the Acheron, Hermeias had told him that Three-Formed Hekate was absolutely certain of the death of her former priestess, Galinthias.

Certain.

Of her death.

...The boy turned slowly---too slowly for a six-year-old, to the mind of Galinthias, daughter of Proitos; and the old woman's little eyes, flashing predatorily in the gathering dusk, looked Iolaos over from head to foot.

Too thoroughly, to the mind of Amphitryon, son of Alkaios of Mycenae.

"Well then, did you recognize the old woman, little one?"

"I did, grandma Galinthias. As if I wouldn't recognize you! Mom speaks of you often..."

Amphitryon tried with all his might to make his voice sound sufficiently friendly.

With little success.

"And where are you running off to, Iolie, with night coming on?" The old woman took a few small, birdlike steps and was suddenly right beside him.

She stood barely a head taller than the child.

[As if you don't know!]{.thoughts} Amphitryon smirked to himself. [You're lying, old woman---you know full well what a trading caravan is bringing from Megara a day before it enters Thebes! Or which infant will have a birthmark on its tailbone---a week before it's even born!]{.thoughts}

He was very much afraid of making a mistake.

He remembered the golden disc he himself had once given this half-mad old woman.

"To the palaestra, grandma."

"Oh, good boy! Oh, what a little toiler, son of a hero, grandson of a hero!... Rosy little cheeks, shot up like an ash tree, you've got the look of Zeus's own eagle about you..."

Amphitryon made the secret sign against the evil eye, almost without thinking.

This did not escape the old woman's notice, and once again she recalled the crimson darkness that had poured over her from the unchildlike gaze of Iolaos when he came to after a two-week fever.

Galinthias was very much afraid of making a mistake.

A mistake could cost her dearly---of late, the Fallen had seldom favored their Possessed one with candor.

"Well, can I run along now, grandma?" Amphitryon added fuel to the fire, shifting impatiently from foot to foot.

"Run along, dearie, run along! All of Thebes is saying: Iolie's grandpa-lawagetas comes to him in dreams, helping his sons to raise a mighty grandson... You must obey your grandpa, little one, grandpa would never steer you wrong! He'll raise Iolie into a real man! Now I'd love to hear it myself, old as I am---what does a grandpa like yours tell his beloved grandson?!"

The old woman's voice betrayed her with a tremor, and through it, like the slender body of a polecat slipping through sedge, there flickered a poorly concealed excitement.

Then again, this unhealthy interest might simply have been an old woman's curiosity---as Amphitryon did not fail to remind himself.

"Grandpa said all sorts of things... that I should grow strong, that I should eat well, that I shouldn't forget to bring him offerings. Only..."

"Only what?" Galinthias leaned forward involuntarily. "Only what, Iolie?! Don't be shy now, I'm no stranger to you, after all---I'll help however I can!"

"Only I didn't tell dad and uncle Alkeides everything. And grandma Alkmene least of all. I got scared..."

"Now what were you scared of, little one? Go on and tell me, don't be afraid of old Galinthias---I treated your father when he was a child, and your uncle too, and you yourself---look how healthy you turned out! Your grandpa gave Galinthias a golden disc as a keepsake, see---what a beauty! Don't tell your own folks, tell me! Grandma Galinthias will understand, grandma Galinthias will help!"

[Could *this* really be passed down by blood?]{.thoughts} flashed through the old woman's mind. [Strange... then what of Alkeides's sons? Or are they still too young?!]{.thoughts}

"Grandpa asked for an offering. Only not an ordinary one---he needs... he needs... he needs a human sacrifice! There. But how am I supposed to offer him a human sacrifice?!---I'm little, I won't manage! And I don't even know how... But grandpa keeps asking and asking---he must need it very badly..."

For a while Galinthias said nothing, gazing at the boy with a strange half-smile. Amphitryon did not hurry her. He stood with his head bowed, not knowing that the old woman was thinking, just then, about seven-gated Thebes, too cramped for two Possessed.

And about sacrifices---the one supposedly requested by the late grandpa, and the one the Fallen had ordered made after a five-year pause.

"And if I help you, little one," the old woman said at last, parting her thin lips, "will you tell grandpa that it was old Galinthias who carried out his command?"

"Of course, grandma!" The joy in the beaming boy's voice was entirely genuine. "I'll tell him, don't you doubt it! Oh, how happy grandpa will be!... Don't worry, he doesn't forget a favor. Never. Should I tell dad?"

"Dad? No, no need to tell dad. Why trouble him for nothing?"

"Right! Why trouble him? So shall we go make the sacrifice right now?"

"To grandpa, you mean?" (Amphitryon suddenly realized that the old woman stubbornly refused to speak his name, calling the dead man just "grandpa.") "No, we won't go now. This isn't something to rush; it needs preparing for. You wait, Iolie, wait and wait some more, and I'll find you myself when the time comes! All right?"

"All right, grandma Galinthias! Only I'd..."

But the hunched little crone was already scurrying away.

And Amphitryon made his way to the palaestra, but not at a skip this time---with a heavy, unhurried gait, as though the weight of decades bore down on his sharp boyish shoulders.

Tomorrow he would have to get in touch with Hermeias, tell him everything, and persuade him not to touch the old woman until they were entirely certain they were taking the actual Tartaros-Possessed.

Which meant---until the very moment of the sacrifice.

As long as Hermeias is ready...

Tomorrow.

Tomorrow he would stop by the market and put two cured fish in the bowl of the loud-voiced rhapsode.

Amphitryon did not know that a week and three days later, a sunburned, muscular man with empty, faded eyes would approach him at the entrance to the palaestra.

"Are you Iolaos?" the man would ask curtly.

"Yes."

"Galinthias told me to let you know everything's ready. Come along."

For a moment Amphitryon would falter, realizing that this time there was no chance to warn the Trickster and wait for the god in winged sandals to appear.

But... it was too late to turn back.

The only thing left was to count on Hermeias, who promised to stay alert.

"Let's go. Only I need to run by the market first."

"Galinthias is waiting."

"Let her wait! I told you---I have to go to the market. It'll be quick."

"Well, all right... let's go!"

And the man with the glassy stare would take the boy with the unchildlike eyes by the hand, and the two of them would set off toward the city market.
