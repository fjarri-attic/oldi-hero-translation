#### 6

Amphitryon went bounding toward the palaestra, whistling, turning over in his mind the chance to savor his childhood consciously, granted to him quite by accident.

How many, besides him, could boast of that?

Life, for all its snares and machinations, still sometimes turned its pleasant side to him; especially these last days, when the words "Grandpa said so!" were enough to settle any argument irrevocably --- whether about the training schedule or anything else.

"Rejoice, Iolaus," Amphitryon thought. "Rejoice, until..."

"Rejoice, little Iolaus! Oh, how you've grown, how you've grown! Quite the big boy now!.."

And childhood was over.

The dust swirling behind him suddenly quickened its pace, caught up, surrounded him, powdering his throat with acrid ash and making his eyes water; the tousled cloud blotted out the sun, and Amphitryon was forced to remember who he truly was.

He had been waiting for that voice.

That very voice --- because back on the Fortunate Isles, on the far side of Acheron, Hermeias had told him that Hecate the Three-Formed was absolutely certain of the death of her former priestess, Galinthias.

Certain.

Of her death.

* * *

...The boy turned slowly --- too slowly for a six-year-old, in the opinion of Galinthias, daughter of Proetus; and the old woman's little eyes, flashing predatorily in the gathering dusk, felt Iolaus over from head to foot.

Too thoroughly, in the opinion of Amphitryon, son of Alcaeus of Mycenae.

"Well then, did you recognize the old woman, little one?"

"I did, grandma Galinthias. As if I wouldn't recognize you! Mom speaks of you often..."

Amphitryon tried with all his might to make his voice sound sufficiently friendly.

It wasn't working well.

"And where are you off to, little Iolaus, running about with night coming on?" The old woman took a few small, birdlike steps and suddenly found herself quite close.

She stood barely a head taller than the child.

"As if you don't know!" Amphitryon smirked to himself. "You're lying, old woman --- you know full well what a trading caravan brought from Megara a day before it enters Thebes! Or which infant will have a birthmark on its tailbone --- a week before it's even born!"

He was very much afraid of making a mistake.

He remembered the golden disc he himself had once given this half-mad old woman.

"To the palaestra, grandma."

"Oh, good boy! Oh, what a little toiler, son of a hero, grandson of a hero!.. Rosy little cheeks, shot up like an ash tree, you've got the look of Zeus's own eagle about you..."

Amphitryon made the secret sign against the evil eye, almost without thinking.

This did not escape the old woman's notice, and once again she recalled the crimson darkness that had poured over Galinthias out of the unchildlike gaze of Iolaus when he'd come to after a two-week fever.

Galinthias was very much afraid of making a mistake.

A mistake could cost her dearly --- of late, the Fallen had not spoiled their Possessed with candor.

"Well, I'll be running along now, grandma?" Amphitryon added fuel to the fire, shifting impatiently from foot to foot.

"Run along, dearie, run along! All of Thebes is saying: little Iolaus's grandpa-lawagetas comes to him in dreams, helping the sons to raise a mighty grandson... You must obey your grandpa, little one, grandpa won't teach anything bad! He'll raise little Iolaus into a real man! Now if only I, old as I am, could hear --- what a grandpa like yours tells his beloved grandson?!"

The old woman's voice betrayed her with a tremor, and through it, like the slender body of a polecat slipping through sedge, there flickered a poorly concealed excitement.

Then again, this unhealthy interest might simply have been senile curiosity --- which Amphitryon did not fail to remind himself.

"Grandpa said all sorts of things... that I should grow strong, that I should eat well, that I shouldn't forget to bring him offerings. Only..."

"Only what?" Galinthias leaned forward involuntarily. "Only what, little Iolaus?! Don't be shy now, I'm no stranger to you, after all --- whatever I can do, I'll help!"

"Only I didn't tell dad and uncle Alcides everything. And grandma Alcmene even less. I got scared..."

"Now what were you scared of, little one? Go on and tell me, don't be afraid of old Galinthias --- I treated your father when he was a child, and your uncle too, and you yourself --- look how healthy you turned out! Your grandfather gave Galinthias a golden disc as a keepsake, see --- what a beauty! Don't tell your own folks, tell me! Grandma Galinthias will understand, grandma Galinthias will help!"

"Could *this* really be passed down by blood?" flashed through the old woman's mind. "Strange... then what of Alcides's sons? Or are they still too young?!"

"Grandfather asked for an offering. Only not an ordinary one --- he needs... he needs... he needs a human sacrifice! There. But how am I supposed to make him a human sacrifice?! --- I'm little, I won't manage! And I don't even know how... But grandfather keeps asking and asking --- he must need it very badly..."

For a while Galinthias said nothing, gazing at the boy with a strange half-smile. Amphitryon did not hurry her. He stood with his head bowed, not knowing that the old woman was thinking, just then, about seven-gated Thebes, too cramped for two Possessed.

And about sacrifices --- the one supposedly requested by the late grandfather, and the one the Fallen had ordered made after a five-year pause.

"And if I help you, little one," the old woman finally unstuck her thin lips, "will you tell grandfather that it was old Galinthias who carried out his command?"

"Of course, grandma!" The joy in the beaming boy's voice was entirely genuine. "I'll tell him, don't you doubt it! Oh, how happy grandpa will be!.. Don't worry, he doesn't forget a favor. Never. Should I tell dad?"

"Dad? No, no need to tell dad. Why trouble him for nothing?"

"Right! Why trouble him? So shall we go make the sacrifice right now?"

"To the grandpa, you mean?" (Amphitryon suddenly realized that the old woman stubbornly refused to speak his name, calling the dead man just "grandpa.") "No, we won't go now. This is not a hasty business, it needs preparing for. You wait, little Iolaus, wait and wait some more, and I'll find you myself when the time comes! All right?"

"All right, grandma Galinthias! Only I'd..."

But the hunched little crone was already scurrying away.

And Amphitryon made his way to the palaestra, but not at a skip this time --- with a heavy, unhurried gait, as though the weight of decades bore down on his sharp boyish shoulders.

Tomorrow he would have to get in touch with Hermeias, tell him everything, and persuade him not to touch the old woman until they were entirely certain they were taking the actual Tartarus-Possessed.

Which meant --- until the very moment of the sacrifice.

If only Hermeias would be ready...

Tomorrow.

Tomorrow he would stop by the market and put two cured fish in the bowl of the loud-voiced rhapsode.

* * *

Amphitryon does not know that a week and three days from now, a sunburned, muscular man with empty, faded eyes will approach him at the entrance to the palaestra.

"Are you Iolaus?" the man will ask curtly.

"Yes."

"Galinthias told me to let you know everything's ready. Come along."

For a moment Amphitryon will falter, realizing that this time he won't manage to warn the Trickster and wait for the god in winged sandals to appear.

But... it was too late to turn back.

The only thing left was to count on Hermeias, who had promised to stay alert.

"Let's go. Only I need to run by the market first."

"Galinthias is waiting."

"Let her wait! I said --- to the market. It'll be quick."

"Well, all right... let's go!"

And the man with the arrested stare will take by the hand the boy with the unchildlike eyes, and the two of them will set off toward the city market.
