#### 4

"I don't like him!"

"Who?"

"I don't like him, I tell you!..."

"Who, then?!"

"That guest of yours! Eurytos!"

"And what is it about him you don't like?"

"Everything! Some basileus he is---neither fish nor fowl! And his retinue's just the same! Not a whiff of wine on them, yet they go about like drunkards... they've frightened the whole household! You're walking down a corridor, and there they are! Silent as shades out of Hades!... If they at least pestered the slave girls, I wouldn't say a word---men are men, and you're no exception, husband dear! Who should know our slave girls better than you---pinch one on the heel and she's already expecting! But these---well, if you're not going to pester anyone, at least don't go scaring people! Like basileus, like household!"

"Yes, his household's a bit off, I grant you," Kreon of Thebes agreed thoughtfully. "So what? They'll stay a while and leave. And as for basileus Eurytos himself---"

"Stay a while?!" Nausikaa flew into a rage, throwing up her plump little hands. "Stay a while?! I want them out of this house by tomorrow! It's either me or them!"

"Have you lost your mind, woman?!" Kreon, already beginning to boil over, was indignant. "What do you want me to do---throw them all out?!"

"Of course throw them out!" Kreon's wife agreed at once.

"You want me, the basileus of Thebes, to break the law of hospitality? To throw out the venerable Eurytos, basileus of Oichalia, great archer and pupil of Apollo himself?! Just because some stupid woman didn't like something about him?!"

"Not 'something,'" Nausikaa mimicked her husband, "but everything! He behaves like a madman, and he drags that girl around with him, shameless creature that he is!... The slaves shy away from him, and the children in this house started howling so hard the moment he arrived that we could barely calm them down! He's a bad man, the gods don't love him, and neither do I... He'll bring misfortune down on all of us, mark my words!"

Kreon only swore in exasperation and hurled his cup at the wall.

"Send him away, send him away before it's too late," Nausikaa wailed, passing from shouting to tears with the abruptness of a true general.

This usually did the trick, but this time Kreon had already worked himself into a genuine fury and ignored his wife's laments.

"Send him away?" he thundered. "Send him away?! Perhaps I should declare war on Oichalia while I'm at it?! Why, if this Eurytos were to carry you off, you fool, even then I wouldn't quarrel with him! You hear?! But who'd want to carry you off?! Who needs you?! Echidna of Thebes!"

"Ah, so you don't need me!" Nausikaa's tears dried instantly. "All you need are your vile guests! So you don't need me, and you don't need the children, and you don't need Thebes! All you want is to feast with your cronies! With this Eurytos, may he fall into Tartaros! No wonder his own son keeps glancing at him and pulling a face! Now there's the kind of man you ought to welcome! Iphitos is a fine man in every way: young, courteous, and---"

"Ah, so that's what this is about?! Taken a fancy to Iphitos, have you?! Drawn to the young ones now?! I see how it is! So I've lived to see this..."

This unseemly scene, which had erupted out of nowhere, went on for a good while longer. The couple had a complete falling-out and withdrew, each to their own chambers. Afterward, for the whole of Eurytos's stay in Thebes, they scarcely spoke to each other, and only once the ill-fated basileus finally departed did they make up, easily and almost at once---and then spent a long time wondering what had come over them.

But before that, a good many other things happened, and it would have been better if they hadn't.
