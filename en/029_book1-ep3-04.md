#### 4

"I don't like him!"

"Whom?"

"I don't like him, I tell you!.."

"Whom, for heaven's sake?!"

"That guest of yours! Eurytus!"

"And what is it about him you don't like?"

"Everything! Some basileus he is — neither fish nor fowl! And his retinue's just the same! Not a whiff of wine on them, yet they go about like drunkards... they've frightened the whole household! You're walking down a corridor, and there they are! Silent as shades out of Hades!.. If they at least pestered the slave girls, I wouldn't say a word — men are men, and you're no exception, husband dear! Who should know our slave girls better than you — pinch them on the heel and they're already expecting! But these — well, if they won't pester them, they could at least not frighten them! As the basileus, so his household!"

"Yes, his household's a bit off, I grant you," Creon of Thebes agreed thoughtfully. "So what? They'll visit a while and be gone. And as for basileus Eurytus himself..."

"Visit a while?!" Nausicaa positively flew into a rage, clasping her plump little hands together. "Visit a while?! I want them out of this house by tomorrow! It's either me or them!"

"Have you lost your mind, woman?!" Creon, beginning to boil himself, was outraged. "What do you want me to do — throw them all out?!"

"Of course, throw them out!" Creon's wife agreed at once.

"So that I, basileus of Thebes, should break the law of hospitality? So that I should throw out the venerable Eurytus, basileus of Oechalia, great archer and pupil of Apollo himself?! Just because some foolish woman took a dislike to him for some reason?!"

"Not 'for some reason,'" Nausicaa mimicked her husband, "but for everything! He behaves like a madman, and he drags that girl around with him, shameless creature that he is!.. The slaves shy away from him, and the children in this house started howling so hard the moment he arrived that we could barely calm them down! He's a bad man, the gods don't love him, and neither do I... He'll bring misfortune down on all of us, mark my words!"

Creon only swore under his breath and hurled his cup at the wall.

"Send him away, send him away before it's too late," Nausicaa keened tearfully, passing from shouting to tears with the abruptness of a true general.

This usually worked, but this time Creon had already worked himself into a genuine fury and paid his wife's lamenting no attention.

"Send him away?" he thundered. "Send him away?! Perhaps I should declare war on Oechalia while I'm at it?! Why, if this Eurytus had carried you off, you fool, even then I wouldn't have quarreled with him! Understand?! Only who'd carry you off?! Who needs you?! Echidna of Thebes!"

"Ah, so I'm not needed!" Nausicaa's tears dried instantly. "All you need are your vile guests! So I'm not needed, and the children aren't needed, and Thebes isn't needed! All you want is to feast with your cronies! With this Eurytus, may he fall into Tartarus! It's no wonder his son looks at his father with such a sour face! Now there's the kind of man you ought to welcome! Iphitus has everything going for him: young, courteous, and..."

"Ah, so that's what this is about?! Taken a fancy to Iphitus, have you?! Drawn to the young ones now?! So that's it! Well, isn't that something..."

This unseemly scene, which had erupted out of nowhere, went on for a good while longer. The couple quarreled themselves out completely and withdrew, each to their own chambers. Afterward, for the whole of Eurytus's stay in Thebes, they scarcely spoke to each other, and only once the ill-fated basileus finally departed did they make up, easily and almost at once — and then wondered for a long time afterward what had come over them.

But before that, a good many other things happened — things that would have been better off not happening at all.
