#### 8

He was too late.

One of his sons lay on a stretcher in the shade of an old elm that grew at the edge of the square, and from his pale face with its sharpened features, from the crimson stains seeping through the fresh bandages, from his labored breathing, Iolaus understood that his son was dying.

The other, standing beside him, raised empty eyes to Iolaus.

"Why did you come so late, dad?" he asked quietly, breaking a long-standing agreement.

Iolaus had nothing to answer.

Amphitryon had nothing to answer, and the old name hung from his shoulders like a threadbare cloak.

The dejected men standing nearby either did not hear, or did not stop to think what the words meant.

The dying man slowly unstuck his heavy eyelids. In his clear eyes there was neither fear nor pain; only the faces of his brother and his father, bent over him, merged into a single familiar face, as if he were looking at himself in a clean, unclouded river.

A faint smile touched his parched lips for a moment.

"A hero... stands... alone..." he whispered, barely audible, and closed his eyes again.

"Iphicles, is that you?" Amphitryon asked, just as quietly.

"I'm... Heracles," the dying man breathed out.

"And you?" Amphitryon peered into the face of the other, and for the first time realized that he could not tell his sons apart.

"I'm Heracles," came the answer, like an echo.

Pressing his forehead against the trunk of the elm, his whole body shaking soundlessly, and no longer ashamed of it, Lichas was weeping.

"He... he saved us all," his breaking voice forced its way through the sobs. "He covered us... wounded... he threw me aside --- and he himself... they fell back, and only then... he fell... It should have been me --- me! Instead of him..."

"A hero stands alone," pounded in Amphitryon's brain like a red-hot hammer. A hero. Stands. Alone. And Jason perishes beneath the wreckage of the *Argo*, Daedalus dies of a strange fever, Theseus is "called home" by his father Poseidon, frenzied bacchantes tear the disconsolate Orpheus to pieces... in Iolcus the corpse of Acastus grows cold, Amphiaraus the Soothsayer marches on Thebes as though to his own execution, Cepheus of Arcadia is killed in battle... and now here, in backwater Pheneus, betrayed by men and gods, one of his sons is dying --- all only because:

A hero stands alone.

Who decided so?

He will find out. But --- later. Right now it does not matter, because here, in eastern Eleia, on the square of Pheneus, beneath an elm, his son is dying.

And no one has the power to save him from death. And if anyone does --- they will not be allowed to.

A hero stands alone.

And somewhere far off, at the very edge of consciousness, the words of Heracles sound dully:

"They killed us both, brother --- and that is why we are both still alive. Heracles is dead. Long live Heracles! From now on there are no more laws for me --- neither human nor divine. And they will learn of it soon. Very soon. And we... we will meet again."

[We will meet again,]{.thought} echoed in the mind of the former lawagetas, who knew better than many what death was.

Or was it the dying man who whispered it?

All around stood warriors, their faces grim in their resolve. Those whom this man had saved at the cost of his life.

They no longer cared about plunder.

Fear had left their hearts.

And in their thirst for vengeance they no longer distinguished between men, monsters, and gods.

::: {.small-scene-break}
:::

Toward evening Heracles died.
