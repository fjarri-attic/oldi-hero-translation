#### 10

"...Dad, dad! Praise... oh, praise whoever it is --- you're awake, dad! Oh, dad, you should have seen it! Gods, heroes, centaurs, satyrs, some kind of nymphs too... Apollo himself was there, and Hermes, and Dionysus, and Pan, and... and even the Thunderer, with all of them!"

Automedusa decided to mention the Thunderer just in case, for the sake of gravity.

"Sweetheart... are you all right?" Alcathous mumbled, eyeing his agitated daughter with mistrust and trying to sit up. "Did they... hit you on the head too?"

Sitting up was not going well.

"Oh, but you didn't see a thing! Gods, straight from Olympus --- and off they went, saving me! And there was a hero with them, called Iphicles! He set me free with a club --- everyone just scattered!.."

At the mention of the club, Alcathous couldn't help touching his bald patch and wincing. Then he looked at his beaming daughter --- no, alas, not Andromeda, to warrant all Olympus turning out to save her! --- at the empty altar, at the meadow, ploughed up every which way...

"Fine, so be it," he drawled, "who knows... and what did you say the hero's name was?"

"Iphicles, dad! He's such a hero, such a hero!.."

"Iphicles?" Alcathous decided to leave the gods in peace and latch onto a more earthly subject, especially since after the blow he'd taken, the telestas's head wasn't working at its best. "Iphicles, is it... no, never heard of him!"

"What do you mean, never heard of him?!" Automedusa nearly burst into tears from the insult, both on her own behalf and her rescuer's. "How could you not have heard of him?! Well, Iphicles, the hero... he even has a brother, Alcides!"

"Alcides?" Alcathous marveled. "Alcides of Thebes?! Son of Zeus and Alcmene, the future Slayer of Monsters?!"

"Oh, forget about him!" the girl jerked her sharp little shoulder scornfully. "Slayer of wine, that's what he is, your Alcides! Kept babbling all sorts of nonsense too, the drunkard!.. But Iphicles --- now he's truly..."

And the girl's eyes clouded over dreamily.

"Are you sure you're not confusing them?" Alcathous inquired hopefully, glancing back at the soldiers who were starting to come to. "It was Alcides who saved you, the future hero --- already a hero! --- while his brother was drunk and talking nonsense. Think again!"

An explosion of indignation was his answer.

"I am not confusing anything! Honestly, dad, how can you be so dense! Iphicles saved me, Iphicles, Iphicles, not that horrid Alcides at all! You just lay there and saw nothing, and I..."

"And she saw nothing either," said someone's suspiciously familiar voice nearby.

Father and daughter turned as one --- and discovered the headman, who had crept up unnoticed, his right hand bandaged in a moderately filthy rag.

"You unwashed scum!" Alcathous choked with indignation and began groping first at his belt, then at the grass around him.

"Here it is, my lord," the headman humbly held out the telestas's sword with a bow. "And here are your soldiers' spears."

Two embarrassed village lads shuffled up awkwardly and laid the spears beside the soldiers, then hurried to step back.

Alcathous turned the weapon over in his hands, at a loss. Hacking down the headman under the circumstances felt somehow improper.

"We had a sign, my lord," the headman said meaningfully. "That human sacrifice is displeasing to the gods. So we beg your pardon --- the gods set us straight!"

"Set you straight, all right," Automedusa giggled slyly. "That's why your hand's bandaged... and your lads' faces are all busted up!"

Alcathous did not need to look long to see his daughter was right.

"So who did this to you, then?" he asked, more amicably now, the headman and the lads huddled together behind his back.

"The sign, though!" the headman reported vaguely.

"And who ploughed up the meadow with hooves, from end to end?"

"The sign," the old man sighed heavily. "That's all its own doing, the cursed thing... I mean, the blessed thing!"

Washing down the tough, scorched mutton with lukewarm wine, Alcathous decided not to touch on signs and gods any further, especially since the matter was settled. He had failed to buy any cattle --- the very purpose of his trip from Megara, where a ravenous army of stonemasons was raising new walls --- the plague had seen to that. But he had managed to learn from the talkative headman that the plague hadn't reached the northern pastures, so...

"You had no business tagging along," he tossed at his daughter. "None at all... I get back to Megara, I'll flay the soothsayer Empedocles alive! Lying scoundrel, told me the road would be a lucky one --- and just look at it... Hyah, shag-legged ones, get moving!"

The unlucky road carried the Megarian telestas's chariot ever farther from Megara and ever closer to the northern slopes of Cithaeron, where the restless Amphitryads, exiled from Thebes, had settled.
