#### 15

"Cheiron, I understand now!---and I'm no longer surprised that I failed to understand it before. We were all blind and deaf; not Tartaros alone---anyone you care to point at is to blame for what's happening... and the first among the guilty was my father, when he announced to the Family: 'Alkmene's son will be the Lone Cleaner, a hero equal to the gods and needing none of their help; and at the end of his life he will receive immortality and ascend to Olympos!' Cheiron, Zeus spoke, and the Family believed! Rejoicing, quarreling, arguing, plotting against Amphitryon and trying to kill Alkmene---we believed Zeus's words, and so we believed in the Lone Cleaner!... And after us, people believed too. No god was ever believed in before his own birth; a human hero first had to prove that he was a hero. And even then they didn't always believe... Polydektes mocked Perseus, who had already slain Medusa; Bellerophon crushed the Chimera, routed the warlike Solymoi, withstood the Amazons---yet Iobates from Lykia stubbornly tried to send Bellerophon to Hades. Here, though..."

"Speak, Hermeias."

"Here, though, all of Hellas, from bottom to top, from Tartaros to Olympos, knew: a great hero is being born! Everyone's hope! And we got what we wanted---a hero equal to the gods! And why wouldn't he be equal, when Kronos the Fallen believes in him, Zeus the Olympian believes, Amphitryon believes, I believe... and all of us, while he was still in Alkmene's womb, were already turning the infant into a hero. But a hero, a Cleaner, must be able to do only one thing---kill. Protect his friends and kill his foes. Alkeides is still little; he hasn't learned to kill yet, but he has learned to fight. Amphitryon taught him, Autolykos taught him, Kastor taught him, the overgrown louts in the palaestra taught him; I myself taught him---not by the rules... So now he fights. During a human sacrifice, the Fallen break through from Tartaros into this child's mind, and Alkeides's reason can't withstand it---a madman, he does what he has always done in answer to an insult. He fights, without telling friend from foe! And when he grows up---he, the hero turned madman, will in such moments kill everyone nearby, be they mortal, Titan, god, or monster. A hero equal to the gods, whose soul is turning into a Tartaros straining to break free, while his reason refuses to serve him---no one will stop such a Cleaner, except..."

"Speak, Hermeias."

"Except his brother, Iphikles. Too many need the great hero Alkeides, but no one, save Alkeides himself, needs Iphikles. These aren't two brothers, Cheiron, not two different people---this is a single being! Alkeides and Iphikles---especially during the fits---are feeling and reason, horses and charioteer, strength and... strength. It's no accident that we're forever mixing up the children, Cheiron! Without Iphikles, Alkeides would be like a runaway team of horses, a rabid beast, a volcano flaring up now and then and scorching everything around it. The unbridled fury of Tartaros would come lashing out of him, the thirst for freedom and vengeance, the riot of unleashed elements---Alkeides's reason, like a tongue of flame in a downpour, has been accustomed since childhood to going out in such moments!---and only Iphikles can set the strength of restraint against the strength of destruction, his own hatred of the Fallen, tormentors of his brother, against theirs; only Iphikles can separate the true Alkeides from Alkeides the madman, only together will the brothers balance the scales. Only Iphikles is able to hold Alkeides back without killing him; only the 'I' can bridle itself."

"Speak, Hermeias. I'm listening."

"But balance is of no use to the Fallen or to the Family. They need a hero. So no one must stop to think that there are two brothers; no one must compare Alkeides with Iphikles. The son of Zeus must eclipse the son of Amphitryon---in short, a hero stands alone. Let the Family forget about Iphikles, and let the Fallen never learn of him at all. Let people call Iphikles simply the great hero's brother. The boys are indistinguishable from one another---from now on, whichever of them wins at the games or in single combat, and whichever of them later performs a feat, will be called Alkeides. Iphikles will fade into the background. Yes, it won't be easy for the real Iphikles, but for Alkeides's sake he must agree to it. Reason is accustomed to suppressing feeling... And Iphikles is reason. Today, for the first time, the two of them realized they are One, a single whole; today, Cheiron, on Pelion, there was born a great hero who did not fear to raise a hand against a god, who did not care who stood before him; today there was born a hero equal to the gods."

"Yes, Hermeias. You are the midwife of the Lone Cleaner."

"But children are children, Cheiron, and life goes on! And today's fit won't be Alkeides's last---so there's no need to shout what we know from the rooftops!"

"Yes, Hermeias. Alkeides's madness is the work of jealous Hera; that's what everyone believes, and... so be it! Let the Fallen remain confident that everything is unfolding according to their designs. People say that the future rests on the knees of the gods, but I don't know these gods. Perhaps you're acquainted with them, Hermeias?"

"Nor do I know them, Cheiron. I simply don't wish to see the future on anyone's knees---not even its own."
