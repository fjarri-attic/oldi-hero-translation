#### 15

"Chiron, I understand now! --- and I'm no longer surprised that I failed to understand it before. We were all blind and deaf; not Tartarus --- anyone, point at whoever you like, is guilty of what's happening... and the first of the guilty was my father, when he announced to the Family: 'Alcmene's son will be the Lone Cleaner, a hero equal to the gods and needing none of their help; and at the end of his life he will receive immortality and ascend to Olympus!' Chiron, Zeus spoke, and the Family believed! Rejoicing, quarreling, arguing, plotting against Amphitryon and trying to kill Alcmene --- we believed Zeus's words, and so we believed in the Lone Cleaner!.. And after us, people believed too. No god was ever believed in before his birth; a human hero first had to prove that he was a hero. And even then they didn't always believe... Polydectes mocked Perseus, who had already slain Medusa; Bellerophon crushed the Chimera, routed the warlike Solymi, withstood the Amazons --- yet Iobates from Lycia stubbornly tried to send Bellerophon to Hades. Here, though..."

"Speak, Hermeias."

"Here, though, all of Hellas, from top to bottom, from Tartarus to Olympus, knew: a great hero is being born! Everyone's hope! And we got what we wanted --- a hero equal to the gods! And why wouldn't he be equal, when Kronus the Fallen believes in him, Zeus the Olympian believes, Amphitryon believes, I believe... and all of us, while he was still in Alcmene's womb, were already turning the infant into a hero. But a hero, a Cleaner, needs to know how to do one thing --- kill. Protect one's own and kill the others. Alcides is still small, hasn't learned to kill yet, but he has learned to fight. Amphitryon taught him, Autolycus taught him, Castor taught him, the overgrown louts in the palaestra taught him; I myself taught him --- not by the rules... So now he fights. During the human sacrifice, the Fallen break through from Tartarus into this child's mind, and Alcides's reason can't withstand it --- a madman, he does what he has always done in answer to an insult. He fights, without telling apart his own from strangers! And when he grows up --- he, the hero turned madman, will in that same instant kill everyone nearby, whether you're a mortal, a titan, a god, or a monster. A hero equal to the gods, whose soul is turning into a Tartarus straining to break free, while his reason refuses to serve him --- no one will stop such a Cleaner, except..."

"Speak, Hermeias."

"Except his brother, Iphicles. Too many need the great hero Alcides, but no one, save Alcides himself, needs Iphicles. These aren't two brothers, Chiron, not two different people --- this is a single being! Alcides and Iphicles --- especially during the fits --- are feeling and reason, horses and charioteer, strength and... strength. It's no accident that we're forever mixing up the children, Chiron! Without Iphicles, Alcides would be like a mad team of horses, a rabid beast, a volcano flaring up now and then and scorching everything around it. The unbridled fury of Tartarus would come lashing out of him, the thirst for freedom and vengeance, the riot of unleashed elements --- Alcides's reason, like a tongue of flame in a downpour, has been accustomed since childhood to going out in such moments! --- and only Iphicles can set the strength of restraint against the strength of destruction, his own hatred of the Fallen, tormentors of his brother, against theirs; only Iphicles can separate the true Alcides from Alcides the madman, only together will the brothers balance the scales. Only Iphicles is able to hold Alcides back without killing him; only the 'I' can bridle itself."

"Speak, Hermeias. I'm listening."

"But neither the Fallen nor the Family need balance. They need a hero. So no one must stop to think that there are two brothers; no one must compare Alcides to Iphicles. The son of Zeus must eclipse the son of Amphitryon --- in short, a hero stands alone. Let the Family forget about Iphicles, and let the Fallen never learn of him at all. Let people call Iphicles simply the great hero's brother. The boys are indistinguishable from one another --- from now on, whichever of them wins at the games or in single combat, and whichever of them later performs a feat, will be called Alcides. Iphicles will step into the shadow. Yes, it won't be easy for the real Iphicles, but for Alcides's sake he must agree to it. Reason is accustomed to suppressing feeling... And Iphicles is reason. The two of them, today, realized for the first time that they are One, a single whole; today, Chiron, on Pelion, a great hero was born who did not fear to raise his hand against a god, who did not care who stood before him; today a hero equal to the gods was born."

"Yes, Hermeias. You are the midwife of the Lone Cleaner."

"But children are children, Chiron, and life goes on! And today's fit won't be Alcides's last --- so there's no need to shout what we know to the whole wide world!"

"Yes, Hermeias. Alcides's madness is the doing of a jealous Hera; that's what everyone believes, and... so be it! Let the Fallen remain confident that everything is unfolding according to their designs. People say that the future rests on the knees of the gods, but I don't know these gods. Perhaps you're acquainted with them, Hermeias?"

"Nor do I know them, Chiron. I simply don't wish to see the future on anyone's knees."
