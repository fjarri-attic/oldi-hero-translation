### First epeisodion

#### 1

It happened two hours before dawn, in that accursed hour when light-winged Hypnos the Sleep, own brother to Thanatos the Killer and Momus the Mocker, reigns supreme; when false visions mingle with prophetic ones and swarm the earth in an unruly throng, making seers toss and turn restlessly upon their rumpled beds.

It happened two hours before dawn.

And Hypnos frowned in displeasure, spilling his poppy infusion, when a thunderous guffaw from three brazen throats shook the southern gates of Thebes and woke, at the very least, half of Boeotia.

"Oho-ho-ho! Ha-ha-ha! Hee-hee-hic! When they campaigned with Heracles against the Amazons! Ho-ho-ho!.."

Loudest of all was Thelem the Nobody, a hereditary guard. His grandfather, the late Thelem the Nasal, had spent half his life sitting at the gates of Thebes; his father, Kranai the Grouch, was at that very moment faithfully asleep at the western post; and Thelem himself, grandson of Thelem and son of Kranai, had for a decade and a half now walked the same well-trodden path.

Thelem's simple and moderately happy life was burdened by only one vexing trifle: having lived thirty years without a nickname, in his thirty-first year he became Thelem the Nobody, and at times it seemed to him that by this he disgraced his worthy forebears.

Especially since spiteful tongues claimed that Thelem owed the nickname to his wife --- or rather, to her answer to her friends' question: "What's Thelem like in bed?"

But then, after all, were there many Achaeans who could boast of having only one misfortune in life?!

"Whew, that's killed me! When they campaigned with Heracles against the Amazons... Gods, that boy would make a corpse laugh!"

"Nothing to bray about, you asses!" the scrawny lad crouched in front of Thelem and the two other guards cried out, his voice ringing with indignation. "That's exactly how it was! I earned this scar when Heracles and I campaigned against the Amazons!"

A three-voiced snort was his answer --- the guards had neither the strength nor the breath left for anything more.

...The boy's name was Lichas. He had turned up in Thebes the morning before and had sat by the gates until evening, entertaining the sentries with his chatter. Then, come evening, Lichas had suddenly gone off into the city, where he vanished until midnight, after which he came back disheveled and out of breath; now he was once again whiling away the time with Thelem the Nobody's watch, intending to leave at dawn.

During his absence, Thelem had managed to eat a hearty supper and think his fill about the good old days. To this last murky five-year stretch, the word "good" did not apply in the slightest. The basileis of Thebes had succeeded one another noisily and often bloodily; three of that procession of rulers-for-an-hour had taken the name of the dead Creon for themselves (luring back his luck, perhaps?); now serving as basileus was Lycus the Brawler, intemperate in his revels and quick to violence, who fully lived up to the name of Lycus of Thebes[^45].

[^45]: Lycus --- Greek for "wolf"; i.e. Lycus of Thebes means "the Theban Wolf."

Lycus had begun his reign by banishing two women from the city for no reason at all --- Alcmene and Megara, Heracles's mother and Heracles's wife. By rumor, the women had moved to Tiryns, where their great kinsman was resting between his labors, and for a long time Thebes lived on the hope that Zeus's wrathful son would appear and free them from lawless Lycus.

They waited in vain.

For variety's sake, people had started gossiping that the basileus Lycus was a werewolf, prowling the city by night in search of prey. Watchman Thelem himself did not believe such tales, though he had once, on a dark street, run into a large gray dog that nipped him on the buttock and bolted, and that was the end of the matter. But many Thebans stubbornly held their ground, tracing the basileus's lineage now to Apollo Lyceius, now to the Arcadian king Lycaon.

The latter, Zeus had turned into a wolf even before the flood for his wild escapades, and then, just to be safe, incinerated with a thunderbolt.

Thebes, without quite noticing it, was turning into a backwater --- and so people seasoned the everyday with the coarse-grained salt of legend. This showed most of all in encounters with talkative travelers like Lichas, because any conversation inevitably came around to the latest deeds of god-equal Heracles --- and the Thebans would instantly remind one and all where the hero's homeland lay!

Travelers' rash attempts to bask a little in the glory of this great fellow-townsman of all Thebans were punished with public mockery --- as, for instance, just now, when scrawny Lichas claimed that he had earned the star-shaped scar on his forehead on the campaign against the Amazons.

* * *

Having caught his breath, Thelem the Nobody carefully wiped his streaming eyes, then reached out and gave the boy an approving pat on the shoulder, nearly knocking Lichas to the ground.

"Well done!" the guard wheezed. "A regular jester! Fancy dreaming that up --- Heracles and I..."

The unlucky scar on Lichas's forehead went dark and livid.

"Yes, us!" Lichas shook off Thelem's heavy hand and flashed his eyes angrily. "Heracles and I! I'll have you know, I've been tagging along after him for six years now! And you, uncle, go grope your own woman, got it?!"

"Six years?" Nobody quickly reckoned in his head. "That means, at the time of your momentous meeting, you'd have been about ten... and Heracles was in Thrace just then, wrestling the man-eating mares from Diomedes the Bistonian! Listen, Lichas, could it be that you're one?!"

"One what?" Lichas didn't understand. "Diomedes, or something?"

Just then the boy looked like a starving rat cornered by three well-fed cats in a playful mood.

"You're a man-eating mare!" the second guard brayed good-naturedly, slapping his ample belly. "Ah, you shameless windbag... go on, keep lying! Where else did you and Heracles go? For the apples of the Hesperides?"

"Nah, he didn't take me along for the apples," Lichas admitted ruefully, knitting his sparse pale eyebrows. "You won't make it that far, he says. Went himself, with his brother. I told him, 'What, you think I'm a baby?' --- and he told me..."

"And he clubbed you on the forehead!" Thelem the Nobody finished for him. "That's what left the scar! All right, hero, here --- you've earned it..."

Nobody was no man of delicate feeling, but Lichas was doing a fine job of helping to kill time, and so deserved a reward. So Thelem rummaged in the provisions basket, pulled out a plump yellow apple, and tossed it to the boy.

"Eat up, get your strength back! Not an apple of the Hesperides, of course --- but then again, you didn't have to go anywhere for it either... Chew, Lichas, and give us more about the Amazons! Is it true their queen handed her belt over to Heracles herself, only for Hera to stir up trouble in disguise?"

"It wasn't Hera," Lichas sighed sadly, making short work of the apple.

"Then who was it?"

"It was me."

Thelem's fellow guards were about to burst into a hearty guffaw, but Nobody surreptitiously showed them his fist --- huge, hairy, and very persuasive. As if to say: the little vagrant would take offense and clam up, and there were still three hours of sitting here ahead of them!

"So we came up to their hen-capital at Themiscyra," Lichas went on meanwhile, "with a whole army. Ten thou, if not more. Only we didn't storm it --- Heracles decided to send an embassy instead. His nephew Iolaus the driver, then Theseus the Athenian... well, and me. Theseus, see, for looks, Iolaus for brains, and me for harmlessness. As the herald. Go on ahead, Lichas, he says, and wave a green branch. Just keep your mouth shut, for the gods' sake!"

"And you?" the fat guard couldn't hold back, winking at Thelem.

"And I couldn't. Iolaus and Theseus started off sweet-talking Queen Hippolyta into handing over the belt peaceably, then they went off somewhere with her and her advisor Antiope, and left me behind. I'm standing there alone like a fool, nothing but spear-women all around... Keeping quiet. Keeping quiet a long time, getting tired --- and then our lot comes back. Theseus is soaked, red in the face, clothes all rumpled; Iolaus looks normal, but the queen and Antiope are glowing brighter than the dawn! We'll give up the belt, they say. How could we not give the royal belt to the great Heracles?! I look --- the queen's got no belt on, she's holding it in her hands, handing it to Iolaus. And her advisor's got no belt either, though nobody sent us for hers. Well, that's when I spoke up..."

"And what did you say?" Thelem stuck his hand into the basket, meaning to reward Lichas with another apple, or maybe a flatbread with a slice of ham.

"What, what... I said the royal belt wasn't for Heracles at all, but for Eurystheus's daughter, that fat donkey Admete! The stupid girl got it into her head, but her belly could only be wrapped round with a Sidonian hawser! So let the Amazons lengthen the belt by a couple of cubits, and we'd wait. And I showed them how much to lengthen it by. Oh, and then it started... Theseus got wounded in the thigh almost at once, Iolaus threw him over his shoulder and made for the chariots! You know how wild Iolaus can get?! I set off after them, waving my branch, and I see --- that cursed belt lying there, everyone trampling all over it... So I stuffed it under my shirt! And that's when somebody hit me on the forehead..."

Lichas didn't manage to finish.

Carried away by the story, the guards had lost themselves and missed the chariot, which had already managed to drive up almost to the very gates.

Thelem the Nobody, grunting, sprang up and rushed to check the bolts. Having satisfied himself they were secure, he cursed talkative Lichas under his breath and pressed his eye to the crack between the gate leaves, meaning to get a look at the newcomer.

He got a look.

Nothing special.

A big, striking man of middle years, standing there leaning on the chariot rail, gazing at the city walls.

Only his eyes glinted like mica.

Watering, are they?

"Hey, Iolaus!" came a shout from the wall. "How did you guess I was in Thebes?!"

Thelem the Nobody ran back from the gate, tipped his head up, and discovered Lichas on the wall.

"Did he fly up there, or what?" flashed through the guard's mind.

"I didn't," the driver's low, slightly hoarse voice rumbled from outside. "Where did I tell you to stay put, you rascal? Well?!"

"Oh, come on," Lichas answered in a lower tone. "What have I not seen in Tiryns? Deadly dull... I'd rather come with you. You think I don't know anything, but I know everything --- that you and Alcides and Iphicles are off to Oechalia, and about the contest at Eurytus the archer's, and about Iole the bride... I was hiding under the table and you never even noticed! Alcides even kicked me with his foot, and I yelped, and he decided it was a dog! Just wait, Iolie, I'm coming down to you right now..."

Dumbfounded, Thelem watched as the boy fussed for a while at the edge of the wall --- the bronze of a triple hook, like long claws, flashed distinctly --- then Lichas gave a jaunty whistle and vanished over the parapet.

"He's got a rope," the fat guard muttered defensively, glancing guiltily at Thelem. "I hadn't even had time to get up, and he'd already thrown the hook. And climbs like a cockroach too, the rascal..."

The third sentry didn't say anything at all.

"Listen, Thelem," the fat man just couldn't settle down, "which Iolaus is that, anyway? Out there, past the wall... The very one? Heracles's driver? So what does that mean, Thelem?!"

"Means what it means," Nobody grumbled angrily, and suddenly rushed for the bolts.

He barely made it in time --- by the time the gates creaked open, the chariot was already about to move off.

Beside the stocky Iolaus --- who on closer inspection turned out to be much younger, about twenty --- an excited Lichas was hopping about, forever trying to grab hold of the reins. A coil of rope with a hook was slung across the boy's shoulder.

"I'm..." Thelem mumbled, running up to the chariot and looking up into the driver's stern face. "I wanted... they call me Thelem..."

"Nasal?" Iolaus asked, his voice trembling strangely.

"No, Nasal was my grandfather. And I'm... I'm Nobody."

Iolaus looked intently into the guard's eyes for a while.

"Nobody?" the driver repeated, calm again now. "Nobody is bad. That's very bad, understand?! A man must not be nobody --- provided, of course, he is a man..."

"Understood," Thelem nodded, shivering from an inexplicable chill.

"And if you understood, then that's already better." Iolaus took a heavy bracelet off his left wrist. "Here, take it, friend! Give it as an offering to your grandfather --- let him hiccup down there in Hades!"

...The chariot had long since vanished around the bend, but Thelem kept staring after it, as though it were real life itself rushing away, having turned aside into Thebes for only a single moment.

"So what does that mean?" the fat guard said, coming up to Nobody and repeating himself for the hundredth time. "Does that mean that windbag Lichas really did... campaign against the Amazons?"

"You know what," Thelem muttered without turning around, "break my nose for me, would you?"

"What for?" the fat man asked, backing away in fright.

"No reason. I'll be Nasal... like grandfather."
