#### 14

"Well then, peace?" Hermeias inquired, when everyone had tired themselves out with laughing and now lay sprawled, exhausted, on the grass by the mouth of the cave.

"Peace," Alcides said, waving a hand limply.

"Peace," Iphicles agreed.

"Peace is the right thing," Chiron seconded. "And now that everyone's had their laugh and made up --- I have a couple of questions for you, heroes."

"Well?" the twins turned to the centaur at the same moment, forgetting their weariness at once.

"Not 'well,' but 'what questions,' is what you meant to say," Chiron remarked, as if in passing, which thoroughly embarrassed the boys. "The first question is for you, Alcides. Forgive me for returning to something unpleasant, but it must be done. You're nearly grown now; you'll understand."

"I understand," Alcides nodded gravely, though it was plain from his face that he understood nothing at all. "If it must be done, then it must."

"Well then, Alcides... do you remember anything at all of what you do during the fits?"

Alcides honestly tried to remember something; stubborn furrows appeared on the boy's forehead, but in the end he shook his head.

"I can't recall it, Uncle Chiron. I remember talking with Dum--- with Hermes about Myrtilus, and then, all at once, we're here, I'm lying down, and Iphicles is standing there with a rock, facing Hermes. That's all."

"Well, what did you feel during that time? If you don't remember --- fine, but even something... perhaps just before you lost consciousness..."

Alcides flinched and looked around like a hunted thing, as if afraid that Pelion might vanish and he'd be left alone with the madness again.

"I don't know," he drew out slowly. "It's like I'm falling somewhere, deep, deep down, and all around it's dark, nothing to see, and everything's humming... someone's holding me, grabbing at my arms, all slimy himself and smelling of mold, and I try to tear free, try again... and I tear free. Then they tell me I was fighting again, or breaking something, and I don't remember a single bit of it. Listen, Dum--- Hermes" ("Oh, call me whatever you like!" Hermeias waved him off) "--- since you're a god, explain what's wrong with me? Or better --- cure me... please. What is it to you?"

There was so much restrained pain in that plea that the Trickster couldn't bear it and turned away, cursing Tartarus under his breath in words that only a god could utter, and only in the heat of the moment.

"Leave him be," Iphicles said, laying a hand on his brother's knee and looking at the centaur with hostility. "Can't you see he doesn't remember anything... ask me instead. They --- the ones who are slimy and smell of mold --- they ask me things too. Only I don't tell them who I am, and then they call for Alcides and want him to be with them. To be slimy too... and to help them get out of somewhere. Then Alcides screams and starts throwing himself at everyone, and afterward he doesn't remember any of it."

Iphicles glanced at Chiron with dry, feverish eyes.

"And I hold onto Alcides," the boy whispered, almost soundlessly. "I hold him, and he tries to tear free... and the ones calling him --- they try too... but I hold on. Because otherwise Alcides will be with them, and I want him to be with me."

"And who are these 'they'?" Alcides asked.

"They..." Hermeias fell silent a moment, thinking it over. "They're very old, boys, very, very old, and that's why... they're hard to understand. They really were locked away, but that's not the point --- the point is that they're not like you. They're not even like us."

"Not anymore, at least," Chiron confirmed, ambiguously. "So, Iphicles, hold your brother tighter when they call him. Otherwise trouble might come of it."

"I know," Iphicles answered dully.

"And one last question," Chiron rolled his shoulders, like a very tired man, or a very tired centaur, who knows he won't get to rest anytime soon. "For you, Iphicles. What did you feel when you were throwing rocks at Hermeias? And afterward, when your brother came to?"

"I was very angry at Dummy then," Iphicles scowled. "Some friend, that!.. I didn't know he was a god. And so what if I had! I could've killed anyone right then --- god or not god... You didn't ask who we were, Dummy, when you were about to... me... Alcides... us... Well, I already said it! And then I realized Alcides had come to, and I was so glad that I just picked up that rock without even trying! Well, then Chiron stepped in... and that was that."

Hermeias and Chiron exchanged glances.

At any rate, it seemed to the twins that it was only a brief, almost unnoticeable exchange of glances --- because Hermeias gave a faint snap of his fingers, invisible snakes hissed softly on the caduceus rod, and much, very much, remained a mystery to the twins, passing right by their awareness...
