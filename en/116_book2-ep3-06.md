#### 6

...It was this limping man --- in a hacked-up leather cuirass with sparse studs, leaning as he walked on a spear with a bent tip --- that Iolaus saw as he drove up to the familiar bend of the Alpheus; somewhere farther downstream there should still have been the remains of the dam the twins had built some fourteen years ago[^81].

[^81]: To clean the Augean stables, Heracles dammed the rivers Alpheus and Peneus, and the flood of water washed away all the dung (rumor has it, along with the stables).

The soldier trudged along dejectedly, looking at nothing, and the former lawagetas, tormented by ill forebodings, hurried to halt the chariot.

"Where are you coming from, hero?" Iolaus asked with feigned languor, barely holding back his agitation.

The soldier stopped and stared at Iolaus dully for a while; then the point of the question finally reached him, and he unstuck his cracked lips.

"From near Pisa."

"You wouldn't happen to be from the army of the great Heracles?" Iolaus climbed down from the chariot and held out to the soldier a wineskin of heavily watered wine.

The soldier drank long and greedily, then handed back the considerably lighter wineskin and, wiping his mouth with the back of his hand, answered grimly:

"I would."

"Surely the glorious Heracles hasn't been defeated, and his men aren't running for their lives?" Iolaus raised his eyebrows in feigned innocence.

The soldier gave a noncommittal grunt and lowered himself onto the roadside.

Iolaus hastened to sit down beside him.

"I didn't sign on with Heracles for this!" the soldier muttered after a long silence, and glanced sideways at the wineskin, which Iolaus immediately put into his hands.

The soldier took several more heroic gulps --- and then, at last, it all burst out of him.

"Never signed up for this!" the soldier bellowed, spraying spit. "Never signed up for this! This isn't a war, it's a curse of the gods! First those Molionids, Augeas's dear relatives, may Erebus take them! They fight like demons, you can't tell if there's two of them or two hundred... chewed through people something awful! Bronze doesn't bite! Arrows snap, spears glance off, swords go dull... we never signed up for this!"

"Saw it yourself, or what?" Iolaus asked softly.

"Saw it. From far off... that's why I'm still alive!"

"So what --- are they really conjoined, or are people lying?"

"Lying," the soldier snapped. "They're separate, but..."

He fell to thinking, searching for words.

"...but they fight as one! Do they feel each other through their backs, or what?! To look at, they're people like any other, huge, granted --- but they're not people, all the same! They move differently, and those mugs are terrifying, and you can't tell --- is that armor on them, or fish scales... saw it myself. From far off. And the ones who were close can't tell anybody anything anymore!"

The soldier gave a jerk and started glancing around.

"Never signed up for this," he wheezed under his breath, "no, never signed up... not a word about those freaks, not a word about gods... who could've known the Olympians themselves would stand with Neleus and Augeas against Heracles?!.. No, never signed up..."

"It can't be!" Iolaus slapped himself on the thighs, feigning the utmost astonishment. "Gods?!"

And everything inside him screamed: "It can!"

This was exactly what Iolaus had been expecting deep down, expecting and dreading --- afraid to admit it even to himself.

The gods were on the side of Salmoneus's brethren.

Against Heracles.

The Family and the repentant Possessed --- against the Cleaner who had seen their common disgrace!

"Can't be? Can't be?! And I'm telling you --- gods! I didn't see them myself, I won't lie ('A surprisingly honest soldier, this one,' Iolaus noted in passing) --- but plenty of people did. Ares Enyalius, and Hera, and even Hades himself! Got it?!"

[Now that part is a lie. I don't believe it. The Family will never drag the Lord out of his Erebus to fight mortals...]{.thought}

Iolaus harbored no warm feelings toward Hades, but he could not picture Senior, either, setting off in person, weapon in hand, to stock his realm with shades.

"We never signed up to fight gods," the slumped soldier droned on meanwhile. "Even if Heracles did wound some of them --- no, we never signed up! That's not what we came for..."

"Heracles wounded one of the Olympians?!" Iolaus sprang up, this time in real earnest.

"He did!" the soldier waved him off. "Well, he is Zeus's son, after all --- man, god, monster, it's all the same to him!.. But we're simple folk, we never signed up to fight gods... we offer gods sacrifices, not a knife in the belly!.."

Iolaus flew up onto the chariot like a whirlwind, flung the wineskin into the face of the blinking, bewildered soldier, and lashed the horses with his whip.

He had to make it in time.
