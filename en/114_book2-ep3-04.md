#### 4

Iolaus was in a hurry.

He had to make it in time.

Behind him lay the farewell to Laodamia in dusty Phylace --- they had said their goodbyes right in the street, outside her uncle's house, in the patchy shade of scrawny olive trees, paying no heed to the snide or astonished stares, unable to break their embrace...

But Iolaus was in a hurry.

Phylace had fallen behind, and the wheels clattered along the mountain roads of rocky Phocis --- toward the Isthmos, the neck of land that joined the mainland to the Peloponnese. Somewhere off to the right, hidden by the mountains, sacred Delphi must have flashed past, and here Iolaus was suddenly in luck: he sensed the presence of a Dromos. Without stopping to think, he sent the wildly neighing horses straight into the tangle of ghostly threads that had appeared before him, as he had done then, on Kos --- and burst into the Dromos before he had even quite realized it. A moment later Iolaus was already on the other side, recognizing almost at once the road onto which the Dromos, indignant at such impertinence, had spat him out.

A little farther on, around the bend, lay Megara, and right behind it --- the Isthmos.

He had to make it in time!

But, unwilling to run the snorting horses to death, Iolaus spent the night in Megara, pulling into an inn he knew from past visits and tossing the reins to the servant who came running out to meet him.

...The tavern was noisy, smelling of cheap wine, male sweat, and scorched meat. Iolaus asked for a small jug of white, a barley flatbread, and a leg of mutton, inquired about a free room, impolitely declined the girl offered along with it --- and sat down in a corner.

He learned what interested him quickly: all of Hellas was brimming with rumors, and the tavern's regulars were not the silent sort.

Heracles's army was successfully, though unhurriedly, advancing through Eleia. Augeas's detachments had moved out to meet the invaders, and it was then, as it turned out, that Neleus of Pylos and the Spartan Hippocoon had come to the Eleian basileus's aid with their men. The tavern immediately erupted in an argument over whether this was true or not, but Iolaus knew it was true. Salmoneus's brethren were hurrying to support Augeas, not doubting that after Eleia their own turn would come.

Still, this news did not trouble Iolaus overmuch. The Amphitryads had set out on the campaign with a whole army of seasoned soldiers, at its core the warlike Arcadians under the command of the former Argonaut Cepheus and his sons. And along the way they were joined by out-of-work mercenaries, youths dreaming of glorious deeds, and simply fortune-seekers of every stripe. Besides, the glory of the invincible Heracles, beloved of the gods, son of Zeus, flew ahead of the army --- Heracles, at whose back the wings of Tyche the Luck always beat.

Only that accursed finger in the sky, that mockingly menacing finger, gave him no peace.

"The Finger of Fate," flashed absurdly through Iolaus's mind.
