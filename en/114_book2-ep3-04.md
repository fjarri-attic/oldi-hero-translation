#### 4

Iolaos was in a hurry.

He had to make it in time.

Behind him lay the farewell to Laodameia in dusty Phylake---they had said their goodbyes right in the street, outside her uncle's house, in the patchy shade of scrawny olive trees, paying no heed to the snide or astonished stares, unable to break their embrace...

But Iolaos was in a hurry.

Phylake had fallen behind, and the wheels clattered along the mountain roads of rocky Phokis---toward the Isthmos, the neck of land that joined the mainland to the Peloponnese. Somewhere off to the right, hidden by the mountains, sacred Delphi must have flashed past, and then Iolaos had an unexpected stroke of luck: he sensed the presence of a Dromos. Without stopping to think, he sent the wildly neighing horses straight into the tangle of ghostly threads that had appeared before him, as he had once done on Kos---and burst into the Dromos before he had even quite realized it. A moment later Iolaos was already on the other side, recognizing almost at once the road onto which the Dromos, indignant at such impertinence, had spat him out.

A little farther on, around the bend, Megara should lie, and just beyond it---the Isthmos.

He had to make it in time!

But, unwilling to run the snorting horses to death, Iolaos spent the night in Megara, driving into the yard of an inn he knew from past visits and tossing the reins to the servant who came running out to meet him.

...The tavern was noisy and smelled of cheap wine, male sweat, and scorched meat. Iolaos asked for a small jug of white, a barley flatbread, and a leg of mutton, inquired about a free room, rudely declined the girl offered along with it---and sat down in a corner.

He quickly learned what he wanted to know: all of Hellas was brimming with rumors, and the tavern's regulars were not the silent sort.

Herakles's army was advancing through Elis successfully, if unhurriedly. Augeias's detachments had moved out to meet the invaders, and it was then, as it turned out, that Neleus of Pylos and the Spartan Hippokoon had come to the Eleian basileus's aid with their men. The tavern immediately erupted in an argument over whether this was true or not, but Iolaos knew it was true. Salmoneus's brethren were hurrying to support Augeias, in no doubt that their own turn would come after Elis.

Still, this news did not unduly trouble Iolaos. The Amphitryads had set out on the campaign with a whole army of seasoned soldiers, its core made up of the warlike Arkadians under the command of the former Argonaut Kepheus and his sons. And along the way they were joined by out-of-work mercenaries, youths dreaming of glorious deeds, and plain fortune-seekers of every stripe. Besides, the glory of the invincible Herakles, beloved of the gods, son of Zeus, flew ahead of the army---Herakles, at whose back the wings of Tyche the Luck are always beating.

Only that accursed finger in the sky, that mockingly wagging finger, gave him no peace.

[The Finger of Fate,]{.thoughts} flashed absurdly through Iolaos's mind.
