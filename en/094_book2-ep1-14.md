#### 14

"Rejoice, disciple! I, Apollo Thyraios[^54], have come to open for you the last door of mortals---the gates of Hades!"

[^54]: "Of the Door"; one of the most ancient epithets of Apollo.

Eurytos of Oichalia spun around sharply.

Behind him, beyond the terrace's white balustrade, an abyss gaped; before him stood the one who had secretly watched the twins in the harbor of Oropos, who had spoken to the fragments of a broken bow in the outer courtyard as Herakles was leaving inhospitable Euboia---before Eurytos stood the wolf and the dolphin, the laurel and the palm, the arrow and the kithara, Delphi and Didyma[^55], the one who had promised the Family Council to watch over the freed Herakles for half a year.

[^55]: The sacred animals, plants, attributes, and cult centers of Apollo.

And in Apollo's merciless eyes a verdict could clearly be read.

"My brother Hermeias told me you like to offer human sacrifices to Herakles," the god added dryly, and a gleaming arrow was nocked to the bowstring. "Well then... Hear me, Kronids upon Olympos and the Fallen in Tartaros: I, Phoibos-Apollo, an Olympian, offer up basileus Eurytos, my disciple, a Possessed one, as a sacrifice to Zeus's son Herakles! So let it be!"

A fiery ray tore free from the bowstring.

...No, Alkeides did not see any of this. It was only that the wind suddenly laughed in his face, dusting his eyes with a powder that smelled of the moldy damp of an earthen cellar; only that the madness of a Herakles nearing forty was different from the past madness of Alkeides of Thebes; the angry god's gaze, burning with a bright flame, rose for an instant out of nothing, blotting out the crenellated towers of Tiryns, and the barely distinguishable words "I, Phoibos-Apollo, an Olympian..." fused into a golden arrow hurtling at Alkeides---understanding nothing, he staggered back, trying to catch the flash of death with his hands, blindly collided with something soft, heard a muffled cry, and plunged into an abyss that hummed with a bronze drone...

::: small-scene-break
:::

The shade of Eurytos of Oichalia stood by the railing, looking down into the abyss---to where, on the rocks below, the basileus's mangled body lay pitifully crumpled.

"So this is how it happens..." the shade said quietly, and turned to face the god a second time.

"Off to Hades with you!" Apollo smirked contemptuously. "Shall I hand you the naulon[^56] for Charon?"

[^56]: The fee paid to Charon for passage across the Acheron.

"To Hades? You are a fool, god, or else you were too hasty---or perhaps both at once. Can it be that your brother Hermeias never told you that sacrifices to Herakles do not go to Hades? Not willingly, in any case. Ah, now I see---he didn't tell you... he forgot. Otherwise, knowing you were dealing with one of the Possessed, you would have thought three times before offering him up in sacrifice to Herakles!"

The god took a step toward the shade.

"You will go where I command! Or can you find some place where the hand of Apollo cannot reach you?!"

"No, you truly are a fool." The shade passed a ghostly hand through the air, opening a Dromos; its glassy threads had a black sheen. "Very well, then---follow me, fearsome and hasty brother of Hermeias Psychopompos!.."

Apollo lunged for the Dromos where the shade of the Possessed had just vanished---and recoiled.

On the other side lay Phlegra.

The Burning Grounds.

The cradle of the Gigantes.

::: small-scene-break
:::

...No, Alkeides did not see this. Trembling all over, he stood at the edge of the wall, slowly coming back to himself---any moment now another drop will fall in the clepsydra, another grain of sand in the hourglass, the shadows will lengthen by a hair's breadth, and Alkeides will lower his eyes.

It is drawing inexorably closer, the moment when Herakles will see Iphitos the archer, dashed to his death; will see the mangled corpse at the foot of the Tirynthian wall.

And he will remember the ringing voice, born of madness:

"I, Phoibos-Apollo, an Olympian..."

Tomorrow, Iolaos and Iphikles, on their return to Tiryns, will learn that Herakles, having killed his former teacher during a fit, left for Delphi.
