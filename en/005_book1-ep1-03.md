#### 3

"I don't believe my eyes! By the hooves of Silenus! Is it truly you, my friend, the great and glorious lawagetas[^9] Amphitryon?! Wine! Wine for me and my friend, red Pramneios, and a jug of cold water! Heroes drink Colchian-style, undiluted, and pour the water over their own heads after the carousing!.. Climb up here, Amphitryon --- up here, on this dais, you feel closer to heaven, and I remember your fondness for all things elevated... No, that's not your fondness, that's Cephalus of Thorikos's, but I remember your tastes too, all the same, or I'm no Elphistic the Troezenian! I remember everything, everything..."

[^9]: Leader of the army, commander.

"You do, you do, but there's no need to shout it to the whole tavern," Amphitryon thought with mild irritation, climbing onto the dais and settling onto a stool beside his old comrade from the Taphian campaign, Elphistic the Troezenian --- a burly, red-bearded hulk of a man in a chiton of expensive cloth, whose original color could no longer be determined, so thoroughly was the garment soaked in wine and grease.

Beside the Troezenian a half-emptied jug stood on the little table, and a gnawed ox haunch lay there, looking as though it had passed through the jaws of a lion.

"Wine!" Elphistic bellowed, and slammed his huge fist down on the edge of the table. The jug jumped, toppled onto its side, and what was left of its contents spilled onto the floor. Elphistic looked at the puddle that had formed, sighed, and added, more quietly now:

"Well, all the more reason, then --- wine..."

Amphitryon knew perfectly well that taking offense at Elphistic, or taking him seriously for that matter, was foolish and pointless. The Troezenian had always been like this, sober (which happened rarely) or in his cups alike --- loud, unguarded, and simple as a table corner.

Then again, Elphistic knew how to fight. And did not know how to betray. Nothing more could be asked of an ally. In the course of the Taphian campaign Amphitryon and Elphistic had grown close, and Elphistic's unbridled fury in battle was well complemented by Amphitryon's cold calculation --- so much so that the latter was even somewhat glad of this chance meeting, which let him forget himself and sink into mindless soldierly revelry.

* * *

Just over two months since his return had been torment for Amphitryon. He threw himself headlong into business, personally overseeing the division of the spoils and the allotment of shares to the families of the fallen; he escorted the herdsmen and their cattle almost all the way to Lake Copais --- the annual tribute to the warlike city of Orchomenus, a tribute Amphitryon himself considered humiliating, though he did not dare say so outright to Creon of Thebes. Then he commissioned a new shield and a dozen spearheads from the armorers; he came home late, was tender with his wife, and every moment feared he would lose control and strike her.

With a backhand swing, the way brothel-keepers in ports strike their cheap pornai whores, when they hold back part of the take.

He understood that Alcmene was not to blame for any of it.

It was simply that whenever he looked at her, whenever he touched her body, listened to her moans and her broken breathing --- he could not shake the thought that just like this, sincerely and with total abandon, she had given herself to that other one, the one of the night, the stranger, unable to tell her husband from a god.

And in the morning he would rise and leave the house.

* * *

The pot-bellied host was already hurrying toward them, a jug in one hand --- the big-bellied jug and its well-fed owner made quite a matching pair --- and a silvered cup in the other. An identical cup already stood beside Elphistic. Such fine tableware was reserved for honored guests only, that is, for those unlikely to slip a cup under their cloak and slink off with it. A light breeze, cool yet laced with the smell of roasting carcasses, played over their flushed faces; the meat, to Amphitryon's surprise, tasted good, and the wine was nowhere near as vile as he had expected at first; he settled himself more comfortably, and the noise of the tavern and the cares of everyday life strangely wove together, blurred, and receded somewhere below --- here, on the dais, in his artificial solitude, Amphitryon truly did feel closer to heaven, and thought, besides, that red-headed Elphistic really did have a knack for choosing the best spot, whether in battle or in a tavern...

Only then did he notice that the Troezenian had been telling him something for quite some time already, and he made himself focus on his companion.

"And here's what I'll tell you, my friend," Elphistic lowered his voice to what he took for a whisper, though the whisper came out exceptionally loud. "The gods clearly favor us! Got it?"

"G-got it," Amphitryon, already a little drunk, nodded his heavy head. "The gods favor us! But... but what made you think that? Did you go to a seer, or what?"

"Just think back on our campaign! All the battles --- top notch! Praise Ares, of course, but you and I weren't slacking off either! Then --- wells turned up exactly when we needed them, and no one had thrown rotten meat down them; deserters came flocking to us; that one, what's-her-name... Comaetho, the daughter of basileus Pterelaos, fell head over heels for you, and the whole Taphos to us on a silver plate... a damned pirate's nest, weren't we right to do it!.."

"And afterwards I killed her," Amphitryon replied darkly, trying in vain to recall the face of the Taphian princess Comaetho. He remembered her scream, when the sword's blade sank into the soft woman's belly without the usual resistance; he remembered the blood on the white peplos too --- but for some reason her face would not come back to him.

At all.

"And good for you!" Elphistic rejoiced, waving the ox bone about with enthusiasm. "I even said as much to Panopeus: listen, Panopeus, old pal, good for our Amphitryon! Understands things right, I told him --- Taphos is ours now regardless, it isn't going anywhere, but that patricide, that Taphian snake, you weren't going to bring her home, were you?! The gods guided your hand, my friend, truly the gods did..."

"And afterwards I killed her," Amphitryon repeated, not listening to Elphistic, pouring himself more from the jug. "Her belly was... soft. And I broke the sword afterward. Shouldn't have, probably..."

"Of course you shouldn't have," Elphistic tried to gnaw at the bone, which had not a scrap of meat left on it, and did manage a bite, but then changed his mind and hurled it at the host, who was busy down by the hearth.

"Shouldn't have! We'll need swords again yet... Any day now we'll march off on campaign once more. I had a sign not long ago! From up there!"

And he jabbed a greasy finger at the sky.

"A sign?" Amphitryon perked up. "What sign?"

Elphistic leaned toward his friend's ear with visible effort and whispered tragically:

"My Oenonia --- well, you know her! --- was visited by Ares the god of war himself!"

"No kidding?!" Amphitryon feigned surprise, since he did in fact know buxom Oenonia, the Troezenian's wife --- and knew her a good deal better than Elphistic himself suspected. And in his memory rose: the night, Alcmene drowsy and indifferently detached, an unpleasant cold in his chest, and her words: "Praise heaven... it worked at last... the captive women wore you out, didn't they?" And the sand, the chill sand of the courtyard scraping painfully at his forehead, and the howl, a howl rising up from within, from depths, from such abysses of the soul as Amphitryon had never suspected he possessed.

Who, then, had been with her that night?!

"I'm telling you straight!" Elphistic would not let up. "Oenonia's carrying his child now! Gone fat, belly ahead of her nose... any day now she'll start oinking."

"What month is she in?" Amphitryon asked mechanically, staring into his cup.

"Third. But her belly's like it's the sixth."

"Mine's in her third month too..."

"What, also Ares?"

"I don't know. Me, probably... or Ares."

"But with me --- no doubt about it! Him! Ares Enyalius[^10]! Oenonia says he never even took off his helmet, the whole time he was at it. An honor, then. Only my fool of an Oenonia --- I can't do a thing with her now! Every single night she goes on: 'Now Ares --- there's a man for you! That was divine! Truly a god... a spearman! And you're a mangy billy goat...' Well, what about me? Fine, I'm no spearman --- though the women haven't complained so far! Listen, Amphitryon, let's go on campaign! Or I'll die trying to satisfy her..."

[^10]: "The Warlike" --- a common epithet of Ares, the god of war.

"Oh, come off it!" Amphitryon ground out through his teeth, surprising even himself. "Divine, divine... We all know how it is --- divine! Grinding their teeth and pacing around the thalamos --- and that's the whole of their lovemaking!"

"What?" Elphistic's eyes bulged.

"That! You know what! The man thing! You and I may die, but at least we'll die as men, and they'll ferry us across the Acheron as men --- while they'll just go on the same as ever... I myself have called myself a god plenty of times, running off to other men's wives! Now Ares, now Hermes, once I even made up some Pantelis-of-the-river[^pantelis] on the spot..."

[^pantelis]: A reference to a joke poem "The morning after they found three corpses there" by M. Bukanov. [TN]

"No, wait now!" Elphistic grabbed Amphitryon by the shoulder. "Hold on! Don't you go slandering the gods to me! Some Pantelis-of-the-river, indeed... My Oenonia won't even lie with me anymore, not since Ares! I've tried putting on the helmet, taking up the shield --- nothing doing! And you talk to me about the man thing..."

"I say it because I know!" Amphitryon, quite forgetting himself, raised his voice. "I know! You can go on loving your Oenonia with a spear for all I care, after Ares --- but I know! One of them paid a visit to my Alcmene too! One of the Olympians!"

"Well, and?!" Elphistic leaned forward.

"'Well, and?!'" Amphitryon mimicked him. "And nothing! He even went and pretended to be me --- no good. Didn't work! No sooner had he lain down on the kline than he left with nothing to show for it. Some spearman! His spear wouldn't even stand on its own, kept drooping. And then, right on cue, I came home --- and did the honors for him, for the Olympian! Fool that I was, I even wondered why Alcmene seemed so... worn out, was it? And the bedding was all rumpled..."

"But she's expecting, isn't she!" Elphistic frowned, not understanding. "You said so yourself!"

"I did say so! Expecting --- but my child! Mine! Mine alone! Divine! Ha!"

"Well, who was it, then?" Elphistic had clearly believed the story, and now what interested him above all was which deity had disgraced himself so shamefully.

"I don't know," Amphitryon said, and pressed his cup to his lips, drinking greedily, splashing wine.

"Well, who do you suspect?"

Amphitryon did not manage to answer. Choking on his wine, he broke into a fit of coughing, and when he heard a crack, he did not at once realize that the leg of Elphistic's stool had snapped. The Troezenian cried out, toppling backward, flailing his arms as though about to take flight, but he could not keep his balance, and his considerable bulk went crashing down from the dais.

There was nothing Amphitryon could do. He sat there, coughing, staring dumbly as Elphistic fell backward, in the worst possible way it could have happened, and struck the back of his head against a bronze spit-hook jutting from the wall.

The cough vanished as if by magic. The blood drained from Amphitryon's face all at once, and the crimson of his cheeks gave way instantly to a deathly pallor, while large beads of sweat broke out on his forehead.

"Elphistic! Are you... are you alive?"

On legs that would not bend, Amphitryon climbed down from the dais where, just moments before, he had felt closer to heaven, and bent over the body of his friend, sprawled on the floor.

"Elphistic..."

The Troezenian was dead. The hook's sharpened end had punched through the back of his skull and driven halfway into his brain, so that Elphistic had died at once --- probably without even having time to be afraid.

Then again, he had never known how --- to be afraid.

And now he never would learn.

People were already gathering around; a hunchbacked slave boy from among the kitchen help fussed and sobbed; someone ran off for a physician, though there was no need for one at all; nearby the host wailed, mourning not so much the absurdly dead guest as the possible loss of profits to his establishment --- for how many men, after all, would come to drink at a place with a bad name?..

"How could this happen?" Amphitryon muttered in confusion, backing away. "How could this happen? So many battles --- without a scratch! And then, here..."

"You'd do well to blab less, my dear fellow," an unfamiliar, unpleasant voice whispered in Amphitryon's ear. "Blab less --- live longer. And if you let your tongue run loose, something might just give way under you too. For no reason at all. I trust you understand what I mean?"

By the time the meaning of these words finally reached Amphitryon, he turned --- with a jerk, his whole body at once, the way he turned in a shield fight --- but saw only a back in a dirty, hooded chlamys vanishing into the crowd. He lunged after it --- no use; the mysterious counselor was gone without a trace.

...Amphitryon came home somber, and stone sober. And fell into deep thought.

There was plenty to think about.
