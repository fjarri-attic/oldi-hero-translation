#### 8

They were running through the night, veering right, to the northeast, cutting sharply around the mighty walls of the Cadmea, all but invisible in the wet darkness, feeling out by instinct where to set each foot; they were running through the night.

More than once Iphicles had gone racing through sodden Cithaeron in the dark, carrying prey on his shoulder far heavier than Galinthias's senseless body --- and he understood perfectly well that no six-year-old boy could have done what his... his father was doing now: subduing rebellious muscles, forcing his breath into a merciless rhythm, taming a child's heart and making it beat steady and tireless, the way a seasoned charioteer masters a young, unbroken horse.

And it all kept surfacing in his memory: the altar, the mangled bodies of the attendants, the beastlike howl tearing up into the sky; himself, Iphicles, casting his eyes about in search of a rope, not even sure yet what for --- whether to tie up the Tartarus-Possessed woman, or to bind her leg once he pulled the sacrificial knife from her thigh --- and Iolaus, who'd come up to the old woman, struck Galinthias a short, precise blow with a stone under the ear, cutting off her howl.

"Safer this way," the child's voice had said then. "Leave the knife alone. Just pick her up and carry her... she won't croak, the old wretch, she's tough as roots!"

Iphicles wanted to ask his father one more thing: what had become of the real Iolaus?

He didn't ask.

And he never would, to the end of his days.

So he would die, in willing ignorance; would die more a son than a father.

* * *

...Midnight caught up with them at a Dromos both of them knew.

Iphicles, surprised by nothing anymore, darted through after his father, while midnight balked in fright, stamping in place, and stopped to wait.

The house where Dummy usually arranged their meetings stood out sharply against the strangely gray sky. But no one waited on the threshold, and Iphicles didn't even need to go inside to know --- the house was empty.

"What, has he gone off carousing or something?!" Without finishing, Amphitryon waved his hand and swore viciously in Laconian, so thickly that Iphicles didn't understand a third of it.

The gist of it, it seemed, was that Hermeias ought to choke on a fish, for some reason.

In the murky twilight, Iphicles saw that the boyish palm was covered in blood --- the fake Iolaus, it seemed, had cut himself throwing that awkward knife. Amphitryon caught his son's glance, clenched and unclenched his fist a few times, which only made the blood flow harder, and bit his lip thoughtfully.

Iphicles waited.

"Follow me!" Amphitryon finally snapped, and ran back toward the Dromos, swaying slightly from side to side.

Iphicles hurried after him, steadying Galinthias, who had begun to moan.

Once again the path wound between the hills, twilight gave way to midnight, and Galinthias began to stir, coming to her senses from the rain and the damp, or from something else --- but suddenly Amphitryon stopped so abruptly that Iphicles nearly knocked the small figure off its feet.

A herma rose up before them.

Iphicles hadn't yet worked out why they'd need a roadside post dedicated to Hermes, when Amphitryon jumped up and landed a proper, honest-to-goodness slap across the stylized face carved atop the pillar.

With a bloodied palm.

Smearing blood on the wood.

"Here's your offering, choke on it!" Amphitryon roared, mixing the divine with the marketplace crude. "Fish is not enough for you, Highness?! This sacrifice, Olympian, take; son of Maia Pleiades and Zeus, him who gathers the clouds; craftiest one, petty thief and oathbreaker! Winged-heeled jester, where in blazes have you been off to?!"

And a second slap, louder than the first, cracked across the herma.

A noise behind him made Iphicles turn.

Not even a noise, really --- more a draft of cold.

No one but the Trickster (except perhaps Rainbow Iris, Hera's personal messenger) could open a Dromos so fast. The ghostly shimmer that had flared up behind Iphicles's back was still only gathering strength, twisting itself into a funnel, when a familiar silhouette, caduceus rod in hand --- and, for some reason, wearing a tall, jauntily cocked Phrygian cap --- was already clambering out of the glowing web, parting the threads with the rod.

By the light of the Dromos, which still hadn't snapped shut, Iphicles saw --- once Hermeias had freed himself and come closer --- that his friend Dummy was stark naked, save for his sandals, the silly cap, and the rod; in his left hand Hermeias held two dried little fish by the tail, and the god's cheek was smeared with blood.

A perfectly distinct print of a small hand.

What's more, certain signs made it plain that the naked Hermeias had just leapt up from a bed where he'd clearly not been alone.

"Have you lost your mind, lawagetas?!" the Trickster shouted, running up. "You could leave someone a stammerer that way! A blood sacrifice, and right across the mug at that --- I could barely make sense of it, half-asleep as I was!.."

For a moment it seemed to Iphicles that he was looking at the world through Hermeias's eyes --- and seeing the dim, blurred outline of a living boy, around whom a powerful, brooding shadow was gathering, far more vivid than the warm creature of flesh and blood.

And Iphicles also thought of the day when the shadow and the body would come into balance with one another.

"You'd sleep through Tartarus itself!" Amphitryon snarled at the god. "I'm out here, you know, busting my hump, catching one of the Tartarus-Possessed for them, her people nearly did my son in --- and you're grumbling that it's poor timing?! Next time I'll be sure to ask them to kindly wait while you deign to fly on over!"

"All right, all right," Hermeias began soothingly, but just then Galinthias jerked sharply on Iphicles's shoulder, then again, and it so closely resembled the convulsions of oncoming death-throes that Iphicles hurried to lower the old woman to the ground.

Into the mud.

The knife was no longer in the dwarf-woman's thigh --- whether she'd managed to pull it out along the way, or it had simply fallen out --- the old woman's legs were streaming with blood from her torn femoral artery, and Galinthias's pallor was the pallor of a corpse.

All the more terrible, then, was the happy grimace on her sharp, animal little muzzle.

"I'm going," Galinthias whispered, barely audible, staring unblinking at the fake Iolaus. "Going... to the Fallen. Killed by the sacrificial knife, in the shrine, by a twice dead man... I go down to Tartarus. Accept the offering, fathers, Golden Age shepherds... accept... me..."

And the old woman's eyes clouded over with a filmy haze, like a slaughtered hen's.

Iphicles, unable to tear his eyes away, stared at the dead woman, at the greenish little cloud that had begun to swirl above the corpse; it thickened, much like the shadow of Amphitryon he'd just seen, and out of the mildew-smelling mist a face emerged, a neck, bony shoulders... hands, making an obscene gesture...

"She's leaving!" flared through Iphicles's mind like heat lightning.

Beside him Hermeias swung the caduceus --- and the serpents from the rod lashed like a hissing whip around the old woman's scrawny neck, their fangs quivering beside her contorted face.

The scaly bonds went taut, and the shadow of the Tartarus-Possessed woman lunged aside, away from the dead body that had sheltered her for so long, away from the boy who had deceived her so terribly, away from the naked god in his winged sandals...

In vain.

Hermeias Psychopompos had led all manner of souls down to Hades, and not all of them had gone willingly.

"The Lord told me to pass on to you, lawagetas," the Trickster said curtly, a vein swelling on the god's forehead as it does under great strain, "that he doesn't forget a favor. And I'll add, for myself --- neither do I. Until we meet again, lawagetas... and thank you."

Iphicles stood over Galinthias's corpse, huddled in the mud, and watched Hermeias vanish, dragging Alcides's curse away behind him.

At least, that was what Iphicles wanted to believe.

Then he closed his eyes and listened to something within himself.

"Dad," Iphicles said, in a voice gone numb, "you know, Alcides is having a fit. Right now."
