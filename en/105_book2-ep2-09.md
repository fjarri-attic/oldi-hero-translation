#### 9

Oblivion refused to let Alcides go, rocking him with an insistent, monotonous croon, swaddling him in darkness and silence, tossing him on soft arms like a plump, good-natured mother trying to settle a fractious child; but the child stubbornly refused to sleep, kicking its little arms and legs, thrashing about --- and oblivion, sighing, took itself off.

Warm.

Dry.

Either there are no suspicious rustlings, or they are hiding themselves very well.

And one needn't be a sage to guess, even with his eyes still closed, that a good deal has happened since Alcides's last memories --- thunder, lightning, a downpour, stones and javelins.

So it is time to sit up and look around.

He sat up and looked around, feeling in his head a painful surf, breaking against the shores of accursed Kos.

"Chiron?" Alcides called softly, already understanding he was mistaken, that this was not the cave on Pelion where he had so often been a guest of the wise centaur, that it was just as quiet and snug here, but did not smell of dried herbs and roots; and besides, Chiron would have found it rather cramped in here.

"Come to, have you?" a solicitous old woman's voice inquired from behind him. "You just lie there, dearie, better to lie than to go gadding about... and Chiron's not here, Chiron's far away, in Thessaly. What would he be doing loitering about here, that four-legged know-it-all?"

Turning, Alcides found in the corner of the cave a hunched, hook-nosed old crone with startlingly clear blue eyes --- a combination strange enough in itself to give one pause.

"Was it..." the words came with difficulty, barely breaking through the booming surf inside his skull, "...was it you who saved me?"

The old woman batted her thick, girlish lashes.

"As if I've nothing better to do than haul strapping lads out of the muck," she giggled, fishing Alcides's clothes out from somewhere and deftly tossing them into his lap. "Oh sure, I lie awake dreaming of it, hauling some great lump like you over my shoulders! No, it was the little one who dragged you here --- soon as there's trouble, he remembers Crete, and in good times he doesn't so much as show his face, the scamp..."

"The little one?"

"To me, to old Dikti[^72], he's the little one; to everyone else --- Dias the father, Zeus the High-Thundering! Understand?"

[^72]: A mountain on Crete, in a cave of which Rhea hid the infant Zeus from Kronus, who was devouring his children.

Alcides nodded, getting dressed. His chiton turned out freshly washed and still slightly damp, his battle sandals with their bronze studs on leather straps had been scrubbed clean of Kos's mud, and the belt, for that matter, was not even his own.

"My thanks for shelter and kindness, goddess Dikti," he said, rising, and bowed low to the old woman.

"Goddess?" she said, surprised. "No, I'm one of the first, one of the Uranids --- of the Titans' own stock... Don't you mind that I look old --- old is just for your benefit, to keep us clear of sin!"

"What sin would that be?" Alcides smirked.

"The main one," the old woman scorched him with the blue flame of a youthful gaze. "When Zeus brought you here, I took one look: a fine-looking man, young, and I thought, soon as he comes round he'll start pestering me --- and you needed your rest, I'd have worried you clean to death! So I changed my shape, for safety's sake... And then Zeus took to grieving over you: however, he says, can we go to Phlegrae without Heracles?! The Family's locked in battle with the Gigantes, and here lies our chief ally, senseless!"

Alcides flinched.

The cave suddenly seemed a trap, a cunning snare with no way out.

"There you lay," Dikti went on meanwhile, "the little one all but in tears, when in flies this chit of a girl, spear and all, near put my eye out with it! Athena Promachos, if you please... And straight off she starts telling her father: Heracles took Kos, killed basileus Eurypylus, so you've been fretting over the wrong man, daddy! Well, the little one claps himself on the forehead, grabs his daughter by the scruff, and off the two of them go, like they've lost their wits... and you they left with me. Patch him up, they said, and then let him go off wherever he pleases!"

Dikti fell abruptly silent.

And almost at once a tall warrior stepped into the cave, pulling off his close horsehair-crested helmet.

The silver lions on the visitor's greaves bared their teeth at the old woman; a single grey lock fell across the warrior's eyes, he brushed it aside and looked straight at Alcides, who had gone still.

"Do you remember me?" the question rang out, clear and resonant.

"Yes," Alcides stretched, deliberately slow, as though after a long sleep, "I remember you, Ares Enyalius. Once, I refused to be your driver."

"Well, today I've come to hire myself out as your escort." Ares drew a sword with an ivory hilt from its sheath and tossed the weapon to Alcides; he caught it out of the air.

"To Phlegrae?" Alcides asked quietly.

"To Phlegrae."

"And are you certain, Enyalius, that I'll go fight for the Family?"

"I am. I suspect you have weighty reasons of your own for taking part in the Gigantomachy, apart from the Family; but one of them I know for certain."

"Which one?"

"Your brother is already at Phlegrae."

And Ares walked out of the cave, without even glancing back to see whether Heracles was following him.
