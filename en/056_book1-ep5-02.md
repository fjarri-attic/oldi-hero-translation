#### 2

Everything was beautiful in due measure: birds whistled and clicked deafeningly in the dark green foliage, the slanting rays of the sun splashed leopard spots across the path the brothers walked... and joyful flies went on circling above the damp hide, while the fleas, in a hurry to emigrate from the lion's fur, had already given Alkeides a thorough biting---he bore this vengeance of the dead lion manfully---and had now set to work on Iphikles, who was less steadfast in the face of temptation and scratched furiously at the itching spots.

"Are we really heroes now?" Iphikles finally broke the silence.

"Of course we are!" Alkeides gave a slight smirk. "Two against one lion---just like Dummy said..."

"Well, the shepherds will be pleased, anyway."

"Uh-huh." Alkeides nodded, clearly imitating Pan, shifting the heavy hide into a more comfortable position as he walked. "We've got ourselves two whole feats now---the daughters of our hospitable friend Thespios were pleased as well."

Iphikles chuckled merrily.

"I wonder---did those thick-legged mares really not notice there were two of us?"

"I don't think so."

"Neither do I. But people are saying it was us---or rather you, Alkeides---who didn't notice that you'd slept with not one but nearly fifty of them..."

"Oh, sure! Only a blind man wouldn't have noticed! And even then, only if his hands and feet were tied and his ears plugged with wax! Remember how they all kept peeking into the megaron when Thespios was entertaining us?"

"I remember, but only vaguely. I was sitting with my back to the entrance and trying to keep my face in shadow... By the way, how many of them were there, really? Everyone says fifty, but I think that's a lie."

"Seeing as we're still alive, it must be. Twenty-five to twenty-eight, no more. I counted at first, then lost count."

"Heroes..."

"Heroes... Who? Us, or them?"

"Doesn't matter. The daughters all take after their father, and Thespios churned out so many of them---may Zeus grant everyone as much! Now he's going around claiming Alkeides pleasured his daughters all at once, in a single night, because he's a hero..."

"A hero... Who?"

"You, of course!"

"Ohh... let him lie. He'll lie, and we'll add that it wasn't even a whole night but an hour---who'll believe him after that?! But at our age our father had already been through two wars, great-grandfather Perseus went up against Medusa, Kadmos founded Thebes, and here we are, pleasuring some basileus's daughters and knocking off this consumptive lion---and that's it! Now, Alkathoos, your Automedusa's father---one like this, all on his own---"

Alkeides suddenly broke off and slowed his pace.

"Sorry, Iphikles."

"Never mind... it barely hurts anymore. She was... gone so quickly. I ought to go to Thebes, take a look at Iolaos---how's he getting on? Some father I make!"

"I said I'm sorry!"

"Never mind... cheer up, brother---we did kill the lion between us, after all! Sorry specimen or not, but he was still a man-eater! They say he'd gobbled up more than a dozen people. So whatever we think of it ourselves, they'll still call it a feat! Remember what Dummy said?"

"That two against one..."

"No! That a hero stands alone. It won't do for the two of us to show up at the camp together. I'll run ahead and announce your great feat---and you'll catch up after, with the hide and the club!"

"All right," Alkeides agreed reluctantly. "Only let's eat something first. I'm dying of hunger, after a feat like that..."

Settling in the fragrant shade of an enormous laurel, the brothers eagerly set upon millet flatbreads with pungent goat cheese, washing it down with tart, heavily watered wine.

This time it was Alkeides who spoke first.

"Listen, Iphikles... The last time we were at Cheiron's, and Dummy had slipped off somewhere, I asked the centaur: what do the gods do when human sacrifices are made to them?"

"I remember," Iphikles said curtly; he never liked this kind of talk.

"And Cheiron said that at that moment the gods shut themselves away from Tartaros, wall themselves off, reject the Fallen! And we... that is, I---I'm not behaving like a god but like the most ordinary coward! I run, I hide, and you watch to see I don't do anything foolish! Fine, when we were children---but enough of that now! We should do what the gods do! We need to fight! Not let those slippery ones inside! Everyone's been grooming us as heroes since birth---father, Autolykos, Iphitos, Dummy, Cheiron---so why do we hide?! A hero must fight! Why should we go looking for monsters, when they're right beside us?! Or rather, inside us. And we're just like children: 'they're picking on the little ones'---and off into the bushes!"

"A hero must fight?" Iphikles echoed, but with a slightly different emphasis than his brother's. "You're probably right."

"Probably? I'm certainly right!"

"I don't know. And I won't know---not until the next fit. That's when we'll see. For now, I'm off to announce the death of the Man-Eater of Kithairon, and your great feat. See you, brother!"

And Iphikles rose lightly to his feet, gathering up the bundle of javelins.

"Well, that's just great," Alkeides grumbled, finishing off the flatbread. "And now I have to drag the hide and the club both..."

"Such is the hero's hard lot." Iphikles grinned slyly and vanished into the tamarisk thicket, unerringly picking out the shortest way to the shepherds' camp.

Alkeides looked at the half-eaten cheese and glumly stuffed the weeping wedge into his mouth.
