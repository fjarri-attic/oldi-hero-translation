#### 2

Everything was beautiful in due measure: birds whistled and clicked deafeningly in the dark green foliage, the slanting rays of the sun dappled the path the brothers walked with leopard spots... and joyful flies went on circling above the damp hide, while the fleas, in a hurry to emigrate from the lion's fur, had already bitten Alcides thoroughly --- who bore this vengeance of the dead lion with fortitude --- and had now set to work on Iphicles, who was less resilient to the itch and scratched furiously at the tormented spots.

"Are we really heroes now?" Iphicles finally broke the silence.

"Of course we are!" Alcides gave a slight smirk. "Two against one lion --- just like Dummy said..."

"Well, the shepherds will be pleased, anyway."

"Uh-huh," Alcides nodded, clearly imitating Pan, resettling the heavy hide more comfortably on the move. "We've got ourselves two whole feats now --- the daughters of our hospitable friend Thespius were pleased as well."

Iphicles chuckled merrily.

"I wonder --- did those thick-legged mares really not notice there were two of us?"

"I don't think so."

"Neither do I. But people are saying it was really just the one of us --- or rather, that you, Alcides, never noticed you'd slept with not one but nearly fifty of them..."

"Well, of course! Only a blind man wouldn't have noticed! And even then, only if his hands and feet were tied and his ears plugged with wax! Remember how they all kept peeking into the megaron when Thespius was treating us?"

"I remember, but poorly. I was sitting with my back to the entrance and trying to keep my face in the shadow... By the way, how many of them were there, really? Everyone says fifty, but I think that's a lie."

"Since we're still alive --- it must be a lie. Twenty-five, twenty-eight or so, no more. I counted at first, then lost count."

"Heroes..."

"Heroes... Who? Us, or them?"

"Doesn't matter. The daughters all take after their father, and Thespius churned out so many of them --- may Zeus grant everyone as much! Now he's going around claiming Alcides pleasured his daughters all at once, in a single night, because he's a hero..."

"A hero... Who?"

"You, of course!"

"Ohh... let him lie. He'll lie, and we'll add that it wasn't even a whole night but an hour --- who'll believe him after that?! Only, our father had already been through two wars at our age, our great-grandfather Perseus went up against Medusa, Cadmus founded Thebes, and here we are, pleasuring some basileus's daughters and knocking off this consumptive lion --- and that's it! Even Alcathous, your Automedusa's father, would go alone against such a---"

Alcides suddenly broke off and slowed his pace.

"Sorry, Iphicles."

"Never mind... it barely hurts anymore. She was... gone so quickly. I ought to go to Thebes, take a look at Iolaus --- how's he doing there? Some father I make!"

"Well, sorry!"

"Never mind... cheer up, brother --- we did kill the lion between us, after all! Nothing much, but a man-eater still! They say he'd gobbled up more than a dozen people. So however we reckon it for ourselves --- they'll still call it a feat! Remember what Dummy said?"

"That two against one..."

"No! That a hero stands alone. It won't do for the two of us to show up at the camp together. I'll run ahead and announce your great feat --- and you'll catch up after, with the hide and the club!"

"Go on, then," Alcides agreed reluctantly. "Only let's eat something first. I'm dying of hunger, after a feat like that..."

Settling in the fragrant shade of an enormous laurel, the brothers eagerly set upon millet flatbreads with pungent goat cheese, washing the food down with sour, heavily watered wine.

This time it was Alcides who spoke first.

"Listen, Iphicles... The last time we were at Chiron's, and Dummy had slipped off somewhere, I asked the centaur: what do the gods do when human sacrifices are made to them?"

"I remember," Iphicles said curtly, never fond of such talk.

"And Chiron said that at that moment the gods shut themselves off from Tartarus, wall themselves in, cast the Fallen away from themselves! And we... that is, I --- I'm not behaving like a god but like the most ordinary coward! I run, I hide, and you watch to see I don't do anything foolish! Fine, when we were children --- but enough of that now! We need to be like the gods! We need to fight! Not let those slippery ones inside! We've been marked out for heroes since birth --- Father, Autolycus, Iphitus, Dummy, Chiron --- so why do we hide?! A hero must fight! Why should we go looking for monsters, when they're right beside us?! Or rather, inside us. And we're just like children: they're picking on the little ones --- and off into the bushes!"

"A hero must fight?" Iphicles echoed, but a little differently than his brother. "You're probably right."

"Probably? I'm certainly right!"

"I don't know. And I won't know --- not until the next fit. That's when we'll see. For now, I'm off to announce the death of the Man-Eater of Cithaeron, and your great feat. See you, brother!"

And Iphicles rose lightly to his feet, gathering up the bundle of javelins.

"Well, there it is," Alcides grumbled, finishing off the flatbread. "And now I have to drag the hide and the club both..."

"Such is the hero's hard lot," Iphicles smirked slyly and vanished into the tamarisk thicket, unerringly picking out the shortest way to the shepherds' camp.

Alcides looked at the unfinished cheese and glumly stuffed the weeping wedge into his mouth.
