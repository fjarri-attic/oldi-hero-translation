### Sixth epeisodion.

#### 1

The awakening was anything but pleasant.

Amphitryon was convulsively tearing his way toward the surface of a nightmarish dream, fighting for every gulp of something that seemed as necessary as air without actually being air; he thrashed his arms and legs in a frenzy, not feeling it and growing ever more frenzied, while the nightmare sobbed and would not let Amphitryon go, its brittle fingers clutching at him, until it cracked beneath his fist and fell still.

That was how it was, if it was at all.

For a while Amphitryon lay motionless on the damp, hot surface --- the one he had striven so hard to reach --- savoring the simple fact of lying there, of the rumbling in his stomach, of the bitter saliva pooling in his mouth, which he could swallow or could just as well spit out, and now a sticky thread ran down his chin, trailing off somewhere below --- but that he no longer felt.

His left eyelid unexpectedly obeyed and moved a little. Just a little, opening the narrowest of slits --- so that Amphitryon would not think that it, the proud left eyelid, depended on him so very much.

A sharp little animal muzzle stared at him, wrinkling warily.

"What's wrong with him, Galinthias?" someone asked anxiously from behind. "It's been the second week already..."

The voice was familiar, but he lacked the strength to place it fully.

The eyelid fell shut, and the darkness thickened once more, lit by crimson flashes, and Amphitryon felt himself buried alive. Around him arched the dome of a tholos --- a tomb in which basileis or their close kin were usually buried; the diameter of the dome's base was always equal to its height, and a corridor led out from the burial chamber, ending at a doorway flanked by two carved half-columns of green marble, but the door itself was barred from the other side, because one does not leave a tholos --- one only enters it, and even then on the shoulders of others, to remain forever; and Amphitryon screamed soundlessly that he had never been a basileus, that he did not want to rot in a tholos, that the living had no place in a tomb...

And once more he returned to life, trembling from the memory, from the soulless silence of the tomb and the crunch of the murdered nightmare.

"He'll live," a little old-woman's voice squeaked with confidence. "I feel it in my gut: Thanatos the Iron-Hearted has let him go. Only..."

"What do you mean, 'only,' Galinthias?" --- in the familiar voice, worry throbbed like a bumblebee trapped in a closed jug. "Only --- what?!"

No answer.

Silence.

The shuffle of feet, the creak of a door...

A cool palm on a burning forehead.

"Are you feeling awful, Iolaus?"

"I don't know about Iolaus," Amphitryon wanted to say, "but I feel awful."

But instead he opened his eyes.

Bending over him was Alcmene's face --- aged, gaunt, somehow unnaturally large, and in her beloved eyes, in their dark, moist depths, care and tenderness flickered, but a tenderness that was foreign, a care that was unfamiliar, frightening; a half-grey lock fell across her high forehead, beaded with sweat, its tip brushing the deep wrinkle between brows still thick, brows that Alcmene had never plucked, even in her youth, to suit the whims of Cretan or Mycenaean fashion --- and Amphitryon reached out, unable to help himself, to brush the lock away.

The hand that appeared before his eyes was thin and fine-boned, like that of the gravely ill.

Choking on horror, Amphitryon watched his wife take this stranger's hand and press it to lips whose warmth the old lawagetas knew so well.

The former lawagetas.

"Praise Paean the Healer, you've come to, Iolaus!" --- the words born of those lips entered Amphitryon like the point of Erginus's blade. "Never mind, grandma will bring you something to drink now..."

And Amphitryon understood, remembering the agony of the sobbing nightmare, that he had just killed his own grandson --- in order to live himself.

"Mom!" someone called from the corridor. "Are you free?"

"Yes, Iphicles, I'm coming," Alcmene said, straightening the blanket over the six-year-old boy lying before her, struck for the thousandth time by his resemblance to his late grandfather.

She went out, carefully pulling the door shut behind her, never noticing how little Iolaus, son of Iphicles and Automedusa, sank his teeth into his own forearm to keep from howling.

Fearfully, wildly, like a wolf caught in a trap.
