#### 11

...Dust.

Dust, dust, dust...

Two left feet strike the ground at once.

Two right feet.

Two chests rise and fall.

Loose white garments flutter, as though their wearers were still running, hurrying, rushing on...

Four pale eyes look at the one who has dared to stop the theoroi bearing branches of laurel and magnolia.

They look for a long time, blinking their colorless lashes in unison, as though not understanding who stands before them, or whether anyone stands before them at all.

Laughter bursts from two throats, hoarse, unrestrained laughter, in which the slyness of an idiot quivers playfully.

As abruptly as it began, the laughter breaks off.

And the two creatures in white garments part with a soft, insinuating step, to the left and to the right, like two arms opening for a tender embrace, and halt a spear's thrust away from the lone man standing in their path.

Laurel and magnolia on either side of the lionskin.

Each of the Molionids is taller than Heracles by very nearly a head; each of the Molionids stoops, trying to peer into Heracles's face.

"Cleaner!" both say in one and the same cracked voice. "I'm glad to see you, Cleaner! There, you don't believe me, you want to kill me, but I really am glad... How funny it's all turned out, Cleaner: you, the son of Zeus, stand before me in a stinking, flea-ridden skin, while I parade about in the white robes of a theoros! You're an outcast, nobody loves you, while I am clean of miasma and pleasing to the gods! Now isn't that funny --- I, who died twice at the hands of the Olympians, I, the malicious Possessed, am pleasing to the gods?! Why aren't you laughing, Cleaner?"

Not a cloud in the sky, and the stunted olive tree behind the silent Heracles languishes in the heat.

"You killed me" --- it seems that Heracles did not utter these words.

It seems they were born by themselves, out of the heat and the silence, were born and took their stand at the roadside, arms folded across their chest.

"So what?" The flat faces of the Molionids gather into folds, like the snouts of turtles, and the slanting, colorless little eyes glint from somewhere deep inside, out of the wrinkled mash.

"So what, Cleaner?! You think anyone cares about that? I know very well that nobody does... after all, I'm just like you now, I'm one in two bodies, I got inside these idiots, I was cutting their flesh, writhing with pain..."

Both Molionids shamelessly hitch up the hems of their garments and turn sideways to Heracles.

Along the thigh, from the waist almost down to the knee, on each creature runs a hideous scar --- swollen, wide, crusted with scabs of dying skin.

"Right here I slashed the screaming body with a knife, my dear Cleaner, I cut apart this offspring of that fool Molione and Three-Bodied Geryon... you think I'm mad? You think right, Cleaner. Even in this we're alike now --- both insane, both unwanted by anyone... Shall we trade clothes, Cleaner? You'll run on, you'll become a theoros, a favorite of the gods, and I'll become Heracles, the son of Zeus... Would you like that? Or would you rather we killed each other again? What do you want, Cleaner? What?!"

The Molionids take a step, then another, and lay their hands on Heracles's shoulders.

"You killed me" --- the dispassionate words are born by themselves, and the man in the lionskin turns into a whirlwind.

When the dust settles, two gigantic bodies can be seen writhing on the ground, unable to rise.

Even their writhing is identical.

And rust-red dirt stains the white garments of the theoroi.

Heracles turns his back on the crippled Molionids and walks toward the stunted olive tree.

By a root jutting from the ground lie a bow and a quiver of arrows.

"Are you going to shoot, Cleaner?" wheezes the cracked voice. "Oh, how I envy you... I can't shoot anymore, Cleaner, I, Eurytus the archer, can't shoot!.. When I look into the distance, I see double! Shoot, then, Cleaner, shoot worthily, so that I can see once more how it's done, so that I can look back on my life --- and then, perhaps, I won't want to come back anymore..."

The arrow's sting glistens with black slime.

"You killed me" --- the words stand at the roadside, arms folded across their chest, and look mournfully at two perfectly identical bodies.

From the heap of white garments protrude two perfectly identical arrows.

Dust.

A finger, stirring in the cramped sky, presses against the back of the departing man, as though urging him on.

A blue finger with swollen knuckles and a bitten nail.

A broad back in a shabby lionskin.
