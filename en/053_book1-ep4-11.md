#### 11

Wayward and headstrong, Automedusa, daughter of Alkathoos from Megara, burst into the twins' lives swiftly, but---alas---not for long: a mere four years. At the end of that span she died in childbirth, bringing Iphikles a son and Alkeides a nephew. The boy was named Iolaos and, at the first opportunity, sent off to Thebes, to his grandmother Alkmene.

Thus was born Iolaos, son of Iphikles and Automedusa, whose future fate would prove, perhaps, even more tortuous and singular than the fates of his father and uncle, for it would be Iolaos who, in his time, would bequeath that he be buried in the grave of his late grandfather Amphitryon (to the no small shock of half of Hellas), it would be Iolaos who would prove a faithful friend and protector to the aged Alkmene and the numerous Heraklids, and years and years later, at the hand of the great Hektor, would fall one Protesilaos, son of Iphikles... Protesilaos, meaning "First of the People," but also meaning "Iolaos the First."

But of all that---in its appointed time.
