#### 11

Wayward and headstrong, Automedusa, daughter of Alcathous from Megara, burst into the twins' lives swiftly, but --- alas --- not for long: a mere four years. At the end of that span she died in childbirth, bringing Iphicles a son and Alcides a nephew. The boy was named Iolaus and, at the first opportunity, sent off to Thebes, to his grandmother Alcmene.

Thus was born Iolaus, son of Iphicles and Automedusa, whose future fate would prove, perhaps, even more tortuous and singular than the fates of his father and uncle, for it would be Iolaus who, in his time, would bequeath that he be buried in the grave of his late grandfather Amphitryon (to the no small shock of half of Hellas), it would be Iolaus who would prove a faithful friend and protector to the aged Alcmene and the numerous Heraclids, and years and years later, at the hand of the great Hector, would fall one Protesilaus, son of Iphicles... Protesilaus, meaning "First of the People," but also meaning "Iolaus the First."

But of all that --- in its appointed time.
