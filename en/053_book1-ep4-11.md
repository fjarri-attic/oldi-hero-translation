#### 11

Wayward and headstrong, Automedusa, daughter of the Megarian Alkathoos, burst into the twins' lives swiftly, but---alas---not for long: a mere four years. At the end of that span she died in childbirth, giving Iphikles a son and Alkeides a nephew. The boy was named Iolaos and, at the first opportunity, sent off to Thebes, to his grandmother Alkmene.

Thus was born Iolaos, son of Iphikles and Automedusa, whose fate will prove, perhaps, even more winding and singular than the fates of his father and uncle, for it is Iolaos who will one day ask to be buried in the tomb of his late grandfather Amphitryon (to the considerable shock of half of Hellas), it is Iolaos who will be a faithful friend and protector to the aged Alkmene and the numerous Heraklids, and years upon years later there will fall, by the hand of the great Hektor, one Protesilaos, son of Iphikles... Protesilaos, which means "First of the People," but also means "Iolaos the First."

But of all that---in due time.
