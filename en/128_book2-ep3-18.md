#### 18

Iolaos got stuck in Phylake not for one day, as he had expected, but for four.

Laodameia had lost yet another pregnancy; his wife's life was out of danger, but Iolaos, comforting the weeping Laodameia, had to resign himself once and for all to the fact that they would have no children.

Then one of the rulers of Thessaly, a man by the name of Philoktetes, came to see basileus Akastos about some disputed plots of plowland---and it took Iolaos a full day and night of drinking with this very Philoktetes, who listened greedily to tales of Herakles's campaigns of old, before the dispute over the plots was settled in Phylake's favor.

Over the past decade Iolaos had managed to forget what it meant to be late.

He was forced to remember.

...They woke him in the middle of the night. Dressing in haste, he ran out into the courtyard---and heard, from a messenger sent from Trachis, the story of the tragedy that had taken place.

Choking on his words, the messenger told how Deianeira, tired of waiting for Iolaos, had sent Lichas ahead to her husband's camp, entrusting him with a festive chiton for the offering of the proper sacrifices in honor of the victory. For the next two days Deianeira was sullen and short-tempered, kept slapping the maidservants, shouted at the children and the household, and then shut herself up in her chambers altogether and would not come out.

At last, from Herakles's camp, pitched at the foot of the mountain ridge of Oite, a courier deathly pale came racing in on a chariot---not Lichas, another man, one of Keyx's trusted people.

From the courier's account it became clear that great Herakles, having put on the chiton he had been sent and begun offering the sacrifices, had suddenly fallen into madness. As soon as the blood of the slaughtered calf gushed onto his clothes, the son of Zeus began to look wildly about, rushed this way and that as if in search of a way out, flung aside Lichas, who had run up to him (Lichas fell from a cliff to his death), and began tearing his clothes off himself, shouting that he was mortally wounded.

One of Deianeira's maidservants---when the courier reached the part about the chiton---suddenly let out a hysterical shriek and started shouting for the whole courtyard to hear that her mistress had kept a little jug of the blood of the centaur Nessos, killed by Herakles's poisoned arrow; and that she herself, so she claimed, had seen how Deianeira, burning with jealousy of the Iole she had never met, had rubbed that poisonous blood into the chiton being sent to her husband---a linen one, dark brown, with little curls of blue waves along the hem.

Deianeira herself heard out the accusation coldly, went up to the gynaikeion and---no one even thought to stop her---fell upon a sword she had kept in her chest.

And by that confirmed the truth of the accusation brought against her.

"Madmen!" Iolaos waved to the excited Philoktetes, who was hovering nearby, to have the horses harnessed. "Lunatics! What truth?! If the chiton was rubbed with a poison that kills at a touch---then why didn't Deianeira herself die first? What, did she rub the chiton wearing oxhide mittens?! And where have you ever seen blood that wouldn't dry up in two years?!"

Soon Iolaos, and Philoktetes, who had tagged along with him, were already hurtling toward Oite, at whose foot Herakles's camp lay.

"Maybe it really was Deianeira?" Philoktetes, gasping in the dense wind that beat into his face and clutching at the sides of the chariot every other moment, was spinning his guesses for the umpteenth time. "Why not, it fits nicely... jealousy, blood mixed half and half with Lernaean poison... though no---what, Herakles didn't notice that his festive chiton was smeared all over with stinking blood?! And don't drive so hard, Iolaos, we'll be smashed to pieces!"

"Better keep quiet!" Iolaos snapped, picking up speed.

He no longer doubted what had happened.

He had seen a linen, dark brown chiton with little curls of waves along the hem once before.

Drenched in blood.

Only not in the blood of the centaur Nessos, and not in the blood of a sacrificial calf---but in living, human blood...

It was in just such a chiton, worn next to the body beneath the armor, that one of the twins had lain dying in Pheneos, on the square beneath the elm.

And the other, the one left alive, more than ten years later had seen exactly the same clothing on himself, stained crimson with blood, and warriors crowding around...

[This is the end,]{.thoughts} Iolaos thought, wincing at the wind lashing his face like a whip. [This is the end. So here it is, the prophecy---Herakles will die by the hand of one already dead. And I thought Teiresias meant me... but it turns out---not me. I don't know who this cursed gift comes from---to go off in spirit into the past---but it all fits!.. He's dying---because he's stuck in the past, in that day when he has already died once! The bloodied chiton, the cry that he is mortally wounded, Lichas thrown aside---poor lad, Herakles flung him away that time too, to save him... This is the end. He is dying for the second time.]{.thoughts}

The earth hurled itself under the horses' hooves like a suicide.
