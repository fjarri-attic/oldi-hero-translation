#### 18

Iolaos got stuck in Phylake not for one day, as he had expected, but for four.

Laodameia had lost yet another pregnancy; his wife's life was out of danger, but Iolaos, comforting the weeping Laodameia, had to resign himself once and for all to the fact that they would have no children.

Then one of the rulers of Thessaly, a man by the name of Philoktetes, came to see basileus Akastos about some disputed plots of plowland---and it took Iolaos a full day and night of drinking with this same Philoktetes, who eagerly lapped up tales of Herakles's campaigns of old, before the dispute over the plots was settled in Phylake's favor.

Over the past decade Iolaos had all but forgotten what it meant to be late.

He was forced to remember.

...They woke him in the middle of the night. Dressing in haste, he ran out into the courtyard---and heard, from a herald sent from Trachis, the story of the tragedy that had taken place.

Choking on his words, the herald told how Deianeira, tired of waiting for Iolaos, had sent Lichas ahead to her husband's camp, entrusting him with a festive chiton to wear for the sacrifices due in honor of the victory. For the next two days Deianeira was sullen and short-tempered, kept slapping the maidservants, shouted at the children and the household, and then shut herself in her chambers altogether and would not come out.

At last, from Herakles's camp, pitched at the foot of the mountain ridge of Oite, a deathly pale messenger came racing in on a chariot---not Lichas, but another, one of Keyx's trusted men.

From the messenger's account it became clear that great Herakles, having put on the chiton he had been sent and begun offering the sacrifices, had suddenly fallen into madness. As soon as the blood of the slaughtered calf gushed onto his clothes, the son of Zeus began to look wildly about, rushed this way and that as if in search of a way out, flung aside Lichas, who had run up to him (Lichas fell from a cliff to his death), and began tearing at his clothes, shouting that he was mortally wounded.

One of Deianeira's maidservants---when the messenger reached the part about the chiton---suddenly let out a hysterical shriek and started shouting for the whole courtyard to hear that her mistress had kept a little jug of the blood of the centaur Nessos, killed by Herakles's poisoned arrow; and that she herself, so she claimed, had seen how Deianeira, burning with jealousy of Iole, whom she had never even met, had rubbed that poisonous blood into the chiton meant for her husband---a linen one, dark brown, with little curls of blue waves along the hem.

Deianeira herself heard out the accusation coldly, went up to the gynaikeion and---no one even thought to stop her---fell upon a sword she had kept in a chest in her room.

Thereby confirming the truth of the accusation brought against her.

"Madmen!" Iolaos signaled to the excited Philoktetes, who was hovering nearby, to have the horses harnessed. "Lunatics! What truth?! If the chiton was rubbed with a poison that kills at a touch---then why didn't Deianeira herself die first? What, did she rub the chiton wearing oxhide mittens?! And where have you ever seen blood that wouldn't dry in two years?!"

Soon Iolaos and Philoktetes, who had tagged along, were hurtling toward Oite, at whose foot Herakles's camp lay.

"Maybe it really was Deianeira?" Philoktetes, gasping in the wind that buffeted his face and clutching at the sides of the chariot every other moment, was speculating for the umpteenth time. "Why not, it fits nicely... jealousy, blood mixed half and half with Lernaian poison... though no---what, Herakles didn't notice that his festive chiton was smeared all over with stinking blood?! And don't drive so hard, Iolaos, we'll be smashed to pieces!"

"Just keep quiet!" Iolaos snapped, picking up speed.

He was no longer in any doubt about what had happened.

He had seen once before a dark brown linen chiton with little curls of waves along the hem.

Drenched in blood.

Only not in the blood of the centaur Nessos, and not in the blood of a sacrificial calf---but in living, human blood...

It was in just such a chiton, worn next to the body beneath the armor, that one of the twins had lain dying in Pheneos, on the square beneath the elm.

And the other, the one left alive, had now, more than ten years later, seen the very same garment on himself, soaked crimson with blood, and warriors crowding around...

[This is the end,]{.thoughts} Iolaos thought, wincing at the wind lashing his face like a whip. [This is the end. So here it is, the prophecy---Herakles will die by the hand of one already dead. And I thought Teiresias meant me... but it turns out---not me. I don't know who this cursed gift comes from---slipping back into the past in spirit---but it all fits!... He's dying---because he's stuck in the past, in that very day when he already died once! The bloodied chiton, the cry that he is mortally wounded, Lichas thrown aside---poor lad, Herakles flung him away that time too, to save him... This is the end. He is dying for the second time.]{.thoughts}

The earth hurled itself under the horses' hooves like a suicide.
