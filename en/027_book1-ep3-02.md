#### 2

"Stifling," Autolycus repeated, for no particular reason, slapping himself resoundingly on his hairy chest.

Amphitryon nodded.

The gymnasium erupted with a chorus of voices and the din of a magnificent brawl.

"Polyctor's cronies are back from the washroom!" Autolycus burst out laughing. "Well, now it starts!... Hold fast, little Amphitryads!"

Amphitryon rose to his feet, winked at Autolycus and Castor, then meaningfully pressed a finger to his lips and, stepping aside, hid himself around the corner of the gymnasium.

Just in time --- the door of the hall suddenly flew open, and two flushed boys shot out, immediately setting about slamming the wretched door shut and wedging it with a hefty length of firewood (evidently stashed there in advance).

Slammed it.

Wedged it.

And shrieked with delight, the way only eight-year-olds can shriek.

"Well, well," Autolycus intoned ominously, and the boys' skinny backs instantly went rigid in anticipation of who-knew-what. "Running riot, are we... Shaming the name of your father and your teachers. Ashamed to look them in the eye, I suppose, so you turn your backsides to them instead..."

The boys sighed and turned to face Autolycus, unsuccessfully attempting to smooth their hair and put on a dignified air.

"Hm," Autolycus drawled, peering at the two utterly identical faces. "Well then, for starters come clear --- who's who?"

"But they were," both brothers grumbled, lowering their insolent eyes without any particular contrition, "they were picking on the little ones..."

"Deaf, are you?!" Autolycus barked at them. "I asked --- who's who?! Little ones..."

"I'm Alcides," the left-hand boy pursed his lips. "You can see for yourself! What's there to ask?!"

A drop of sweat ran down his cheek to the corner of his mouth, and he promptly licked it away.

"And I'm Alcides," the right-hand boy declared, offended. "I'm Alcides, and you're an idiot. And a liar."

A drop of sweat ran down his cheek to the corner of his mouth, and he promptly licked it away.

Then stuck his tongue out at his brother.

Such a broad tongue, too, like a shovel... lying with a tongue like that must be a real pleasure.

Castor weighed the spear in his hand and glanced with interest at Autolycus, who was tugging at his beard. Autolycus stoically bore this look, cracked his knuckles, and reached for the forked teacher's staff.

"Well, I'm Iphicles," the left one hastened to report. "I swear by your dad Hermes, teacher, I'm Iphicles... and my eyes are smart, and I've been behaving well..."

"And only a fool would believe you," the right one finished. "But teacher Autolycus isn't a fool, he's smart, he can see right away that I'm the one who's Iphicles, because I'm good, and there's no reason to beat me with the staff... and Polyctor's a mangy goat, that's what I told him!... And I'll say it again..."

The gymnasium door shuddered menacingly under pressure from within.

"Move the log," Autolycus ordered, turning slightly away to hide a treacherous smile. "We'll sort this out later."

The brothers exchanged glances --- and two bare feet instantly kicked the log away, sending it tumbling end over end through the air. Castor raised an appraising eyebrow, glancing sidelong at Autolycus; the wrestler snorted into his beard, watching the boys leap clear of the door --- and a good dozen adolescents came tumbling out of the gymnasium, forming a heap on the ground.

"The little ones!" the brothers whooped with joy. "They're picking on the little ones!.."

And they hurled themselves into the tangle of bodies, trampling on someone's backs, kicking at napes and backsides, dealing out blows left and right with sheer relish. A cloud of dust rose; Autolycus tumbled off the curb laughing and started rolling in the sand like an excited animal, and even on Castor's well-proportioned face something resembling condescending approval appeared.

"Enough!" Amphitryon commanded, emerging from around the corner of the gymnasium, and everything fell silent. The former lawagetas's imperious voice seemed to sober the whole gathering at once --- Castor went back to his spear, Autolycus began brushing himself off, the adolescents stopped their shouting, and the Amphitryad brothers, with visible regret, abandoned the field of battle and came up to their father.

"Alcides?" Amphitryon said, looking straight at the son whose neck and shoulders were scratched as if he'd fought his way through a wild rosebush.

"Yes, Father," the boy answered meekly.

"Iphicles?" This was addressed to the second, with bleeding scrapes on both legs.

"Yes, Father..."

"Ten laps at a chase pace! And five more at a march pace... Now!"

* * *

By the time the twins had dashed off toward the running tracks, and the indignant adolescents, who had tried to complain, had instead been sent off to the discus field; by the time the dust had settled, Autolycus had finished laughing, Castor had carried off his spear, and Amphitryon himself had left the palaestra altogether and set off for home --- a richly adorned chariot drawn by a pair of black horses drew up at the palaestra.

Autolycus, not troubling himself to throw on so much as a stitch of clothing, watched sullenly as the chariot halted twenty paces from him and, its reins looped over a post, a very tall youth of seventeen or eighteen leapt down and made his way toward Autolycus with a dancing gait.

"Rejoice!" the visitor called out while still some way off. "So this is the famous palaestra of Amphitryon Perseid?"

"So it is," Autolycus replied without any great warmth, setting one foot on the edge of the curb and lacing the fingers of both hands over his bulging knee.

The wrestler's powerful shoulders shifted slightly forward, and his peaceable stance began to look something like an athlete's stance before the start of a bout. Autolycus, you see, had no love for strangers and got on with them poorly, awkwardly --- but then, when the cunning oath-breaker Autolycus, son of Hermes Psychopompos, did happen to like someone...

Iphitus apparently sensed something, for he stopped well short of reaching Autolycus. To an outside observer, the pair would have looked quite comical --- a naked, stocky, mighty man, as if grown into the earth, in the very prime of his years, and a long-legged, long-armed stripling in a bright blue chlamys over a snow-white chiton, blinking bashfully and shifting from foot to foot.

Not being an outside observer, however, Autolycus instantly managed to gauge the hidden strength lurking within the youth --- and the teacher's shoulders relaxed, his black eyes glinting good-naturedly in his bearded face.

"My name is Iphitus," the youth introduced himself hastily. "Iphitus from Oechalia. You've heard of it, I expect --- it's on the island of Euboea..."

"Long way to come," Autolycus nodded. "Here to study, then, Iphitus of Oechalia?"

"Hardly," the youth answered indifferently. "More likely to teach. But how do you know I'm Iphitus of Oechalia? Are you acquainted with my father, by any chance?"

Autolycus felt a twinge of unease --- a rare thing for him. Iphitus of Oechalia? Acquainted with his father? Come here to teach?! Why, hadn't he just introduced himself as Iphitus from Oechalia... wasn't that the same thing as Iphitus of Oechalia?

"With which father?" the wrestler blurted out.

"With mine," Iphitus repeated. "With Eurytus, basileus of Oechalia. You know him, don't you?"

"No, never had the honor," Autolycus was gradually recovering himself. After all, what was so remarkable about it --- finishing lessons at the Theban palaestra and coming face to face with the eldest son of the basileus of Oechalia (who but the heir would so calmly call himself "of Oechalia"?), a young man who'd come all this way from Euboea for the specific purpose not of studying, but of teaching...

Teaching whom?

Him, Autolycus, of all people?!

Then --- teaching him what?!

Wrestling? No, surely not wrestling --- he was standing much too far away... Autolycus knew from his own experience that seasoned wrestlers, even in the course of a friendly conversation, tended to keep close to their interlocutor, within arm's reach; it got into the flesh and blood, became second nature, a habit, because farther away felt uncomfortable, closer felt dangerous, while like that, half a step short of a grip, felt exactly right.

No, Iphitus was no wrestler.

Nor was he a shield-bearing warrior --- because Castor, for one, always kept at spear-thrust distance, four or five cubits from his interlocutor, trying to maintain a margin of empty space (not as much as his brother Polydeuces, a fist fighter, liked, but about half again as much), and Autolycus had noticed more than once that in conversation with Castor he tended to crowd the man toward the nearest wall, unconsciously trying to close the distance, which in turn made Castor take a step back.

And this visitor stood even farther off than Castor, and it was plain that this was simply how he found it comfortable to converse...

A kitharode? Then the lad would never surpass Linus!

A charioteer? The bearing wasn't right.

Just some brash young pup? Didn't seem like it either...

Puzzling it over, Autolycus glanced, without meaning to, straight into the eyes of Iphitus, who had by then stopped blinking --- and suddenly understood everything, understood it even before he noticed what sort of object was lashed to the rail of Iphitus's chariot. The young man's squint was altogether too piercing, and the wrinkles crowding the corners of his eyes were far too knowing for his age; not a gaze but an arrow --- the kind of arrow you see already lodged in yourself, see it for the first and last time...

An archer.

And there on the chariot was a bow --- heavy, tightly strung, considerably larger than the ordinary sort, of which Autolycus had seen plenty in his lifetime.

"So you're the son of Eurytus the Archer..." Autolycus muttered, shaking his head thoughtfully. "No, young man, I've never seen your father, but I've heard of him more than once --- though back then he wasn't yet a basileus, when I happened to hear of him. What made your father let you go off alone, then, to Thebes?"

"Why alone?" Iphitus was genuinely surprised, wrinkling his youthfully smooth brow in a comical way. "Not alone at all... it's just that father went straight to Amphitryon's house with his retinue, while I came here to meet the local teachers. You understand, the teachers here include Castor the Dioscurus, and Autolycus Hermesid, and..."

Iphitus broke off, apparently realizing who his interlocutor might turn out to be.

"And you... are you Castor? Or Amphitryon himself?"

"Then why not Autolycus?" Autolycus inquired, amused.

"Yes, right!" Iphitus of Oechalia winked at the wrestler --- as if to say, you can't fool us! "As if! Autolycus, now, he's a famous trickster, sharp as they come, but you... you've got such an honest face on you!"

Not even when the brothers Alcides and Iphicles had been trampling poor Polyctor and his cronies had Autolycus laughed so hard.
