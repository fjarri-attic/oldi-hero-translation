#### 7

All day long he kept meeting exhausted soldiers trudging away --- alone, in twos, in threes...

Now and then he stopped and questioned them, biting his lips in impotent fury.

Everything was confirmed.

The mad Molionid fighters, acting together as a single creature; the Olympian gods on the side of Pylos, Eleia, and Sparta...

"I'm telling you --- it was Poseidon!"

"What Poseidon, on dry land and without a trident?! It was Hades, the Lord of the Shades..."

"Zeus, maybe?"

"Oh, come off it! Zeus without his thunderbolts?! And Zeus would never go against his beloved son! I'm telling you --- Poseidon!.."

Heracles's army was retreating. Never mind that in the battle the great hero had wounded either Hera, or Ares, or Hades himself --- it changed nothing. Except, perhaps, that it kept the retreat from turning into a headlong rout.

The soldiers had no wish to fight gods and monsters. Mercenaries, they wanted to kill men, and then plunder rich Eleia, and Pylos and Sparta along with it, if they got the chance.

Iolaus listened to them, bit his lips, and lashed the horses.

He had to make it in time.

Toward evening Iolaus overtook a detachment of well-armed warriors hurrying in the same direction as he was. In the thickening dusk he did not recognize them at first as the Tirynthian veterans and Telamon's Myrmidons --- not until he saw a familiar broad-shouldered figure up ahead.

"Iolaus!" Telamon cried, genuinely delighted. "Where have you been hiding, driver?! As soon as I heard what was going on here, I crossed straight over from Salamis to the mainland, gathered my lads --- and came here! And look, I met up with reinforcements from Tiryns... Shall we make camp?"

"No. We keep going. I think we'll get there by morning."

"So do I," Telamon nodded in agreement. "Forward!"

They intercepted the fleeing Arcadians an hour before dawn. It was from them that Iolaus and Telamon learned that Augeas's troops, led by the Molionids, had burst into Heracles's camp while it was still dark --- even though a temporary truce had been declared just before, at their own initiative --- and wrought bloody slaughter.

One of the twins had stayed behind with the Arcadians to cover the withdrawal, while the other led the surviving soldiers away to the small town of Pheneus, which was only a stone's throw from here.

Cepheus of Arcadia had fallen in battle along with his sons (one more oar on the phantom *Argo* left without a rower), and the brother who had covered the retreat was gravely wounded.

As for the Arcadians, they had been scattered and put to flight.

Helios, growing steadily hotter, was already climbing toward the zenith when Iolaus's chariot, drawn by two horses ridden into the ground, rolled into the central square of Pheneus.

Iolaus knew at once that he was too late.
