#### 2

The onlookers had long since scattered in every direction, driving before them a murky wave of rumor, gossip, and idle talk, by the time Amphitryon was finally let into the gynaikeion.

He stopped by the kline where a pale, exhausted Alkmene lay propped against the pillows, meant to... he never did remember what he'd meant to do, staring instead at the two bundles lying beside his wife.

Two.

Two bundles.

"Twins," Nausikaa beamed, and after her, every woman in the gynaikeion broke into smiles as well. "Twins, hero! What a man you are---equal to Zeus himself! Don't just stand there---go look at the children, kiss your wife..."

Amphitryon, his legs numb beneath him, obediently went around the kline, kissed the cheek of quiet Alkmene, somehow seeming a stranger to him---and felt his lips, against his will, spreading into a perfectly foolish, madly happy smile.

Two utterly identical little faces---cross, browless, barely peeking out of their swaddling---scrunched up at the same instant, and a two-voiced whimper filled the gynaikeion.

"Alkeides and... Iphikles," Amphitryon thought aloud. "Yes, that's what I'll call you: Alkeides and Iphikles. And the gods... let the gods sort it out themselves. Isn't that right, children? You and I will sort it out, and the gods can manage on their own..."

"Gone quite dizzy with joy," Nausikaa grumbled with mock irritation. "Talking all sorts of nonsense... Better tell someone to run into the city and shout that you've had twins!"

"They'll do without," Amphitryon cut in. "We're in no hurry---they'll find out by tomorrow regardless, so let's not go tempting fate. And here's what..."

He looked at the twins once more and pointed at random to the one lying on Alkmene's left.

"This one takes after me," Amphitryon declared confidently. "By heaven, the spitting image!"

"And this one?" Nausikaa asked, pointing to the other.

"And this one---after the Thunderer!"

And he barely kept himself from bursting out laughing at the sight of the nannies nodding gravely.

Amphitryon had now entirely forgotten strange Galinthias, daughter of Proitos, as if she had never existed at all; nor did he know that back home, in gold-rich Mycenae---from which Amphitryon had been driven out by his own uncle Sthenelos for killing another uncle of his (and father-in-law besides), wanax[\*](#fn-17){.noteref} Elektryon---to power-hungry Sthenelos and Nikippe, daughter of the cunning Pelops[\*](#fn-18){.noteref} and granddaughter of the gods-cursed Tantalos, just yesterday evening a sickly, premature boy had been born.

The first son, after two daughters.

The boy would be named Eurystheus, and, strange as it might seem, he would survive---which would strip Amphitryon and his descendants of any hope of one day ruling in Mycenae, their homeland.

No, Amphitryon knew none of this---and even had he learned of Eurystheus's birth, it would not have troubled his spirit, for he was not, in truth, inclined toward ruling cities. He would have laughed, poured dark Chian wine into a cup, and drained it to the health of every child born in those days.

And he would have been greatly astonished had some seer told him that, more than half a century hence, his wife Alkmene would be presented with the gray head of that very boy named Eurystheus---and that Alkmene would gouge the dead eyes from that horribly grinning head with her weaver's shuttle.

Greatly astonished---and would not have believed it.

But he would have been wrong.
