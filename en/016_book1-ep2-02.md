#### 2

The onlookers had long since scattered in every direction, driving before them a murky wave of rumor, gossip, and idle talk, by the time Amphitryon was finally let into the gynaeceum.

He stopped by the kline where a pale, exhausted Alcmene lay propped against the pillows, meant to... he never did remember what he'd meant to do, staring instead at the two bundles lying beside his wife.

Two.

Two bundles.

"Twins," Nausicaa beamed, and after her, every woman in the gynaeceum broke into smiles as well. "Twins, hero! Aren't you the man --- equal to Zeus himself! Don't just stand there --- go look at the children, kiss your wife..."

Amphitryon, his legs numb beneath him, obediently went around the kline, kissed the cheek of a quiet, strangely unfamiliar Alcmene --- and felt his lips, against his will, spreading into a perfectly foolish, madly happy smile.

Two utterly identical little faces --- cross, browless, barely peeking out of their swaddling --- scrunched up at the same instant, and a two-voiced whimper filled the gynaeceum.

"Alcides and... Iphicles," Amphitryon thought aloud. "Yes, that's what I'll call you: Alcides and Iphicles. And the gods... let the gods sort it out themselves. Isn't that right, children? You and I will sort it out, and the gods can manage on their own..."

"Gone quite dizzy with joy," Nausicaa grumbled with mock irritation. "Talking all sorts of nonsense... Better tell someone to run into the city and shout that you've had twins!"

"They'll do without," Amphitryon cut in. "We're in no hurry --- they'll find out by tomorrow regardless, so let's not go tempting fate. And here's what..."

He looked at the twins once more and pointed at random to the one lying on Alcmene's left.

"This one takes after me," Amphitryon declared confidently. "By heaven, the spitting image!"

"And this one?" Nausicaa asked, pointing to the other.

"And this one --- after the Thunderer!"

And he barely kept himself from bursting out laughing at the sight of the nannies nodding gravely.

Amphitryon had now entirely forgotten strange Galinthias, daughter of Proetus, as if she had never existed at all; nor did he know that back home, in gold-rich Mycenae --- from which Amphitryon had been driven out by his own uncle Sthenelus for killing another uncle of his (and father-in-law besides), wanax[^17] Electryon --- to power-hungry Sthenelus and Nicippe, daughter of the cunning Pelops[^18] and granddaughter of the gods-cursed Tantalus, just yesterday evening a sickly, premature boy had been born.

[^17]: Wanax (wanaka) --- king. (Mycenae was ruled by kings of higher rank and social standing than basileis.)

[^18]: Pelops --- son of Tantalus, killed by his own father (Tantalus, having prepared a meal from Pelops's flesh, served it to the Olympians) and restored to life by Hermes at Zeus's command. Pelops later treacherously hurled Myrtilus the charioteer, son of Hermes, from a cliff --- thereby bringing a curse down upon his entire line. Among the Pelopids there followed bloody feuds between brothers, matricides, and the like.

The first son, after two daughters.

The boy would be named Eurystheus, and, strange as it might seem, he would survive --- which would strip Amphitryon and his descendants of any hope of one day ruling in Mycenae, their homeland.

No, Amphitryon knew none of this --- and even had he learned of Eurystheus's birth, it would not have troubled his spirit, for he was not, in truth, inclined toward ruling cities. He would have laughed, poured dark Chian wine into a cup, and drained it to the health of every child born in those days.

And he would have been greatly astonished had some seer told him that, more than half a century hence, his wife Alcmene would be brought the gray head of this very boy named Eurystheus --- and that Alcmene would gouge out the dead eyes of that horribly grinning head with her weaver's shuttle.

Greatly astonished --- and would not have believed it.

But he would have been wrong.
