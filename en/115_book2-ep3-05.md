#### 5

He woke at dawn, snatched a quick bite---and by the time the pale-pink, not-quite-awake disc of Helios had clambered out from behind the mountains, the hooves of Iolaos's horses were already drumming across the Isthmos, every orgyia bringing the former lawagetas closer to the Peloponnese.

Not far from Corinth Iolaos once again steered the team into the maw of yet another Dromos that happened to lie along his way---and shot out onto a new road like the demon Taraxippos[\*](#fn-79){.noteref}, raising clouds of dust and very nearly plowing into a family of scrawny acacias.

Soon the cobbled approach to white-walled Argos came into view, and Iolaos put his horses to a walk toward the nearest gate, hoping to pick up the latest news in the city.

But the Argives couldn't have cared less about either Herakles or Elis---they were busy preparing their own campaign, against seven-gated Thebes. Why Thebes in particular, how the city had offended them or, on the contrary, caught their fancy, remained a mystery to Iolaos.

He did not go to see the city's basileus, Amphiaraos the Soothsayer---a former Argonaut and a former ally of Herakles. He had no wish to be the skeleton at someone else's feast. Especially since Amphiaraos the Soothsayer flatly refused to march on Thebes, while his cousin Adrastos, together with a whole gang of Achaian rowdies, was busily prodding the Argive basileus along.

"He's a seer, isn't he!" they whispered in the streets. "Bet he smells trouble coming!..."

As always, a woman was at the bottom of it---Eriphyle, the basileus's wife (the half-sister of the warlike Adrastos, and the one person Amphiaraos had once sworn to obey in everything), had given her husband her blessing to go to war, and now the seer basileus, unable to break his oath, had locked himself in the palace and was drinking heavily, cursing his wife, himself, Adrastos, Thebes, and everything else under the sun.

"Bought her off, the stupid woman!" they whispered in the streets. "Bought her off with the Necklace of Harmonia[\*](#fn-80){.noteref}!..."

"Adrastos promised it to Eriphyle, so she'd talk her husband into the campaign!"

"Adrastos, my foot!... It's just what's written in a man's fate..."

"Cursed necklace... cursed campaign," muttered the old women, making warding gestures.

[Looks like another bench on the *Argo* will soon stand empty,]{.thoughts} thought Iolaos as he left Argos.

Right at the city's gate a snatch of an argument reached him:

"...of all mortals!"

"You're lying! Everybody knows the strongest of mortals is the great Herakles, a distant kinsman of mine!"

"Ha! My goat's your kinsman, and not a distant one, either, but a close one! I'm telling you---the Molionids are the strongest now! Because they're *conjoined*! Get it?! There's two of them, and there's no way to part them..."

"You're a conjoined goat yourself! Big deal, the Molionids!... And named after their mother, not their father, to boot!"

"Yeah? Well, your mother..."

Iolaos did not catch the end of the conversation---he was hurrying on.

But the strange nickname lodged in his head like a splinter: "the Molionids." He had heard it before---and he knew whom it meant. The first misbegotten children of Phlegra, the Molionid brothers, conjoined twin idiots, sons of Molione, Augeias's sister, and of Three-Bodied Geryon. It's clear why they were named after their mother---Augeias will never in his life admit who their father really is. And no one will pay any attention at all to Molione, who has lost her mind. So people make guesses, trying one father after another on for size: Poseidon, Helios, Augeias's brother Aktor, Augeias himself...

People say all sorts of things. But few know the truth. And those who do mostly keep quiet.

The Molionid brothers. Augeias's allies. Kteatos and... Eurytos.

A coincidence?

After all, the name "Eurytos" is not all that rare.

The finger in the sky swayed, mocking him.

Iolaos was driving his horses into the ground, racing west.

He had to make it in time.
