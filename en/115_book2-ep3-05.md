#### 5

He woke at dawn, ate a hasty bite --- and by the time the tender-pink, not-quite-awake disc of Helios had clambered out from behind the mountains, the hooves of Iolaus's horses were already drumming across the Isthmos, every orgyia bringing the former lawagetas closer to the Peloponnese.

Not far from Corinth Iolaus once again steered the team into the maw of yet another Dromos that happened to lie along his way --- and shot out onto a new road like the demon Taraxippos[^79], raising clouds of dust and very nearly plowing into a family of puny acacias.

[^79]: Literally "horse-frightener"; a demon from among Hades's subjects.

Soon the cobbled approach to white-walled Argos came into view, and Iolaus put his horses to a walk toward the nearest gate, hoping to pick up the latest news in the city.

But the Argives couldn't have cared less about either Heracles or Eleia --- they were themselves preparing a campaign against seven-gated Thebes. Why Thebes in particular, how the city had offended them or, on the contrary, caught their fancy, remained a riddle to Iolaus.

He did not go to see the city's basileus, Amphiaraus the Soothsayer --- a former Argonaut and a former ally of Heracles. He had no wish to be the hangover at someone else's feast. All the more so as Amphiaraus the Soothsayer flatly refused to march on Thebes, while his cousin Adrastus, together with a whole gang of Achaean rowdies, was busily shoving the Argive basileus in the back.

"The seer!" they whispered in the streets. "Bet he smells trouble coming!.."

As always, a woman was at the bottom of it --- Eriphyle, the basileus's wife (the half-sister of the warlike Adrastus, and the one person Amphiaraus had once sworn to obey in everything), had blessed her husband to go to war, and now the seer basileus, unable to break his oath, had locked himself in the palace and was drinking himself senseless, cursing his wife, himself, Adrastus, Thebes, and everything else under the sun.

"Bought her off, the silly cow!" they whispered in the streets. "Bought her off with the Necklace of Harmonia[^80]!.."

[^80]: Harmonia was the wife of Cadmus, the founder of Thebes; her wedding necklace (a gift from Aphrodite) was a source of misfortune for its owner.

"Adrastus promised it to Eriphyle, so she'd talk her husband into the campaign!"

"Adrastus yourself!.. It's just that what's written in one's fate..."

"Cursed necklace... cursed campaign," muttered the old women, making warding gestures.

"Looks like another bench on the *Argo* will soon stand empty," thought Iolaus as he left Argos.

Right at the city's gate a scrap of an argument reached him:

"...of all mortals!"

"You're lying! Everybody knows the strongest of mortals is the great Heracles, a distant kinsman of mine!"

"Ha! My goat's your kinsman, and not a distant one, either, a close one! I'm telling you --- the Molionids are the strongest now! Because they're *conjoined*! Get it?! There's two of them, and there's no way to part them..."

"You're a conjoined goat yourself! Big deal, the Molionids!.. And named after the mother, not the father, to boot!"

"And your mother too..."

Iolaus did not catch the end of the conversation --- he was hurrying on.

But the strange nickname lodged in his head like a splinter: "the Molionids." He had heard it before --- and he knew whom it meant. The first misbegotten children of Phlegrae, the Molionid brothers, conjoined twin idiots, sons of Molione, Augeas's sister, and of the Three-Bodied Geryon. It was clear why they had been named after their mother --- Augeas will never in his life admit who their father really is. And no one will pay any attention at all to Molione, who has lost her mind. So people make guesses, trying on Poseidon, Helios, Augeas's brother Actor, Augeas himself as the father...

People say all sorts of things. Only a few know the truth. And whoever knows keeps quiet all the more.

The Molionid brothers. Augeas's allies. Cteatus and... Eurytus.

A coincidence?

After all, the name "Eurytus" is not all that rare.

The finger in the sky swayed, mocking him.

Iolaus was killing his horses, racing west.

He had to make it in time.
