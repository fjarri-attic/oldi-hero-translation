#### 9

"...a-hoy, I see the mountains, see the peaks..."

The song rasped, faded, choked on hooting gurgles, and then, spiteful and stubborn, broke through again:

"...of Cithaeron, a-hoy, cut the throat..."

And immediately --- from afar, sharp as the crack of a whip:

"Dad, stop the chariot!"

But the chariot had already stopped on its own, a mere three or four orgyiai[^35] short of the nearest herma.

[^35]: A unit of length, around two meters.

"What's the matter, daughter?" A middle-aged man, tanned black by the sun, turned his head --- bald well beyond his years --- trying to make out where the old robber's song had just come from, while at the same time reining in the snorting horses.

Behind the chariot, in a cloud of slowly settling dust, two soldiers past their youth shifted from foot to foot, clearly escorting the father and daughter. Their grim, weary look spoke of a long road already behind them, and also of the fact that riding in a chariot was a good deal better than walking.

"They're killing someone over there, dad!" A black-haired adolescent stamped a long, coltish leg --- clad in a short exomis that bared the left shoulder and part of a chest barely budding, immature, almost boyish.

Even the woven belt was tied the way men wore it, at the waist.

To the right of the road, beyond a lushly blooming meadow, a rather sizable crowd could be seen, made up of ragged folk dressed in greasy hides (despite the heat) --- locals, beyond any doubt.

Farther still, the edge of a forest began.

The crowd had gathered around something in sullen silence... it was hard to make out just what, but through the gaps between bodies one could glimpse the flanks of a roughly hewn stone and the quite filthy arms and legs of some creature sprawled backward across it.

The song --- if the sounds being produced deserved to be called a song --- belonged to none other than the creature on the stone.

"A petty brigand," the bald charioteer smirked. "A sheep-thief... he'll fall silent soon enough. He'll finish the song in Hades!"

"Are they killing him, dad?! Are they?!"

"Not killing --- sacrificing," the reasonable parent began, but the girl (or young woman?) apparently saw no great difference and, ignoring her father's shout, had already jumped down to the ground.

"Order them to stop, dad!"

The soldiers exchanged glances and smirked --- they had long known that the Megarian telestas[^36] Alcathous, or Alcathous the Bald, obeyed exactly two people in this world: the basileus of Megara, his father-in-law, who was named Megareus after the city, and his thirteen-year-old daughter, Automedusa.

[^36]: A nobleman, close to the ruler.

"And why exactly should I get involved?..." Alcathous didn't finish.

It turned out his headstrong daughter was already racing across the meadow toward the people gathered by the stone.

"Stop! Where do you think you're going?! Automedusa, stop! Do you hear me?!"

As if --- all of Alcathous's calls were utterly in vain.

"Follow me!" the man snapped, having given up on calling the girl back with words, and jumped down from the chariot, running through the meadow after long-legged Automedusa, his guards close behind.

By then she had already burst into the crowd of villagers, wrinkling her nose at their smell, and reached the very altar, beside which stood the local priest with a curved knife in his hand.

No, actually, this was not a priest (despite the tattered cloak, the pride of many generations), but rather an elder; or not even that --- an elder ought to be stately, white-bearded, full of dignity, while this sacrificer's beard was some murky, indeterminate thing, stateliness was entirely absent, and as for dignity...

A headman.

A village headman --- and that at best.

"A-hoy, cut the throat, a-hoy, tear the veins!..."

The headman had already raised the blade over both song and sacrifice when someone's tenacious fingers, nails razor-sharp, dug into his hand, and a piercing shriek rang out right in his ear:

"Don't you dare! Don't you dare kill him!"

Scared half to death, the headman dropped the knife and spun around, expecting to find some deity beside him, come to disrupt a rite it found unwelcome --- but instead saw an unfamiliar adolescent standing before him.

The adolescent plainly suffered from nothing divine --- except perhaps nerve.

Seized by righteous fury, the headman said nothing, only landed the little wretch a resounding slap, which sent the pup rolling through the grass with a squeal, crying out for someone to help.

And help was not long in coming. Someone's strong hand seized the headman by the collar, while someone's even stronger knee kicked him just below the back, so that the stout headman went flying headfirst, no less gracefully than the impudent adolescent had just before him.

"Don't you dare!" --- the words were the same, but the voice was different: stern, used to command. "Don't you dare raise a hand against my daughter!"

To the cult servant's credit, it must be said that the thought of a divine origin for the man shouting and kicking never so much as crossed his mind.

"What daughter?" the headman bellowed, struggling to his feet. "And who are you, anyway?! And why are you and your impudent... daughter interrupting the rite of sacrifice to Great Pan?!"

The crowd closed in around Alcathous and his two soldiers, muttering with ill intent.

"I am Alcathous, telestas of Megara," the bald man said, groping for the hilt of the sword hanging at his belt. "And you---"

"And you get out, back to your Megara!" the headman cut him off, seething with anger and feeling the support of his fellow villagers. "And pray to Pan that he lets you make it home safely! Blasphemer!"

Taken aback, Alcathous was about to give the overreaching headman a fitting rebuke, but then the unforeseen happened. The victim, forgotten by everyone, had by then managed to use the dropped knife to free himself of his bonds --- and the robber, with a wild whoop, bolted at full speed for the edge of the forest.

The villagers confined themselves to loud shouting --- no one dared chase after the freed sheep-thief running for his life, especially since they had tied him up while he slept off a monumental drinking bout.

The headman's face, never much to look at to begin with, now resembled the goat-face of the Chimera.

Alcathous still had time to notice the headman making some sort of signal, and yanked his sword from its scabbard --- but sweaty bodies in stinking hides piled onto the telestas; someone cried out, taking an elbow to the ribs, another began snuffling through a broken nose, the sword flashed above the crowd... and a blow from a heavy club to his bald head sent Alcathous tumbling into the dark of unconsciousness.

Seeing Alcathous down, the guards put up little resistance and passed out with something like relief. After which the captive men were bound securely and laid off to one side under the watch of local youths, two of whom proudly clutched the spears taken from the soldiers.

Automedusa, meanwhile, was first hobbled and gagged, then solemnly laid upon the altar; the crowd settled back into its earlier state, and now the beaming headman once again called upon the good god Pan to protect their herds and put an end to the plague.

"To you, O Pan, son of Hermes, Great and Merciful, we bring this sacrifice," the headman intoned, raising over his head the knife left behind by the fleeing robber, "and we beg for---"

No, today was decidedly not his day.

A strange bird gave a short whistle through the air --- and the headman, dropping the ill-fated knife for the second time that day, cried out and clutched his bloodied right hand, pierced clean through by a long arrow with a narrow bronze beak.

The crowd turned as one many-bodied creature, ready to tear, trample, and destroy --- and froze on the spot, eyes bulging, mouths gaping; some even began dropping to their knees.

The latter were the quickest-witted, for what else is one supposed to do at a Manifestation?

A Manifestation of the gods.

...First to bear down on the poor villagers, not touching the ground, came a furious Hermes --- his cloak with its bordered edge thrashing in the wind, rainbow arcs fluttering at the heels of his famous sandals, the caduceus rod leveled straight at the headman and the girl stretched out on the altar.

Right behind the Cyllenian, hooves thundering deafeningly, galloped an enormous bay centaur, bearing in his mighty arms some young --- but evidently very important --- god, while another young god (or was it the same one?.. This is madness!) sat astride the centaur's back, brandishing a heavy, knotted club.

Clinging to the centaur's tail, curly-haired Dionysus hurried along on unsteady legs, thyrsus in hand; keeping pace with Dionysus was Great and Merciful Pan (though at that particular moment truly Great, but by no means merciful), his hooves pounding no worse than the centaur's.

And behind them all poured a whole horde of satyrs and bacchantes, above whom towered the gigantic figure of sun-faced Apollo, already nocking another arrow to his bowstring.

More and more gods kept arriving, appearing in the middle of the meadow straight out of air that trembled with fear; and the villagers realized they no longer had it in them to feel awe or terror.

It turned out they did --- when one of the young gods swept down off the horse-man's back like a whirlwind and began clubbing anyone within reach, anywhere it landed!

The god's wrath was so great, and the villagers' legs so quick-witted, that very soon the only original participants left by the altar were the wounded headman, plainly unable to move (from fear, not from the wound!), Alcathous and his soldiers (still unconscious), and Automedusa on the stone, staring wide-eyed at this unheard-of invasion.

The young god hurled his club at the groaning headman, walked up to the altar, picked up the knife, examined it with a sour grimace, swung his arm (Automedusa squeezed her eyes shut in terror) and quickly cut through the ropes binding the victim's legs.

"Right, up you get, what are you lying around for?" the young god muttered, taking Automedusa for a boy just like everyone before him. "And spit out that gag, enough drooling on it..."

"Thank you," the girl answered, off the mark, first opening her eyes and freeing her mouth of the gag. "Are you a god?"

"Nah," her rescuer shook his head indifferently. "That's a god," and he pointed over his shoulder at Hermeias, hovering in the air nearby. "And that's a god. The one with the horns, I mean..."

This last was about Pan.

"And I'm... a hero, that's what I am. There."

"Yeah, we're heroes all right!" another youth, borne by the centaur, slurred behind him, and Automedusa realized with horror that the speaker was dead drunk.

"And what's your name, hero?"

"Iphicles," Iphicles muttered, having forgotten this time who he was supposed to call himself in situations like this.

"And I'm Alc--- hic!.. Alcides!" the second one announced, having likewise clean forgotten their agreement. "I'm his brother! Or no, he's my brother! There, that's right now..."

Automedusa did not dignify Alcides with any attention, intent on peppering Iphicles with questions, but then Hermeias intervened.

"You'd best be on your way, girl," he advised without ceremony, unerringly guessing the sex of the failed sacrifice. "There lie your companions --- go see how they're doing instead! Go on, go on!"

Only then remembering her father, Automedusa gave a little yelp, clapped a hand over her mouth, and dashed off toward Alcathous and the two soldiers, who still hadn't come to.

"So it's a girl?" Iphicles marveled.

"Looks like we'll have to send you off to study with the bassarids," the Trickster sighed. "They'll teach you how a boy differs from a girl, all right..."

Iphitus the archer, meanwhile, hopped down off the shoulders of the satyrs who had been carrying him (fearing he'd be late, he had shot straight from that moving pedestal), grumbled, "They've jabbed my whole backside raw with their horns!" --- and noted with some surprise that he had already grown quite comfortable in this noisy, quasi-divine company.

Grim-faced Pan walked over to the headman, snapped off the arrowhead, and with a single yank pulled out the shaft lodged in his hand.

The headman opened his mouth but was afraid to scream.

"It'll heal before you die," Pan informed him unkindly, giving the wound a brief glance.

Then the woodland god stepped over to Hermeias, and the Trickster began explaining something to him at length, while Pan listened, now and then nodding his heavy horned head in agreement.

Finishing the conversation, Hermeias politely but firmly dragged everyone away. Indeed, there was no point in lingering any longer, and the newly opened Dromos welcomed the crowd graciously, letting it pass back onto Cithaeron.

Everyone except Pan.

The dazedly blinking headman and Automedusa, who had torn herself away from her senseless father for a moment, watched the gods vanish into the shimmering funnel; both saw how, at the last instant, the hero named Iphicles, seated on the centaur's back, turned and let his glance slide by... Automedusa hoped it had slid over her, over her angular little figure, while the headman feared in earnest that the hero wanted to fix his face in memory --- the luckless priest that he was.

But, be that as it may, the Dromos took the hero into itself along with his drunkard of a brother and the bay centaur; and the meadow emptied.

"O Great Pan!" the headman wailed, coming to his senses and staring devotedly at the goat-legged, goat-horned deity. "We have a plague!.. We wanted to offer you a sacrifice..."

"A plague, you say!" the deity mimicked him sullenly. "Your mugs are enough of a plague without one! Fine, then, for the sheep's sake... what did the sheep ever do wrong?"

"And pigs," the headman put in hastily.

"Pigs, right enough," Pan responded cryptically. "So, here's how it is: I'll take away the plague, you offer sacrifices, only not human ones! That kind of sacrifice doesn't please me. Understood?"

"Understood!" the headman nodded happily. "Sheep please you, pigs please you" ("Fruits please me too," Pan added), "and people don't please you! How could I not understand?! My favorite pig --- straight to Great Pan's altar!.."

"Good lad... now, whatever you saw today --- forget it. Nothing happened. And explain that to your people too. There was a sign, that's all. And if anyone starts wagging their tongue, a plague will be the least of your troubles! I'll set that one on you too --- the one who was chasing you all with his club..."

"No need, merciful one!" the headman wailed, and abundant old man's tears streamed down his cheeks. "We'll keep quiet, we'll pray, just not the one... with the club!"

"That's more like it... make sure you tell your people. The plague will stop tomorrow."

Pan grunted and trudged off.
