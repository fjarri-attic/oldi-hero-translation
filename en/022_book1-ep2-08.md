#### 8

When the rattling clatter of hooves died away, and the chariots of Amphitryon and his escort had vanished around the bend in the road, but the dust they had raised had not yet had time to settle --- a man in a ragged, hooded chlamys walked into the house like its master, climbed onto the terrace, threw off his rags, and brazenly sat down in the chair from which Amphitryon, now departed, had risen not long before.

He turned out to be a dark-haired youth with a hooked, aristocratic nose, dressed in a dandyish chiton belted with an artfully embroidered girdle, and reddish-brown sandals of soft leather, which the youth had been too lazy to take off. Taking a sip from the half-finished cup, the uninvited guest smiled, though his eyes remained serious.

No youth has eyes like that --- eyes somehow resembling those of Autolycus, who had risen halfway from the chair beside him.

"Rejoice[^22], Father."

[^22]: Rejoice (Greek chaire) --- the customary greeting among the Hellenes.

It was not the youth who said this.

It was Autolycus.

"Hello, sonny," the youth answered carelessly. "How's the life, how's the mood? They say you're off to Thebes?"

"I agreed, Father. I saw you --- and I agreed."

"You always were quick to understand. Amphitryon, by the way, is no fool... and he's right --- both of them need to be trained. Equally. Just in case."

"Trained --- for what?"

"For everything. People need a hero. And your gramps up there on Olympus needs a hero too. Anywhere you spit --- everyone needs a hero..."

And with excellent aim, the youth flung a gnawed bird's leg at the helmet hanging on the wall --- Autolycus's famous leather helmet, studded with boar's tusks.

...Meanwhile Amphitryon's chariots raised their unhurried dust along the winding road away from Argos, and on both sides of the road rose hills of virgin green. Here and there, in the distance, grazing herds could be glimpsed, resembling the snowcaps of mountains. It was early autumn, the sun was warm, and nothing disturbed the peace of the tranquil landscape spread out all around.

The horses walked slowly --- and why hurry, after all? --- and just as unhurriedly flowed the thoughts of Amphitryon, who had for a time entrusted the reins to his charioteer.

His thoughts, however, were slow, but by no means as peaceful as the landscape around him.

In the end Autolycus had agreed --- and that was good. He would teach the growing twins not only the art of wrestling, but would surely pass on to them no small share of his famous cunning, which Autolycus had inherited from his father, Hermes Psychopomp, that is, the Soul-Guide.

And wrestling without cunning is like a spear without a point.

Castor the Dioscurus had also agreed to teach the brothers combat in full armor --- and that, too, was good. Amphitryon would not, without extreme need, fight Castor, whether with spears or with swords. Castor the Laconian, brother of indomitable Polydeuces, was strong, strong and merciless. There was only one trouble --- he was proud beyond measure. He fancied himself the best charioteer in the Peloponnese --- and not only in the Peloponnese, at that. Well, let him indulge his vanity...

Amphitryon smirked complacently despite himself, stroking his beard.

No, the charioteer's art he would teach his sons himself. Castor might take offense, true... Well, to Tartarus with him! Just so long as he came to Thebes as promised --- once there, they'd talk him round, keep him. And who knows, his brother Polydeuces, the fist fighter, might turn up too --- unable to help himself, he'd start by retraining them, then move on to showing off something new, and then (the Moirai working in mysterious ways) get stuck at the palaestra for a year or two...

He would need teachers of every kind. And not warriors only. As it happened, just before Amphitryon's departure, Linus, brother of the divine Orpheus, had turned up in Thebes. It seemed he meant to settle there... Praise be to Apollo Musagetes[^23], if so --- one could not find a better kitharode tutor!

[^23]: Musagetes --- Leader of the Muses, one of Apollo's epithets.

The future teachers were young, young and hot-blooded. Linus was thirty-one, the most seasoned of them; Autolycus was nearly thirty; Castor had only just turned twenty-five. Older men could have been found --- they could, but it wouldn't do. Amphitryon had found exactly the ones he wanted to find. Castor was stubborn and fierce, Autolycus cunning and tireless, Linus still unable to forgive Orpheus his talent --- they would teach harshly, demand much of the children, show no mercy for their tender age, grant no leniency.

Then, Olympian, we'll see who laughs last! Everyone knows that Alcides is your son; and only you and I, dread Dias, Zeus the Father, Brontes the Thunder-Wielder, know the truth. We know it, and both of us will keep silent. I --- because the lives of my children and my wife are dear to me (and my own is not indifferent to me either). You --- because your honor and your manly dignity are dear to you. Yes, I will hold my tongue, Olympian, I will swallow every word I would like to hurl in your face; Elphistic has already paid for my long tongue with a hook driven into the back of his own skull --- enough! I will hold my tongue. I will not smirk slyly in your temples.

But you and I will know the truth, Olympian, night thief!

To Danae you came as a golden rain, to Europa --- as a bull, but to Alcmene you came as me --- which means my shape suited you well! Fit at the shoulder, fit in height, fit to the measure... was it not too tight a fit, Thunderer?

And you will flinch, seeing an earthly man, a mortal, son of a mortal, doing what a half-god, son of great Zeus, was meant to do!

And to everyone but the two of us, he will indeed be a half-god...

Everything will come to pass, everything will happen just as you wanted... only you, Olympian, will have had nothing to do with it!

Isn't that so? Well, answer me --- strike with lightning, thunder from a clear sky!

You need a hero equal to the gods?

You shall have him.

And that will be the only revenge I, Amphitryon Perseid, can allow myself.
