#### 17

Iolaos burst into Trachis like a hurricane.

The servants and slaves did not even think of barring his way as this frenzied man, with the face of a demon broken loose from Erebos, swept through the galleries of the western wing of the house and forced his way, uninvited, into the women's quarters.

And if they did think of it, they prudently judged the thought not their brightest.

"In the name of the black pools of the Styx, woman, why did you send your husband off to this idiotic war?!" Iolaos snarled by way of greeting.

A dozen slave women, sitting at their weaving shuttles in the gynaeceum, jerked up their heads as if on command and stared at the newcomer in fright.

Deianeira rose unhurriedly, smoothing in passing her peplos, which fell in stately folds as it was, and smiled a welcome with a sensual mouth like a blood-drenched flower.

Strange as it might seem, bearing four sons---Hyllos, Ktesippos, Glenos, Onites---and a daughter, Makaria, had not spoiled her figure in the least. A tall, stately woman with large, somewhat sharp features, her embroidered girdle tied just beneath a bosom that had lost none of its firmness, her strong hands resting on the shuttle calmly and surely.

There was something of Athena Pallas about Deianeira.

And in his time Iolaos had not been surprised to learn that Herakles's young wife was quite handy with a light spear, and devoted no small amount of time to that art.

He could not stand such women.

Perhaps because they would have made perfectly decent men.

"Rejoice, nephew!" There was not a hint of offense or ill will in the deep, low voice, but a slight arch of her brow was enough for the quick-witted slave women to file out of the gynaeceum one after another.

Iolaos dropped heavily onto a bench that creaked beneath him and silently counted off the whole dozen Olympians, so as not to blurt out to his "auntie" in the heat of the moment what he thought of her.

"Was the road to us in Trachis a good one?"

Iolaos nodded and counted off the Olympians in reverse order.

"Do you really think, nephew, that I, a weak woman, could make god-equal Herakles go off on campaign against his will?"

"I do," Iolaos declared bluntly.

"And you're right to think so," Deianeira unexpectedly agreed, lowering herself with lazy grace into the chair opposite. "You always struck me as cleverer than you look... nephew. Now think about this: who am I here, in Trachis? A hanger-on, a chance Aetolian woman with no connections and no kin in these parts, tolerated only because she managed to hook an aging Herakles! There are twenty-odd years between him and me! If he ascends to Olympos tomorrow---what will become of me? What will become of my children?! Or will you take us into your house, nephew dear?.. No, you won't take us, you'll be afraid---my bed runs hot, and your wife is jealous!"

Iolaos suddenly grew calm.

And this calm, an icy, battle-ready calm, was not at all to his liking.

"And what follows from all that, aunt?" He smiled crookedly, and at the sight of that smile Deianeira, falling silent, felt that she had been too frank with this man, on whom she had designs of her own.

"What follows is that only material wealth can secure a worthy future for me and my children (Iolaos noted to himself that repeated '*my* children')! And the spoils Herakles takes when Oichalia falls will be anything but superfluous! Is it my fault that the gods sent me a famous but mad and no longer young husband, and now poor Deianeira has to think for two?! Well, tell me, Iolaos---is it my fault, is it?!"

Deianeira had flushed, her black eyes shone, her full bosom rose and fell in gusts---she was quite lovely at that moment, but a barely perceptible vein of calculation, pulsing somewhere in the depths of this mature, experienced woman, made Iolaos think that all of it was a skillful performance.

And that Herakles's wife was only waiting for him to reach for her.

He did not move from his seat.

On the contrary, crossing one leg over the other, he coolly studied her, recalling the gossip of the Thessalians: that Herakles, being a son of the Bull, slept with anything that moved (most likely he simply did not notice the difference between the women who climbed onto his kline); but that Herakles's wife was not to be outdone by her husband in the lists of Eros---though, unlike her spouse, she told men apart perfectly well, both clothed and unclothed.

*...me and my children...*

"...But this time you were wrong, much-wise Iolaos." The flush left Deianeira's cheeks, and the shine in her eyes slowly dimmed. "I didn't drive Herakles to march on Oichalia. He made up his mind to go to war himself. And he wouldn't even listen, neither to me nor to Keyx, when we tried to get it into his head that Eurytos of Oichalia had died long, long ago. 'I must kill Eurytos!' your stubborn uncle announced. 'He withheld my rightful winnings from me. He will die.' A month later Herakles and Keyx's men set out for Euboea. Well, I didn't object... and why would I?"

[Eurytos?!]{.thoughts} flashed through Iolaos's head. [Could it be... no, nonsense! I would have known... Herakles has simply mixed up the past with the present. And this mare didn't object, and Keyx didn't object either---small wonder! Why not plunder Oichalia, holding up Herakles's name like a shield?! A sacred cause!]{.thoughts}

There was a knock at the door of the gynaeceum.

"Come in!" Deianeira called.

At first Iolaos did not recognize the warrior who came in---a lanky, out-of-breath lout in a leather cuirass and a light helmet pushed back on his head.

"Iolie!" the lout bawled for the whole gynaeceum to hear and made to hug him. "Old pal! And here I was about to head from here to you, with glad tidings! Oh, this is great!"

Now it was impossible not to recognize Lichas---only he could bellow like that.

"Hold on, will you!" Iolaos squeezed Lichas in his embrace a little harder than was proper, and Lichas began gulping greedily for air and stopped shouting right in his ear. "Take it in order! Has Oichalia been taken?"

"Well, obviously!" Lichas wheezed happily, poking his bristly beard into Iolaos's face. "They opened the gates for us themselves!"

"And... Eurytos?"

"What about Eurytos? What's the matter, Iolie, have you gone soft in the head too? What Eurytos?! Honestly, it's like you've all ganged up..."

Iolaos sighed with relief and let go of Lichas.

"The spoils, the spoils---heaps of them!" rattled on the messenger, who had come to his senses. "Captive men, captive women... you won't believe it, Iolie---we picked up that would-be bride there! Iole! Herakles ordered her kept apart and not mistreated---so we keep her apart, we don't mistreat her, all proper... Hey, Iolie, where are you off to?"

"Home," Iolaos threw over his shoulder from the door. "Wait for me here, Lichas! I'll dash over to Phylake, warn my people---and straight back here! We'll ride to the coast together! Just you wait for me, lad! Do you hear?!"

"What Phylake, it's half a day to our camp from here, no more..." Lichas began, but Iolaos had already run out of the gynaeceum.

The messenger only spread his hands, turned to Deianeira, and set about answering her questions.

He had expected questions about Herakles's health or the size of the spoils taken---but what he had to talk about first and foremost was the would-be bride, Iole.

Talk at length.

In detail.

And with every word Deianeira grew gloomier and gloomier.
