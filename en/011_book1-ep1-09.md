#### 9

...Amphitryon took off his woolen pharos cloak, which by some miracle had not been lost in the wild ride, and threw it over his wife, huddled in the corner; for an instant his eyes and Alcmene's met, intertwined, became one, like the bodies of lovers on a night's kline --- and Amphitryon turned away slowly, hunching under an invisible weight.

Barely able to move his feet, as though each step cost him a year of his life, he approached the edge of the cliff and stopped, looking out at the sea. Wet locks of hair clung to his forehead in rings, his chiton was torn from the armpit down to the middle of his back, a wide scrape burned red on his thigh; he stood in the rain and looked down, to where, on the narrow strip of shore, on the wet shingle, lay the wreckage of his chariot, a stallion thrashed in agony with its legs broken, and the retreating sea uncertainly licked at the corpse of the second horse.

Behind Amphitryon's back a loud, hysterical neighing rang out --- apparently it was the bay mare, the cause of everything that had happened.

For some reason Amphitryon wanted to believe that the bay mare was to blame for everything.

He forced himself with all his strength to believe it --- and could not.

He stood and watched the waves, like blind bulls, dashing in every direction, now shattering against the rocks, now butting one another, while from the shaggy clouds hanging threateningly over the bay burst forth sheaves of blinding lightning, lashing the boiling sea... the sea alone... just the sea, and nothing more.

And somewhere far below, beneath his feet, a muffled rumble kept sounding, as though someone were trying to rock the earth loose, but someone stronger still held the roots of the earth in his hands, the way Amphitryon himself, not long ago, had held the reins of his chariot, and the earth, in spite of everything, remained motionless, groaning hoarsely with pain.

Or was that merely thunder rolling?

The sky lashed the sea in fury with whips of lightning and taut streams of downpour, and the sea snapped back reluctantly, like a bristling beast still growling and baring its teeth, yet already ready to drop to its belly and crawl away; in the rumble of the earth and the peals of thunder sounded words that men could not understand --- and between sky and sea stood an exhausted man in a torn chiton, and the elements were afraid to look him in the eye.

"Whoever you are," Amphitryon whispered, "whoever you are..."

And did not finish.

Instead, he turned his back on the sky and the sea, looked over his frightened, silent little army, gave his wife and the groaning Licymnius a crooked smile --- and began to sing... no, rather, to bellow across the whole coast:

"Ha-a-ai, a storm over the sea, ha-a-ai, hundred-headed Typhon... well then, children of Echidna! Ha-a-ai..."

"Ha-a-ai, a storm over the sea," the veterans took up with adoration in their voices, and even the badly battered Licymnius joined the common chorus. "Ha-a-ai, a storm over the world!.."

"That's better," Amphitryon muttered, touched the scrape on his thigh with a finger, winced, and strode off, away from the cliff.
