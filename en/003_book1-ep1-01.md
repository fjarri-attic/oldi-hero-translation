### First epeisodion

#### 1

...It happened two hours before dawn, in that accursed hour when light-winged Hypnos the Sleep, son of star-strewn Nyx, reigns supreme; when the sentries of seven-gated Thebes nod at their posts, starting awake again and again to glance about them --- while Hypnos laughs without a sound and splashes poppy infusion from palms cupped like a bowl into the faces of the negligent watchmen.

It happened two hours before dawn.

From afar, from the direction of the Cephissus valley, came a barely audible rumble --- broken, like the sound of stones sliding down the slopes of Cithaeron --- and it rushed on, drawing nearer and growing louder, gradually drowning out the chirring of cicadas, echoing among the crowns of the cypresses and setting startled birds to beat their wings in alarm.

"Hark at him, driving hard," grumbled Thelem the Nasal, senior watchman of the post by the southeastern gates of Thebes, and wrapped himself tighter in his woolen cloak.

"Who, uncle?" timidly asked the youth sitting beside Thelem --- little more than a boy --- blinking his long, girlish lashes.

"How in blazes should I know who?" Nasal answered sullenly, and the youth shivered, listening to the approaching rumble, then drew closer to himself the short spear with its leaf-shaped point.

Thelem glanced sidelong at the youth, snorted, scratched the caved-in bridge of his nose, and twisted his face into a grimace that could, with great generosity, be called encouraging.

"Don't be scared, silly," fatherly notes crept into Thelem's voice. "Grab for your spear every single time, and I'll have you thrown out of the watch. Better you learn to listen..."

"But what is there to listen to, uncle?" the youth shrugged, bewildered. "It rumbles and rumbles... just as if some monster were galloping on a hundred hooves."

"Prick up your ears!" Nasal barked at the foolish youngster. "What uncle am I to you?! To you, you nitwit, I am master senior watchman! Understood? Monsters he's seeing! I'll warm your hide for you in a moment!.. A hundred-hoofed wonder..."

"Leave the lad be, Nasal," the third sentry drawled lazily --- a stocky, bearded man in a leather cuirass sparsely studded with bronze plates. Until then the bearded man had sat leaning against the wall in silence, idly twirling a plucked stalk of grass between his fingers; now, bored of that occupation, he decided to join the conversation.

"And you, Philid, keep out of it when nobody's asked you!" Thelem snapped, striking his fist on his knee in vexation. "The boy's been on watch two months now --- high time he learned to tell a monster from a chariot! Ah, you'll spoil him rotten..."

"A chariot?" the youth raised his eyebrows in astonishment, arching them like a little roof. "Then... we ought to raise the alarm, uncle master senior watchman!"

"What for?"

"What do you mean, 'what for'? If it's a chariot, then it's... enemies!"

The bearded man burst out laughing and clamped the stalk between his strong teeth, while Thelem cuffed the youth lightly on the back of the head.

"Enemies... You knotty-headed blockhead! Enemies don't come riding up on a single chariot, and they don't drive like that down an unfamiliar road, at night besides! This is one of our own, someone local, who both knows the way and isn't holding the reins for the first time. Only who? What do you think, Philid?"

"I..." bearded Philid began, but just then the rumble grew very close, and Philid sprang nimbly to his feet without waiting for Nasal's order, and started climbing the ladder up onto the wall, which at that point was no higher than fifteen cubits.

He gained the wall only just in time.

A heavy war chariot, harnessed to a pair of lathered horses, hurtled out from around the bend in the road, catching and crushing a thorny maquis bush growing by the roadside --- and the driver, barely visible in the pre-dawn gloom, hauled on the reins with a powerful jerk, checking the horses' run and forcing the animals to a halt right at the gates.

"Open up, you sleepyheads!" the driver's low voice rolled like thunder across the countryside, and the youth glanced fearfully at Thelem, who rolled his mighty shoulders as if warming up before a fistfight. "Hey, you'll sleep through the whole city! Quick about it!"

"You'll wait till morning," Philid called down from the wall. "Orders of basileus[^6] Creon --- no one's to be let into the city before dawn. Clear?"

[^6]: The ruler of a city. An analogue would be a high king or a grand prince.

"What, don't you recognize me, Philid?"

The newcomer's voice no longer resembled thunder --- no, now it was more like the growl of a seasoned lion, sated for the moment but ready to grow hungry again at any minute.

Philid was still thinking it over, peering with a hand shading his brow, when Thelem had already bolted from his spot and set about unbarring the gates in a fluster.

"At once, my lord," Nasal fumbled with the bolt, which refused to give, and the youth watched the senior watchman in amazement, for he had never seen him like this before. "I'm already... already..."

The gate leaves shuddered and creaked --- and the chariot rolled slowly through the opening. Thelem, grown remarkably obliging, all but flung himself under the wheels, and at the last moment managed to catch the reins thrown to him.

"Welcome back, my lord!" Nasal bowed low, pressing a gnarled paw of a hand to his heart. "Ah, you've run the horses ragged... well, never mind, I'll walk them, rub them down, they'll be good as new! Don't you worry yourself, my lord..."

The arrival leapt lightly down from the chariot, his broad cloak billowing out, and clapped Nasal companionably on the shoulder --- so that the burly watchman staggered considerably, but held his footing, baring his teeth in a happy grin.

"Bring the chariot round to my house after your watch ends!" the night visitor ordered. "You'll get a pithos of wine and clothing for all three of you. Oh, and one more thing --- don't fret over your brother. He's alive, he'll be home by noon --- I've ordered Panopeus of Phocis to march for Thebes at dawn. We took a fine haul on Taphos, enough for everyone! And your brother's a good soldier --- men like him aren't passed over when the spoils are shared out. Well, farewell, watchman Thelem!"

And with a broad marching stride he strode off down the street leading toward the center of the city and the Cadmea, the inner fortress.

"He remembers," Thelem grunted with satisfaction, beginning to unharness the snorting horses. "Remembers me, remembers my brother, remembers every soldier by sight... Even from when he and I first marched against the Teleboans together --- he remembers it all. In a word --- a hero! If he told me: come, Thelem, the two of us against the Lapiths[^7] --- I'd go! I swear by Zeus Dodonaios, I'd go! Says my brother will be home by noon... ah, and won't we drink our wine unwatered tonight!"

[^7]: One of the tribes descended from the Titans; the tree people.

"But who was that, uncle?" the youth started to ask, but Nasal did not hear him, lost in joyful dreams of the drinking bout to come.

"That was Amphitryon," Philid answered in Thelem's place, having come down from the wall. "Amphitryon Perseid[^8], friend of basileus Creon and the pride of all Hellas. Got it, greenhorn?"

[^8]: Perseid is a descendant of Perseus; in this case, his grandson (compare Zeus Kronid, "son of Kronus," or Autolycus Hermesid, "son of Hermes," and so on). Sometimes the ending "-ad" was used instead (Aload, Heliad, etc.).

"Got it," the youth nodded eagerly. "Amphitryon Perseid, the pride of Hellas. The one who married his own niece, and then killed his father-in-law with a club. What's not to get --- the pride, and so on..."

"Well, what can you expect from a fool?!" Philid scratched the back of his head and spat in frustration. "I won't stick up for you with Thelem anymore! Killed his father-in-law... It was an accident! And besides --- I'd have killed my own father-in-law long ago! The old stump has worn me past bearing!.. And yet I don't kill him! Because I'm no hero. Now if I were a hero..."

The youth nodded once more, not listening to Philid, gazing at the street down which the hero Amphitryon Perseid had walked only moments before.

The street was empty, but the youth still seemed to see the wings of the expensive cloak and the confident stride of the night visitor, who looked like a god.

Merry, fearless, and merciless.
